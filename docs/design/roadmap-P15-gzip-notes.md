## What the next phase inherits

### The instruments

- **Five figures read gzip**, each re-taken by `measure.py --figure <id>` from
  any commit carrying its instrument: `inflate-throughput` (serial inflate per
  engine against the plain parse), `gzseek-backend` and `gzseek-spacing`
  (`crates/gz-seek/examples/gz_read.rs`), `gzip-first-pass` (four writers'
  files, the `gzip-1mib-chunks` leg, its verdicts `measure.gzpass_outcome`,
  `gzpass_verdict` and `gzpass_chunk_verdict`) and `gzip-resident`. Their
  inputs are generated by `scripts/generate_gzip_input.py`, never committed,
  and kept beside `control` in the SSD cache.
- **`crates/gz-seek/examples/spec_cost.rs` emulates the scheduling** with no
  scan beside it: `par` drives the pass as `ahead.rs` does and prints the
  account, its room and lead set by `DECODES`, `GUESSES` and `LEAD`. It
  rebuilds in seconds where `pgdt` takes a quarter of an hour, so a lever is
  tried there first.
- **The hand probes are `runs/p15.17.1/probe.sh`** (contained, `perf stat`
  over `control_gz` at chosen counts) and **`runs/p15.19/pairs.py`**, which
  reads alternated reps of two builds by `measure.gzpass_beats`. A probe is
  the account, never the gate, when its builds are frame-pointer `profiling`
  ones.
- **`--heaptrack-recipe` records the first pass's pair**
  (`HEAPTRACK_FIRST_PASS_AXIS`). `reserve`'s step pair has not been recorded
  under the recipe as repaired at 15.18.
- **The zlib-ng `cc` build's defect is drafted as an upstream report**,
  `runs/p15.9-zlib-ng-cc-probe/issue.md`, for the maintainer to file; no
  plan waits on it, so it is no `UF<k>`.

### What stays unattributed

Each is named with what would attribute it; none blocks anything.

- **Why the upper counts gained at the shipped chunk**: the twenty-three- and
  twenty-four-job rows are faster at `055c8d27` than at `ab7f630c`. A
  profile of the twenty-four-job pass at each chunk would say.
- **The BGZF leg's per-reader secant is past its charge** in `gzip-resident`,
  every cell's remainder still under `MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND`. A heaptrack
  pair of that leg at 24 and 4 jobs would name it.
- **A resume's fixed cost**: no `gzseek-spacing` row resolves it, its
  `starts` row less its `forward` row lying inside both spreads. Pricing it
  takes far more resumes in one run, or an instrument timing the begin alone.
- **`gzseek-spacing`'s `units` rows at 64 and 128 MiB sit highest**. A
  candidate is the instrument's one-unit output buffer outgrowing the
  last-level cache.
- **No figure reads a fetched first pass**, its time or its resident, and the
  download a fetched pass adds to each task's charge is unmeasured.
- **The longer chunk's second cost**: every task is charged its chunk twice,
  and `gzip-first-pass` affords every count its memory, so a budget seating
  fewer workers than the default count is unread.

### Behaviour a later phase meets

- **A `parse` over a fetched gzip file reads more than its pass**: the scan's
  restart at the file's start reads behind the frontier, and the DDL read
  back after the scan fetches whole units (`KD136`).
- **A one-job `parse` of a single member ends on the block path**: the DDL
  spans read after the scan land in the last unit once the pass has handed
  over, and a first-pass worker's budget affords one reader's block-path
  charge.
- **`scan started` on a fresh first pass reads `bytes=at least 0`**, the open
  extent before anything is decoded; the manual explains it.
- **A fetched frontier's wait for a decode is reached by no test**: it goes
  back to the runtime a piece at a time, and the decode's own pieces land
  first.
- **A take is asserted by sequencing, never by racing**: a fetched task
  reaches the runtime between its fetch and each decode, so a raced take
  failed 14 of 80 runs under contention. A take needs blocks shorter than a
  chunk, and is read before the end, the `Frontier` going at the hand-over.

## Negative results

### Writers and fixtures

- **A regenerated `compress.sql.gz` cannot be compared with the committed
  `default.sql`**: `\restrict`'s key and the `now()` column move every run
  (D69), and regenerating the schema moves 84 files. The fixture test checks
  framing and major instead. Every routine major writes the same header,
  `1f 8b 08 00 00000000 00 03`.
- **`flate2` over zlib-rs at level 1 ends no block on a sync flush**, where
  the default level ends one a flush, so a test wanting units inside a member
  flushes at the default level. Its default level wrote two blocks over the
  1.4 MB statistics fixture.
- **A `deflateParams` level change fails through `flate2` over zlib-rs**
  (`set_level` flushes with no output buffer), so a mixed-level member is
  sync-flushed raw streams concatenated (`test_support::gzip_levels`).
- **The tests' payload ends a block every three to six 8 KiB chunks** at
  zlib-rs's default level; the take tests read a member flushed every 8 KiB
  (`flushed_indexed`).
- **The PyPI zlib-ng is not Fedora's at level 6**, so it is not the `pg_dump
  -Z` a label would name; the generator reaches another `libz.so.1` through
  `ctypes`, `uv`'s Python having `zlib` built in and `fedora:44` no Python.

### Engines and builds

- **zlib-ng has no CMake-free binding**: `libz-sys`'s `cc` build fails its
  function table at 1.1.30 and leaves AVX-512 symbols undefined at 1.1.23,
  and Debian trixie packages none. A `libz-sys` build given both modes builds
  stock zlib, so a leg that lost its feature reads as the `zlib` one, which
  is why the harness asks each build its engine.
- **`libz-sys` 1.1.30 declares neither `inflateGetDictionary` nor `crc32_z`**,
  and its `z_stream` takes no null allocator, so `zlib.rs` declares both and
  hands its legs `calloc` and `free`.
- **`Z_TREES` cannot report a block's type through zlib-rs 0.6.8**: after a
  header stop on a stored block it reads the data as lengths. The engine reads
  the three header bits from the bytes instead, which holds on every engine.
- **A stop just after the final block makes no progress** (`Z_BLOCK`,
  `data_type` 192), so the engine inflates on to the stream's end in the same
  step.
- **`rusty-rapidgzip` 0.2.1 was read and not built**: a streaming whole-file
  decoder with its own threads, no index and no window-primed decode. Its
  block finder is the nearest code to the speculation's search.

### The parallel pass

- **A stored header has aliases**: any earlier bit whose bits up to the
  boundary are zero reads the same length, so a chunk is taken at any alias
  rather than the search guessing.
- **A false dynamic header can decode garbage to an end-of-block code**, so a
  refusal past a boundary is searched past too, bounded for damaged bytes.
  Fixed and last blocks are not searched for, three bits matching anywhere.
- **On `control_gz` a marked decode almost never comes clean**, so the plain
  path is not what a speculation spends. An exact clean mark a copy was
  slower than the source-region mark in each of three forms.
- **Pushing speculations further ahead does not make two or four jobs
  faster**: with fewer workers than a speculation costs inflates, guesses land
  too far apart to chain. Runs with gaps the frontier decodes unguessed are no
  faster than the free-running pass; a lead of two to six chunks past the lag
  is one plateau, and three or four times the lag overshoots.
- **A chunk shorter than a member may hold no member start**, so it is never
  taken itself; a one-task fetched pass at 8 KiB chunks over 21 KB members
  took nothing.
- **A file whose plaintext is a small fraction of its compressed bytes gives
  the caller no chance to run decodes**: `Parallel::read` returns only at a
  checkpoint or a full buffer. Only a crafted file has that ratio.
- **The decode-buffer pool did not lower what a worker holds**: the spare
  buffers stay resident where the allocator kept a freed one. What it buys is
  a charge that bounds the buffers by construction.
- **The secant cannot settle a term of a task's charge**: on the limit leg
  every term whose worst case outruns its charge holds nothing.

### The first pass in `pgdt-io`

- **A BGZF run is not ended by a stated length or an `ISIZE` the decode
  contradicts**: the pass learns either only at the member's end, which a
  resume could not recover.
- **No `Format::Bgzf`**: BGZF changes how an index is found, not the format a
  cached index is checked against. The probe's leading bytes are not handed
  to the walk, its first read covering them.
- **A spill file made eagerly at open was not taken**: `info` and the
  DataFusion provider open a pass before refusing to scan, and an unwritable
  cache directory would have turned "no cache" into a spill refusal.
- **A read behind the frontier is not checked by the resumed forward
  decode's CRC-32s**, which compare nothing for a decode left mid-unit, nor
  by decoding a closed unit whole, which holds plaintext the charge does not
  name.
- **The pass's constructors take a `String`, not the `Origin`**: naming
  `source::origin` from `first_pass` closes a module cycle through the
  openers.

### Measuring it

- **A shared L3 matters most on the 3900X** (core k is CPUs k and k+12, three
  cores to an L3): two jobs on two CCXs are far slower than on one; an SMT
  sibling costs about as much at two jobs; the clock a few percent; memory
  bandwidth nothing.
- **`perf stat` counts no switch or migration here**: at
  `perf_event_paranoid` 2 every event is `:u`. `/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/sched`
  holds them (`runs/p15.19.1/sched.py`). `ls_mabresp_rmt_cache` reads zero on
  a single socket.
- **A probe pinned to as many CPUs as `--jobs` oversubscribes them**, the
  scan's thread one more, so pinned legs compare only with each other.
- **Two builds of one source differ by layout**, and a leg build against the
  shipped one keeps that difference: part of 15.20's chunk margin narrowed
  when 15.21 swapped which length was the leg.
- **The image's heaptrack writes `.gz`**, and a report piped from one
  container into another loses its tail; each report runs its pipe inside one
  `sh -c`.
