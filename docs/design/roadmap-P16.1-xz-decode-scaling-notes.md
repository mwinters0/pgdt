# P16.1 — `xz-decode-scaling`: the decode probe becomes an instrument

What the next slice inherits. What this phase committed to is
[`roadmap-P16-parallel-scan.md`](roadmap-P16-parallel-scan.md); what has landed
is [`../status/STATUS.md`](../status/STATUS.md).

## What exists now

- **`pgdump_query/examples/xz_decode.rs`** — an example target, so it is in
  neither the library nor the shipped CLI. It opens an `.xz` file, plans the
  whole plaintext range under `xz_seek::Bulk::new(workers, budget)`, drains it
  through `RangeRead::read`, and prints `key=value` lines about what it did.
- **`scripts/generate_xz_input.py`** — the two `.xz` inputs: `--from-dump`
  compresses a plain dump at koji's own container parameters, `--from-koji`
  copies a stream-aligned slice of the koji download and **refuses one whose
  compression ratio is outside 14–18×**, which is the density every rate this
  figure publishes is a rate of.
- **`scripts/measure.py`** — the `xz-decode-scaling` register entry, in
  `FIGURES`; compressed inputs; a derived input; the `warm-parallel` regime;
  per-figure container memory; and the instrument-report channel.

**The published table is [`measurements.md`](measurements.md), "What a second
decode worker buys"**, taken at `7d21c6e` and naming that commit in its own
marker; why that took a second commit is the last section here.

## The findings, and what they mean for the rest of the phase

The numbers below are the published medians
([`measurements.md`](measurements.md), "What a second decode worker buys").

**The probe reproduces exactly, and the near-linear reading does not survive
past four workers.** One worker on real koji bytes decodes 3.00 GiB in 7.40 s —
~435 MB/s, against the thesis table's ~446 — and four reach ~1.50 GB/s, which
is the second probe to three significant figures. So nothing in the spec's
arithmetic *up to four* is wrong. Past that the koji leg flattens hard: 5.84× one
worker at eight, 7.16× at twelve, and **7.78× at twenty-four**, where the
twenty-fourth worker buys essentially nothing over the sixteenth.

That bears on the thesis table directly, because that table extrapolates a fused
worker's rate linearly to "~12 fused workers reach the HDD's 4,656 MB/s offer".
Twelve decode workers on koji's bytes reach 3,116 MB/s, not twelve times
435 MB/s — so the worker counts the table names are **floors on what will be
needed** rather than estimates of what will suffice. **The conclusions the table
draws are unchanged** — decode is still far below every device's offer, still
the stage worth parallelizing, and the fused shape is still self-balancing for
the reason the spec gives — but the slices that price parallelism
(`parallel-scan-throughput`, and the leader's own worker count) should read this
figure rather than the table's arithmetic.

**The two legs are far apart at one worker and bend differently**, which is the
reason the figure has two legs rather than one. A decode rate is per plaintext
byte, so it is a property of the *content*: the generated control compresses
5.45× and decodes at ~205 MB/s on one core, where koji's 15.70× bytes decode
at ~435. The control also keeps climbing where koji has stopped — 10.80× one
worker at twenty-four against koji's 7.78×. A figure taken on generated bytes
alone would have published a curve that says something quite different about the
corpus the phase is aimed at.

## The calls worth knowing about

**The koji leg is a stream-aligned slice, not the file.** Three things forced
it and each would have cost a different thing:

- The download is 31,150 concatenated streams and building its seek table means
  walking every footer — **85 s** before a byte is decoded
  ([`architecture.md`](architecture.md), "The compressed source"). Every
  reading would have been a reading of the walk.
- It is 40 GB, so it cannot be staged on tmpfs, and reading it off the HDD makes
  the device the bound at high worker counts — which is what
  [`measurements.md`](measurements.md), "Scan throughput by input shape" refuses
  when it says the HDD is not a fourth regime.
- Any contiguous run of whole streams is itself a valid `.xz` file, so a slice
  costs nothing to make and decodes to exactly what those streams held.

**It is cut from the middle, and the offset is a sample rather than a
representative.** koji's first 3 GiB of plaintext compresses **56.19×** against
the whole file's 19.41×, so a slice taken at the head would publish a decode
rate for bytes unlike the rest of the dump; `PGDQ_KOJI_XZ_OFFSET` seeks past it
— one seek, not a read of everything skipped — and the 128 streams taken there
compress **15.70×**.

**What no offset can be is representative, and the profile is why.** Sampling
eight streams at twelve depths costs a seek and ~10 MB each, and the download is
nowhere near homogeneous enough for one number to stand for it:

| offset | ratio | | offset | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 G | 33.05× | | 20 G | 15.89× ← the chosen region |
| 2 G | 25.56× | | 24 G | 15.23× |
| 4 G | 27.15× | | 28 G | 11.50× |
| 8 G | 28.15× | | 32 G | 16.83× |
| 12 G | 31.74× | | 36 G | 22.88× |
| 16 G | **5.02×** | | 39 G | 24.10× |

koji's regions differ from one another by 6.6×, so 15.70× against the file's
19.41× is not "the file's own neighbourhood" — it is one draw from a wide
distribution, a little denser than the middle. Re-taking the figure at another
offset would move the absolute rates and change nothing else, which is what
makes the offset cheap to reconsider and pointless to agonise over.

**So the curve is the finding, and an absolute rate travels with its density.**
Two claims come out of this figure and they are not equally transferable. The
scaling curve is a within-leg ratio against that leg's own one-worker row, and
the profile above barely touches it. The absolute rate is a property of the
*bytes*, and this figure's own two legs are the proof: the control at 5.45×
decodes ~205 MB/s on one core where koji's 15.70× decodes ~435. Fitting those
two points gives rate ∝ ratio^0.71, which would put the file's 19.41×-average
bytes nearer ~500 MB/s. So nothing may say "koji decodes at 435 MB/s"; it says
*3.00 GiB at 15.70× decodes at 435 MB/s* — and
[`architecture.md`](architecture.md) now carries that qualification on every
rate it states.

**The control leg is empirically koji's dense end, which is why two legs are
enough.** Its 5.45× sits on top of koji's densest sampled region — 5.02× at
16 G — so the two legs already *bracket* the corpus rather than merely
contrasting synthetic with real, and the floor the phase should size workers
against is the control's ~205 MB/s, not koji's ~435. A third koji column at a
second offset was considered and refused for that reason: it would re-measure
the spread leg one already spans, and a table of one file at three depths says
something about koji rather than about the decoder.

**The slice's density is asserted, not remembered.** `PGDQ_KOJI_XZ_OFFSET` is a
bare constant, and a moved offset — or a koji dump refreshed next year — could
land in the 12 G-like 31.74× band and republish a rate for quite different bytes
under the same table heading. That is the same failure mode the instrument
already refuses twice, where what comes out is a plausible table rather than an
error. So the generator divides the slice's plaintext by its compressed size the
moment it has cut it and **refuses anything outside 14–18×**
(`generate_xz_input.py`, `check_koji_density`); the figure's own table quotes the
ratio and the band beside it, from the instrument's delivered byte count, so a
published rate can never appear without the density it is a rate of.

**The band brackets a draw, and the region it was drawn from is homogeneous.**
Sub-slices of the chosen region — its first 1, 8 and 127 streams — compress
15.82×, 15.89× and 15.70×, so the width is not absorbing within-region noise; it
is absorbing a slice cut slightly elsewhere, against a corpus whose regions
differ by 6.6×. The head of the download re-measures at exactly the 56.19× the
profile above recorded, and the gate refuses it.

**The gate is at the cut, not at the emit, and both places had a claim.** What
argues for the emit is that publication is where the claim is made; what settles
it is that the generator is the only place the wrong bytes can still be cheap.
A gate that fired inside `run_xz_decode_scaling` would throw away a sitting's
readings to report something the generator could have refused before the first
one, and no path through the harness reaches a sitting with a slice the gated
generator did not cut: `generate_xz_input.py`'s bytes and the `--from-offset`
argument are both hashed into the input's stamp, so a moved offset, a changed
band or a changed generator all regenerate the slice.

**Landing the gate regenerated both `.xz` legs.** The stamp covers the
generator's source, and `control_xz` names it too — so the publishing sitting
recompressed the 3.00 GiB control at preset 6 and re-cut the koji slice off the
HDD before its first reading. Minutes, not an hour, and it happens inside the
`--figure` run.

**The generated leg is a *compression of* `control`, not a second generation.**
`InputSpec.derives_from` is what says so, and the source's stamp folds into the
derived one's — otherwise a change to `generate_perf_data.py` would regenerate
every plain input and leave the compressed leg measuring pre-change rows.

**A regime is a device plus a gate, and this one shares a device.**
`warm-parallel` stages exactly where `warm` does and differs only in its
contention row, which gates `cpu_steal_pct` and nothing else. That is not
laxity: a reading that deliberately occupies 24 threads makes "the machine is
busy" the measurement, so `cpu_busy_pct` and `psi_cpu_some_pct` would fire on
every rep. What witnesses such a reading instead is inside the table — every
cell is a ratio against the one-worker row of its own leg and of its own
sitting, which is the co-measured-floor argument the throughput tables already
make, with a row standing in for the `dd`.

**The instrument refuses two things rather than reporting them**, because both
fail by producing a plausible table: a plan that admits fewer workers than were
asked for (which would put one reading in two rows and read as a flat curve),
and a short decode (which would read as a fast one).

**The rate's denominator comes from the instrument.** A compressed input's
plaintext volume is in its seek table and nowhere the harness can `stat`, so the
binary prints what it delivered and the table divides by that. `parse_reported`
and `Session.reported` are the channel; it is not a second reading, and nothing
else uses it yet.

**The container is 2 GB, against the register's 512 MB.** Twenty-four decoded
24 MiB blocks, their compressed windows and their LZMA2 dictionaries are around
950 MB on the generated leg. `Figure.memory` carries it, `emit` sets it per
figure and resets it, and the figure's own table says so — an apparatus
departure that a table inherits silently is the failure the register's one-line
apparatus statement exists against.

**16.4 and 16.5 inherit a number from this, not a mechanism.** Nothing here
touches `io.rs`, `XzSource` or the buffer pool: the instrument reaches past the
library to the decoder's own bulk entry point precisely so that the figure is a
property of the decoder rather than of what the library currently does with it.
So `XzSource`'s internal concurrency (16.5) is measured *against* this curve
rather than being expected to change it — if it comes back materially below,
the difference is the library's, which is the comparison the figure was built to
make possible.

## How the table reached `measurements.md`, and why it took two commits

A figure published outside a stamped sweep declares the commit it was taken at
inside its own marker, and `--stale`, acknowledgement spentness and
`--verify-additive` all argue from that commit
([`measurements.md`](measurements.md), "A figure may be published outside the
sweep"). That is what a sitting taken from a working tree cannot satisfy: with
the instrument, the generator and the register entry uncommitted there is no
commit that contains them to name, and the parent names a tree where the
instrument does not exist — a marker pointing there would report the figure
stale against the very commit that lands it, with no acknowledgement able to
excuse it.

So the apparatus is committed first and the sitting is taken from that commit,
which is `7d21c6e` — the density gate, whose landing also regenerated both `.xz`
legs, `generate_xz_input.py` being one of the figure's `depends`. The published
readings are that sitting's; the earlier ones survive only as the orientation
figures quoted above.

**This is a general property of an instrument slice, not an accident of this
one.** Any slice that both builds a measuring apparatus and publishes a reading
from it lands in two commits, because the reading has to name a commit the
apparatus is already in. The harness enforces the ordering rather than leaving
it to be remembered: `--figure` prints the commit it will write into the marker
before the first reading, and refuses a sitting whose commit does not descend
from the stamp.

```sh
cd scripts && uv run measure.py --figure xz-decode-scaling
```

Folding it in moved the entry from `measure.UNTAKEN` into `FIGURES` and replaced
the session stamp's accounting sentence with the one `--check` prints, the
register having grown from 18 figures to 19.
