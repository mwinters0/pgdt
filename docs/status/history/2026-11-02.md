# 2026-11-02

## P33: what only reads a value holds none of it, and a query's decode has a factor per column

The `/dwal` over 33.9's two STATUS entries — a data-level parse's statistics copying an escaped field whole, a filter
term's key a copy beside the batch's — settled the spec's "What a held line costs" with the maintainer. The facts it
rests on:

- `ColumnGatherer::observe` (`statistics/gather/column.rs`) unescapes every field whole, though all it keeps of a long
  one is head-bounded: bytewise bounds clip to `CLIP_BYTES`, `Canonical::Bytea` borrows hex and renders an escape
  form's head, a keyed order and a dictionary entry give up past `DICTIONARY_ENTRY_MAX_BYTES`, `Clipped::locate_bytes`
  places a value by its head and length. What it reads of the rest is a length, UTF-8 validity, a character count for
  a typmod and the `bytea` hex check. 33.8 saw no copy in a default parse because mimalloc's retention absorbed it.
- A filter term unescapes its field, then `field_key` copies it again — `Text`'s `to_string`, `PaddedText`'s trim,
  `Bytea`'s decoded `Vec` — before the batcher unescapes the same field anew (`predicate/eval.rs`).
- **The spec's "every decode shortens a value" was false.** A key over an array or `jsonb` value is a tree
  (`NestedKey::Array`'s `Vec<Option<NestedKey>>`, `Jsonb::Array`), tens of bytes an element where the text spends two,
  built by a filter's ordering term and by a strict check (`field_refused`'s `checked_key`, `field_key`'s
  `jsonb_key`). The batch's own decode of an `int8[]` or `float8[]` writes eight bytes an element into its
  `List<Int64>`/`List<Float64>` child, and `decode_array` first stages the elements as a `Vec<Option<Cow<str>>>`
  (`batch/builder.rs`, `append_array_level`).
- Two keys cannot be compared in text order: a `jsonb` object's pairs are sorted and de-duplicated
  (`uniqueifyJsonbObject`, `value/json.rs`), a multirange's members sorted and merged (`multirange_canonicalize`,
  `value/nested/key.rs`). Arrays, records and ranges compare in text order, and a strict check needs validity alone.

Settled, in the spec's "What a held line costs" and "Where it lives": a filter's term, a strict check and a parse's
statistics walk a field's unescaped bytes and hold nothing that grows with it, `check_row`'s whole unescape of a text
for its typmod included, but for an ordering key over a `jsonb` object or a multirange, which holds positions; a nested
value decodes straight into its builders; a parse draws once, strict or not; a query draws once plus the largest decode
factor among its columns and key factor among its terms, folded by pgdt-query from factors beside the builders and the
normalized keys. The spec rejects the alternatives.

The work is **33.13–33.16**, slices admitted after spec time, after 33.3.3 and before 33.4, whose ledger takes the
factors: a parse's reads first, landing the element walk 33.14 and 33.16 consume; a filter's scalar and in-order terms,
a rework of `predicate/eval.rs`, apart from 33.15's normalized keys, a new mechanism; the nested decode and the factor
fold last, with `long-line-resident`'s `int8[]` query and `jsonb` filter legs (the spec's "How it is shown"). 33.3.2 and
33.3.3 stream lines no pass decodes and keep their places.

## P33: a strict check reads a scalar off its bytes, earning 33.13.3

The `/dwal` over 33.13.1's STATUS entry, settled with the maintainer. The facts
([`../../design/roadmap-P33.13.1-walk-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P33.13.1-walk-notes.md),
"What 33.13.3 inherits"): a strict check collects every scalar part's text, which a
number's leading zeros or blanks, or a `polygon`, `path` or `bit varying` element
`pg_dump` writes, makes as long as its line; and a nested `box[]` is collected whole.
The spec's "What a held line costs" already says a strict check holds nothing that
grows with a value, so 33.13.1 shipped part of its row: its row is rewritten to what
landed, and **33.13.3**, before 33.13.2, holds the rest.

Settled: **every scalar reader reads a byte source forward**, holding only what its
answer needs — an integer's accumulator, `numeric`'s digits past its leading zeros
(I63), a float's significant digits to the bound past which only a nonzero one
matters, a date's fields in its work buffer — a `&str` being one source, so one
grammar per type and no second parser beside each decoder. Rejected: charging a
strict parse its line plus the longest part it collects, twice its line for nearly
every table, for a copy `pg_dump` makes only for a geometric, `bit varying` or long
`numeric` value; borrowing a part where it lies contiguous in the line and copying
only an escaped one, which no `pg_dump` line pays but leaves a hand-written escaped
part uncharged, the input contract being what PostgreSQL reads. A nested `box[]` is
walked over `;` (I22), each element `box_in`'s.

## P33: a parse streamed is re-grilled, and a refusal names a head

Settled in the same `/dwal`. **The spec's rejection of a gathering parse streamed field
by field reopens** on "a codec whose decoders stream", which 33.13.3 completes on the
input side — the grammars walking (D143), every scalar reader on a source, statistics
keeping heads (D76, D142) — so a parse, strict or not, could fold each field as the
census does (D138), hold no line and draw nothing. **That is grilled on P33's spec as
its own session before 33.13.2**, whose "a parse drawing once", and 33.4's charge for a
parse, rest on the rejection; 33.13.3 lands either way.

**A refusal holds a head of its field** (`M277`): `check_row` puts the whole unescaped
field in `FieldRefusal.value`, printed whole by `Error::FieldRefused` and persisted under
`ignore` as `ColumnRefusals::first`, where the restore's `CopyFromErrorCallback` prints
it through `limit_printout_length`, 100 bytes (`copyfrom.c`, `MAX_COPY_DATA_DISPLAY`).


## P33: a gathering parse streams, its reader's form chosen by evidence

The re-grill of the spec's rejection, with the maintainer, settled in the spec's "A pass holds a line only where
it decodes it", "A census holds only the fields it decodes" and "How it is shown", and in STATUS's slices. The facts:

- Datanommer's long lines are one field: `msg`, a `text` column, makes every line over 1 MiB, its longest decoded
  value 450 MB of a 462 MB line, the next column's 146 KB (`runs/datanommer-survey/report.md`, §§3, 5). Holding a
  field in place of the line buys that dump nothing.
- Every reader pulls from one contiguous field: `Unescaped` (`pgdt-core`'s `copy.rs`) looks ahead in its slice,
  `Source` (`value/source.rs`) offers `next`, `peek` and `fork`, and the walks recurse (`grammar/check.rs`,
  `nested/input.rs`) or keep a stack over a pull (`walk_json`). A fork's lookahead is unbounded in places: an
  unquoted element's whitespace run (`nested/input.rs`, `UnquotedElement`), a `bytea` leaf read twice
  (`check.rs`, `bytea::read_unescaped`). `TextFold::push` (`value/text.rs`) alone carries its state across runs,
  and no unescaper feeds it so.
- The carry's `FieldFold` (`scan/chunked.rs`) hands a field's raw escaped runs, split on tabs only, so an escape
  can straddle two runs; a row streams only once a second chunk fails to end it, and a data-level parse never
  streams, `Builder::reads_rows()` holding its lines.
- Of a long field, the gatherer needs a head, a running length, `TextFold`'s state and where trailing blanks
  begin; only a sum, which has no head bound, and `bytea`'s hex check with `bytea_unread` read it whole. No row
  reads a later field.
- PostgreSQL holds a line whole to restore it (`copyfromparse.c`, `line_buf`, then `attribute_buf`).

Settled: a gathering parse streams, every reader fed its runs and keeping a bounded state, so a parse draws nothing
and is never refused for memory; each reader resumes with one grammar per type, and were none to, the question
reopens rather than admit a second parser. A query's held line is its own tree, grilled apart, named as the
spec's reopen on D23 rather than an inbox entry, P33 being specified. **33.13.3 waits on this grilling** and lands
in the form it settles, its pull-form scalar readers otherwise rewritten by the next slice.

Settled in the second round: **the reader's form is chosen by evidence**, `33.17`, admitted ahead of 33.13.3 —
an `async` walk over a byte source, today's pull shape compiled into a state machine, if it prices within
`nested-decode-micro`'s noise of today's pull, else hand-written push state machines, PostgreSQL's own route for
its incremental JSON parser (`src/common/jsonapi.c`, `pg_parse_json_incremental`, which it calls "somewhat slower
than the Recursive Descent pattern"); runtime cost, not implementation cost, deciding. A reader pulling its next
chunk itself is rejected on D74. No lookahead past a fixed bound, the sites today being `nested/input.rs`'s
`UnquotedElement` whitespace fork and `check.rs`'s `bytea` leaf read twice. `json` depth's bits, PostgreSQL's
`json_in` recursing under `check_stack_depth` (I72, "Scope limit"), are uncharged.

Settled in the third round: the census streams its counted containers through the same readers, so no mapping pass
holds a field — D138's `Kept::Whole` goes, its rejection, a second parser, answered by one grammar resuming;
`BlockObserver` becomes a fold over a row's field runs, as `CensusFold` is, a held row that fold fed once, so
`Builder::reads_rows()` no longer holds a data-level parse's lines; and the acceptance is two hand runs, a strict
parse below the reference dump's longest line and a query reading its `msg` below its clusters (the spec's "How
it is shown").

Settled in the fourth round, closing the re-grill: the slices, in STATUS's order, are 33.17, then **33.18** the
input walks and unescaper resuming, 33.13.3 on its source, 33.13.2 rewritten to the output walk and the gatherer's
sum and tier over it, **33.19** a parse streaming its lines, **33.20** the census holding no field, then 33.14–33.16
and 33.4, which charges a parse nothing. `M277` blocks P33, landing before 33.19, whose strict parse cannot hold a
refused field whole; a refused block is discarded whole and an ignored refusal leaves its row observed, so no
streamed row's statistics are undone (`gatherer.rs`, `observe_row`).
