## P33: a held line grows outside the allocator

The `/dwal` over 33.8's two STATUS entries settled the spec's "What a held line costs": a line that outgrows its chunk
grows outside the global allocator, so the ledger draws bytes held on every allocator. The facts it rests on: both
terms are mimalloc's — its `realloc` allocates, copies and frees, and a freed buffer stays resident until its purge —
while glibc grows a block past its mmap threshold by `mremap` and frees it by `munmap`, holding neither (33.8 notes,
"Negative results"); and `Bytes::from(Vec)` (bytes 1.12) keeps the whole capacity, so a frozen line pins up to twice
itself. Lowering mimalloc's purge delay in `pgdt` stays `KD148`'s question, untouched here.
The same review placed the move at the chunk term, where the line's draw begins, and the growth on `mremap`, Linux's
(D104), over a reservation of I71's bound per held line; macOS's shape is filed in P34's inbox. The work is **33.10**,
a slice admitted after spec time, after 33.3.1 (which takes a pass decoding nothing out of the carry) and before 33.9
and 33.4.
The maintainer added that each block's longest line belongs in the map (spec, "A held line is charged, and admitted in
file order"); `CopyBlock` records no line length today, so it is a `CACHE_FORMAT_VERSION` bump.
It is **33.11**, after 33.10 and before 33.4: the record, its bump and `pgdt info`, the carve sized from it in 33.4 and the
refusal before a byte in 33.5. A held line allocated at the block's longest up front, skipping the remaps, waits on
33.10's probe leg; it saves only the remaps, faults being the same either way, and costs commit rather than resident, so
a material gap makes it the arrangement's choice from the environment's commit policy, never a setting. Whether a

## `M276`: the datetime readers end at the server's work buffer

The readers in `value/datetime.rs` took a part's leading zeros at any length, so a field `date_in`, `time_in`,
`timetz_in`, the timestamp inputs or `interval_in` refuses for overflowing `ParseDateTime`'s work buffer (I83, I84)
