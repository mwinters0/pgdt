## The recurrence has two halves, and the harness only fixes one

`M17` was specified against apparatus drift: every re-take so far had been a
one-off `runs/` script, so each one re-derived the apparatus from scratch. That
is the visible half.

The other half is that **code changes silently invalidate figures**. `M11`
changed one line in `map::Builder::on_row` and invalidated both census figures.
`M12` changed a generator's output bytes and armed a stale-input trap in the
whole-file bench that `M15` had to disarm. In neither case did anything
announce it — someone noticed, which is not a mechanism.

So each figure **declares the paths it depends on**, and the harness can say
which figures a diff has made stale: the census figures on `map.rs`, the nested
tables on `nested.rs`/`batch.rs`, the quadratic on the map plus the cache, every
figure on its generator. It is the same forcing function as the emit contract's
table shape — a figure that cannot say what invalidates it is one nobody has
thought about. `CLAUDE.md` gets the command and its read-trigger when the
harness lands, because the answer should be *computed* rather than remembered,
and remembering is exactly what failed twice.

## One sweep means the whole doc, not the stale parts of it

Re-taking seven correct tables looks like churn, and the argument that it is not
is that **the doc differences across tables**: the census-attribution table is
quoted as reproducing the census `parse` figures, and the warm
scan-throughput table exists so the `INSERT` ratio becomes a division against
its cold twin. A doc whose tables come from two sessions reintroduces the
fourth standing rule's failure — differencing across sessions — one level above
where that rule was written. Seven tables moving by the session drift is evidence
the drift is real, and the ninth rule already forbids reading a small movement
as a result.

The stamp is what makes "one session" checkable instead of asserted, and it
answers "are these figures from before or after my change" at a glance. Every
previous answer to that question was archaeology.

## A heading may quote a number, because it is not an address

Four headings in `measurements.md` quote a figure — "costs 7% of a warm scan",

## The first full sweep died on 6,799 bytes, and the budget is now computed

The sweep of 2026-08-28 ran 36 minutes and lost three of eleven figures. The
tmpfs staging budget was a constant — 9 GiB — chosen as the nominal sum of the
largest figure's three inputs. Every generator overshoots its target by a few
kilobytes, so `control + large_object + insert_run` is 9,663,683,215 bytes
against a budget of 9,663,676,416: **over by 6,799 bytes**, one part in 1.4
million. Eviction then had nothing legal to take, and `scan-throughput-warm`
and `nested-end-to-end` failed twenty minutes in; `cross-file-floor` fell over
behind them with a bare `KeyError`, because it borrows their readings.

A unit test claimed to prevent exactly this. It summed **nominal** sizes, so it
agreed with the constant instead of with the disk.

Three things changed, and the first is the one that matters:

- **The budget is computed, not configured.** The harness already knows which
  inputs each figure needs and what they weigh on disk, so the ceiling is the
  largest single figure's own inputs plus 10% — only one figure's inputs are
  ever resident, and eviction handles the rest. `PGDQ_MEASURE_TMPFS_BUDGET_GIB`
  survives only as a cap. A constant would have to be re-guessed on every
  machine, and raising it to a round 10 GiB would have made the sweep need a
  `/dev/shm` this machine happens to have.
- **A sweep preflights.** Each figure's need against the budget, the budget
  against the staging filesystem's real free space, and the inputs still to
  generate against the input cache's disk — all before the first measurement,
  reported together, exit 2. Every one of these was knowable at second zero.
- **A figure whose shared reading failed is skipped by name**, not by
  `KeyError`: "skipped — borrows from `nested-end-to-end`, which failed".

## `--stale`'s job is binary, which retires the question of how narrow it is

Replaying `--stale` against `M7`'s real commit (`2eb51f4`) flags **8 of 11
figures**, all through `pgdump_query/src/map.rs` — including

It also shows that per-figure attribution is not what the tool is for. `map.rs`
is declared by eight figures, so most changes mark most of the doc stale — and
since the one-sweep rule means a stale figure forces the *whole* doc to be
re-taken, the actionable output is binary: re-sweep, or don't. One true
positive settles it, and per-figure detail is explanatory colour.

whether a sweep was taken against a dirty tree, so the two answers are
consistent by construction rather than by anyone keeping them in step.

## `M20`: closing a decision entry is filing it, and it has an owner

The keystone cut "Decisions worth another look" from about twenty entries to
one, and the question it raised is why the section silted up at all. Reading
the struck entries out of `ff9c8f3^` answers it: they had **not** stayed open.
All but one recorded in their own text that they had been reviewed and had
stood or been reversed. The section was not a backlog of unanswered questions;
it was a pile of answered ones.

Two causes, both structural.

**Closure had a trigger and deletion did not.** "An entry leaves when the
maintainer has looked at it" names no moment, because looking happens in
conversation. The session that hears the answer holds the delete key and
instead annotates the entry — which is cheaper to justify, since it looks like
preserving the record — and once annotated the entry reads as handled, so no
later session touches it either. Nothing in the repo marks that a review
occurred, which is why only a keystone could find them.

**An affirmed call has nowhere to be filed.** Every entry whose review changed
an artifact says so in its own text: *"all three docs now state it flatly"*,
*"generalized into `roadmap.md`'s standing rules"*, *"queued as out-of-band"*.
Those were sediment the moment the fold-in landed. The residue is the other
kind — a call reviewed, affirmed, and changing nothing — whose reasoning the
fact-routing table had no row for. That is the class that cannot honestly be
deleted on the spot, and it is indistinguishable in shape from the class that
can.

So the rules added to [`../../process.md`](../../process.md) are four: closing
means filing then deleting, never annotating; the session that hears the answer
does it, in that session; five entries hard, because the section's value is
entirely in being read every time; and the out-of-band ledger's admission rule
applied here — if the maintainer would only nod, it is a known gap, an inbox
entry, or a ledger row instead. The fact-routing table gains the missing row,
and `CLAUDE.md` carries the close trigger, since that moment fires in ordinary
conversation and no skill covers it.

**The one worked example was already half-filed.** `depends` staying coarse —
per-figure attribution is not the goal, because a stale figure forces a whole
re-sweep — had its reasoning in this file, under "`--stale`'s job is binary",
and its *decision* in no design doc at all. So the standing rule was reachable
only by someone who knew which day to read, and the keystone session that
relied on it was reading the STATUS entry. It is now a *Rejected:* paragraph in
[`../../design/measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md) beside the
`--check`/`--stale` description, pointing back here for the evidence. That
split — decision in the doc that holds it, evidence in the dated entry — is
what the fourth rule now produces by default.

**What this does not claim.** Whether the affirmed entries bought continuity
was the open question, and the answer is that they did not, on the one occasion
it could be tested: the composite column's share was affirmed on 2026-08-27 and
reopened by the 2026-08-28 sweep, and the reconsideration was driven by
`measurements.md`'s ~0.5 µs/row floor rule, producing a fresh entry rather than
reopening the old one. The standing entry was not the retrieval path even for
its own scenario.

naming the missing shared module as the reason it was duplicated. Both crates
now have `tests/common/mod.rs`. What *drives* the library — `collect`,
`drain`, `census_of` — deliberately stayed put: those differ per file in ways
that matter, and pooling them would rebuild the same problem one level up.

**`layering.md`'s rule-1 check was reading an incomplete list.** The doc says
rule 1 is read off the import lists and prescribes `rg '^use crate::'`; three
modules named part of their dependencies inline instead (`map.rs` imported six
and reached for eight more, `index.rs` 6/14, `stream.rs` 11/14), so the grep
under-reported them. Every crate-internal dependency in production code is now
in an import list, and the doc gains a counter-check that stops at the first
`#[cfg(test)]`. `batch.rs`'s upward `crate::stream` import is deliberately in
the list too: it is a deviation the doc already records, and it belongs where
the check can see it.

**Ten of twelve figures are stale by path and none is expected to have
moved.** `--stale` invalidates on a declared path being touched, which is the
coarseness the harness chose on purpose (`measurements.md`, beside the
`--check`/`--stale` description). What actually changed in those files is
imports, one extracted `Builder` method called once per span, and
`preamble.rs`'s `skip_quoted` — which runs per `CREATE TABLE`, not per row.
`Builder::on_row` was left alone by name, since two figures' recipes patch it.
So no measured quantity should move, but the standing rule is that a figure's
staleness is computed rather than argued, and the doc's tables must come from
one sweep: a re-take is `uv run measure.py --all` against the commit that
lands this, folded in whole. Until then `measurements.md`'s session stamp
still correctly says what it says — the figures *were* taken against `ff9c8f3`
— and `STATUS.md` carries the outstanding sweep.

**What was deliberately not touched.** `range_struct`/`list_of` are restated
in three test modules and in `pgtype.rs`; that is the tests independently
spelling out the expected Arrow shape, and sharing the production helper would
make them tautological. `map.rs::step` stays one 228-line state-machine match,
because splitting it scatters the modes. The two deviations under
`layering.md`'s "Known deviations" stay where they are, per that doc's own
instruction not to fix them opportunistically.

### The `M21` re-sweep ran, and is not foldable

`runs/measure-20260828T115846`, `exit=0` in 1500 s against `952e492`, all 11
figures, no failures. It settles what it was launched for and refuses what it
was going to be used for.

**`M21` moved nothing.** `--drift` against the `ff9c8f3` sweep
(`runs/measure-20260828T034948`, still on disk) over 46 shared readings: median
absolute drift **1.8%**, every *cold* reading within **±0.2%**, and the largest
movement in the whole sweep is `dd` → `/dev/null` on tmpfs at **+20.1%** — a
control containing none of this project's code. Where pgdq's warm readings
moved most (`COPY` parse, +12.5%) they moved *less* than the `dd` beside them.

**But its warm regime was 12–20% slower than the sweep it would replace**, so
folding it in would rewrite claims for apparatus reasons. The load-bearing one
is the `INSERT`-vs-`COPY` per-byte CPU ratio, quoted in
[`../../design/pg-dump-compatibility.md`](../../design/pg-dump-compatibility.md),
[`../../design/roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md) and the P7 inbox: 8.26 s /
0.510 s = **16.2×** becomes 8.29 s / 0.573 s = **14.5×**. The numerator held
(+0.4%) because an `INSERT` scan is CPU-bound; the denominator inflated with
the `dd` control. The ratio's physics did not change.

Two others moved for pure-noise reasons and are worth knowing before anyone
reads them as regressions. `cross-file-floor`'s composite-column share went
+0.99 → +0.31 µs/row, which is below the ~0.5 µs floor that same figure
establishes — it was never a measurable quantity. And `nested-decode-micro`'s
`÷ copy` ratio went 195.5× → 127.3× with the decode and render medians
*identical* (3.72 µs, 1.64–1.66 µs): the denominator is a 601-byte memcpy
control that moved 27.5 → 42.1 ns, which is the noisiest measurement in the
document.

**Which devices this concerns.** Cold figures read `/dev/sda1`, a Samsung 860
EVO SATA SSD; warm figures read `/dev/shm`, which is RAM and not a device at
all; `parse-cache-out` writes to that same tmpfs; the scratch `--dqcache` for
the other `parse` figures lands on the container's overlay on `/dev/nvme0n1p2`
and is a few KB. So the warm figures never touch storage, which is why a
20% move in a tmpfs `dd` is a memory-bandwidth or CPU story rather than a disk
one — and why the SSD-bound cold figures, at ~560 MB/s, were unmoved.

### `M22`: every reading carries a witness

The drift above was visible and **not diagnosable**, which is what this fixes.
Design and rejected alternatives are filed beside the mechanism in
[`../../design/measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md), "The
apparatus"; what belongs here is the evidence.

**The governor hypothesis was wrong, and measuring it was cheap.** The
proposal that an 8× scaling range under `powersave` was moving the
memory-bandwidth-bound readings is false on this box: three runs of a 3 GiB
tmpfs `dd` under each governor give a median of **0.270 s either way**, with
the busiest core at **4546.9 MHz under `powersave` and 4546.4 MHz under
`performance`**. `amd-pstate-epp` is hardware-managed; the governor name is
very nearly cosmetic and the EPP does the work. So `--pin-governor` ships off
rather than as apparatus — turning it on would oblige a full re-sweep in
exchange for nothing measurable. It is kept because a machine on
`acpi-cpufreq` with a real `ondemand` governor would show exactly the effect,
and there it is a one-word fix.

That 0.270 s also says the machine is currently in the *fast* state the
`ff9c8f3` sweep saw (0.269 s), not the slow one the `M21` sweep saw (0.323 s) —
so whatever moved it was transient and is not frequency.

**A metric that would have lied.** The first cut averaged frequency across all
24 cores, which for a near-single-threaded scan is dominated by the 23 idle
ones: it reported 1.86 GHz for a window whose working core was at 4.5 GHz. The
sampler now records the busiest core apart from the all-core mean, and the
apparatus line quotes the busiest. The two together are also what says whether
anything *else* was running.

**What the next sweep owes.** It is both the `M21` re-take and the run that
sets `measure.CONTENTION_LIMITS` from observed distributions, after which the
gate can be armed. Until then `CONTENTION_LIMITS` is empty and gates nothing,
which is a deliberate state and not a bug.

### The `M22` sweep, folded in — and the old figure was the outlier

`runs/measure-20260828T140055`, `exit=0` in 1433 s against `fa186ab`, 11
figures, no failures, folded into
[`../../design/measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md) whole.

**The machine was quiet, and that is now a measurement.** Across 182 readings:
`cpu_busy_pct` p95 4.3% cold and 4.5% warm, `steal` 0.00% throughout, busiest
core 3.59–4.09 GHz, ≤66°C. Every table carries its own apparatus line saying
so.

**Two telemetered sweeps agree where the un-telemetered one disagrees.** The
`INSERT`-vs-`COPY` per-byte CPU ratio reads 16.2× in `ff9c8f3` (no telemetry),
14.46× in `952e492` and 14.57× in `fa186ab`. It is now **14.6×** wherever it
appears. The `COPY` leg is what moves: half a second, memory-bandwidth-bound,
0.510 → 0.573 → 0.534 s, while the CPU-bound `INSERT` leg holds at 8.26 → 8.29
→ 7.78 s. The conclusion the earlier entry reached — that `952e492` was the
noisy one — was wrong, and the evidence that corrects it is the telemetry
`M22` added.

**What still is not explained.** The warm tmpfs `dd` floor sits at 0.309–0.320 s
across both recent sweeps against `ff9c8f3`'s 0.268 s, on a machine the
counters show idle. It is not contention, not steal, not frequency, and not the
device — a warm figure never touches storage. The candidates left are memory
layout and TLB behaviour over a 34-day uptime with tmpfs on 4 KiB pages
(`ShmemHugePages: 0`, since `shmem_enabled` is `[advise]` and neither `dd` nor
`pgdq` asks for huge pages). Cold readings, which *are* device-bound, are
identical to ±0.2% across all three sweeps.

**The gate is armed, per regime.** A cold run drops the page cache and reads
3.00 GiB off the SSD, so it stalls on I/O for a fifth of its window *by
construction* — p95 21.3% against a warm figure's 5.4%. One global I/O limit
would fire on every cold reading or none, so `psi_io_some_pct` is gated in
neither regime and the limits that are set (`cpu_busy_pct` 15,
`psi_cpu_some_pct` 5, `cpu_steal_pct` 2) sit at roughly 3× the observed p95.
That per-regime split is the finding the calibration run produced; it was not
visible before there was telemetry to look at.

**A declared dependency that had never been checked.** `session-drift` declares
`scripts/measure.py` — the harness's own timing path is the apparatus it
measures — but `figures_touched` iterated the *sweep* list, which excludes the
derived figure, so the declaration was inert. It now iterates the register, and
`--stale` tells a derived figure to re-take itself with `--drift` rather than
sending the reader to an hour-long sweep.

**The composite column's share moved again** — +0.62 µs/row against a −0.17
floor, per-rep ranges still not overlapping. Four sweeps have now read that one
quantity at +0.31, +0.39, +0.62 and +0.99 µs/row, a spread wider than the
quantity, which is why `measurements.md` still reads it as a bound. The STATUS
entry stays open with its numbers updated.
