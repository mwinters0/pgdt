# 2026-09-16

## A plain `query`'s parallelism is bounded by the batch pin, not by the budget

Reviewing 20.6's first "Decisions worth another look" entry established where
a plain `query`'s one-piece arrangement actually comes from, which the entry
stated but did not follow. The call the entry raised — a stated `--memory`
leaving a plain dump on `DEFAULT_MEMORY_BUDGET`, exactly as a discovered limit
always did — **stands**, and `decisions.md`, "D83" is unchanged.

**The binding term is `QueryOptions::max_source_span`**, defaulted to `64 << 20`
at `pgdump_query/src/batch.rs:146` and added per-worker by `plan_partitions`
(`pgdump_query/src/stream.rs`). A plain source's own footprint is 8 MiB
(`PLAIN_PARTITION_CHUNKS × chunk`), so the charge is 72 MiB a worker and
`at(2)` is 144 MiB against a 64 MiB budget: `WorkerMemory::affords` floors to
one. The span is a **caller-side batch pin**, not the source's cost, and it
equals `DEFAULT_MEMORY_BUDGET` by coincidence of two lineages, which is what
makes the budget account for exactly one reader.

**`--chunk-size` cannot reach it.** The chunk term is 8 MiB of the 72; even at
a chunk of zero, `at(2)` is 128 MiB against 64. The manual is accurate on this
— [`../../manual/dump-inspection.md`](../../manual/dump-inspection.md),
"`--jobs` and `--memory`" names the batch term rather than the chunk term —
and its `--chunk-size` sentence is about what a plain read *holds*, a narrower
claim than restoring a split.

**What was retired is one lever, not the behaviour.** A flagless run has always
resolved 64 MiB on plain, before and after 20.6, a discovered limit reaching
the same zero-cost arm of `Parallelism::fit`. Only a stated `--parallel-memory`
ever beat it.

**The option the entry's binary excluded** — neither billing the plain source a
real per-reader cost (`KD25`) nor handing a zero-cost source the whole cap
(`D3`) — is to derive the span rather than fix it, which
[`../../design/roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md), "Two tunables fit pgdq to
hardware: memory and parallelism" already prescribes: the implementation
interprets the two numbers "however it must — a pool's depth, a statistic's
granularity, whether a path is afforded at all". `plan_partitions` receives the
`Parallelism` and the span together, so the derivation needs no new plumbing
and no third flag, which that rule forbids.

Admitted as `M111`
([`../../design/out-of-band.md`](../../design/out-of-band.md)) and landed the
same day; what it settled is the section below.

## What 20.6 did to the `parallel-*` legs

Settled by arithmetic against the carving, no run taken. `PARALLEL_LEGS`
(`scripts/measure.py`) is four legs, and the stated
`--memory stated_allowance(PARALLEL_BUDGET)` reaches them differently now that
`Parallelism::within` carves a typed number.

- **Plain, `parse`** — 64 MiB budget from `fit`'s zero-cost arm against an
  8 MiB footprint: eight readers. `PARALLEL_JOBS` runs to 24, so its 12, 16 and
  24 rows now resolve the same eight workers as its 8 row.
- **Plain, typed `query`** — the same 64 MiB against 8 MiB plus the 64 MiB
  `max_source_span`: **one** sub-stream at every count. The leg's axis is flat,
  so it no longer measures what the figure registers it for.
- **The two `.xz` legs** — a real `WorkerMemory`, so `margin_allowance` binds
  where it did not before: at a 2432 MiB allowance the ceiling is 1,689 MiB
  against a 2,048 MiB cap. **Their resolved count is unchanged** — 29 readers
  afforded instead of 35, both past the axis's top of 24 — but the *budget*
  they announce falls from the stated 2,048 MiB to `memory.at(24)`, 1,369 MiB,
  because the old path passed `fit`'s count through while keeping the typed
  number as the budget. That budget is what clamps the pool's slots (`KD21`),
  so it is `parallel-peak-rss`, not the throughput table, that moves on this
  leg; by how much is 20.9's to read.

Before the change a stated budget was taken whole — `recommended_within` passed
`fit`'s *count* through but kept `memory_bytes` as the budget — so all four legs
ran at 2 GiB.

**`QUERY_SUBSTREAM_CAP` carried a reading that had gone false**, and is
corrected here. Its docstring said the dict was empty at `PARALLEL_BUDGET`
"rather than an omission", because no leg's count fell inside `PARALLEL_JOBS`;
the plain typed-`query` leg's does now, at one. It is hand-computed by design —
a mirrored Python reimplementation of `worker_count` would be a second
authority — so nothing failed and no `--stale` could see it. The dict now reads
`{"control": 1}`, its arithmetic is restated against the new carving, and the
two tests that pinned the empty reading assert the new one.

## `M111`: a plain `query`'s batch span is derived, and `--jobs` buys readers again

The rule and its floor are
[`../../design/decisions.md`](../../design/decisions.md), "D84"; the mechanism
is `plan_partitions` and `derived_source_span` in `pgdump_query/src/stream.rs`.
`QueryOptions::max_source_span` is now a ceiling that `table_stream_partitions`
spends against the budget before it cuts the count, and the value it settles on
is written back onto the sub-streams' own options, so the charge the plan was
solved against is the size the batches actually flush at.

**The floor is `MIN_SOURCE_SPAN`, one shipped read chunk**
(`pgdump_query/src/batch.rs`), settled by arithmetic rather than a reading: a
chunk-shaped source retains by the read chunk, so below that unit the span
stops bounding anything a batch could avoid holding while still costing rows
per batch. It is defined as `DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE` rather than repeating its
value, the argument being about the unit.

**What a plain `query` now resolves**, at `DEFAULT_MEMORY_BUDGET` against an
8 MiB reader — the whole axis, pinned by
`stream::tests::a_stated_span_is_spent_down_to_seat_the_readers_and_no_further`:
one sub-stream at `--jobs 1` (the stated span already affords it, so nothing is
narrowed), two at 24 MiB of span, four at 8 MiB, and seven from `--jobs 8`
upward, where the span is on its floor and the count is what gives. The
shortfall past seven is a `ParallelismBudgetLimited` as before; the narrowing
itself is a new `PlanNoteKind::BatchSpanNarrowed`, printed by the CLI as a
`note:`. The two legs of `pgdump_query-cli/tests/statistics.rs` that 20.6 left
planning one sub-stream plan three again, and each now *asserts* the count off
that note rather than assuming it.

**`QUERY_SUBSTREAM_CAP` is `{"control": 7}`** (`scripts/measure.py`), still
hand-computed on its own argument; the `.xz` legs are untouched, a
partition-retaining source never being charged the span. Every `parallel-*`
figure is stale on that path, which 20.9 re-takes.

`KD25` is unchanged: pricing a plain reader properly still wants the reading of
a parallel plain scan on a real device that its entry asks for.
