"`KD17` struck: the cap is `pgdt query`'s merge"). `peak-rss`'s 4,000-block row

**What `M185` changed.** The rule and its reasoning are
[`../../design/measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md), "The
apparatus": the tmpfs budget is the constant `measure.warm_bound`, two
full-size inputs plus 64 MiB (6.06 GiB at 3.00 GiB inputs), every figure's warm
set is asserted to fit it, preflight reserves it by `fallocate`, a staging
failure aborts the sweep, and `Stager.warm_path` records an input as staged
only once its copy has finished. The standing rule "Re-take a comparison table
whole" there now says which tables may be split.

- **The splits.** `statistics-gathering` (12.00 GiB) and
  `scan-throughput-warm` (9.00 GiB) run one sweep per input; each of their
  rows stays within one input. `nested-end-to-end` (9.00 GiB) runs control
  with composite, then control with arrays (`M186`, below).
- **Every `scan-throughput-*` table has a `dd` floor per input**, and each row
  is read against its own floor, affirmed with the maintainer; the reasoning is
  beside `_THROUGHPUT_ROWS` in `scripts/measure.py`. The published tables have one floor until the sweep above
  re-takes them.
- `cross-file-floor` (6.00 GiB), `reserve` (4.37 GiB) and
  `parallel-scan-throughput` (3.68 GiB) fit unsplit. Every other figure's warm
  set is at most 3.01 GiB.

**The quota.** The 12.5G is systemd's default for a regular user: 80% of the
tmpfs's size at login, which is 13,109,721 KiB, applied by
`systemd-user-runtime-dir` to `/tmp` and `/dev/shm` (systemd 258 and later,
`devShmLimit`/`tmpLimit` in the user record). For an `/etc/passwd` user, only a
whole-record userdb drop-in changes it, and only from the next login.
`statvfs` and `df` cannot see the quota; `quotactl_fd` with `Q_GETQUOTA` reads
it without privilege. `shmem_fallocate` charges the quota exactly as a write
does, and gives back everything it added when it fails (Linux 7.1,
`mm/shmem.c`). A tmpfs that root mounts separately carries no quota. The
machine has 31 GiB of RAM, and `/dev/shm` and `/tmp` are each a 16G tmpfs.

## `nested-end-to-end` co-measures control with each nested file

Reviewed with the maintainer: `M185`'s premise that none of its three splits
subtracts one input from another was false for `nested-end-to-end`. Besides
`cross-file-floor`'s row 1 (`_per_row_diffs(session, "nested-end-to-end",
"control", "composite")` in `scripts/measure.py`), the section's headline —
"A typed query over nested columns costs 6.6 µs a row more than a string one"
in `../../design/measurements.md` — is the arrays file's per-row difference
minus control's, and its "untyped baseline is file-dependent" finding compares
the files' `strings` legs. Under `M185`'s grouping the headline would subtract
across sweeps, and a 5.6% session shift ("What a session's own drift costs")
on the arrays file's typed−strings gap is about 0.46 µs/row, the whole of
`cross-file-floor`'s resolution.

Settled, and landed as out-of-band `M186`, which blocked P28 because 28.9's
sweep re-takes the figure:

- **Control is measured in both sweeps**, (control, composite) and (control,
  arrays), each nested file read against its own sweep's control, the headline
  a per-rep paired arrays−control difference as `_per_row_diffs` takes one,
  the table carrying a control row per sweep. One extra input's reps, inside
  `measure.warm_bound`. *Rejected:* the landed grouping, which publishes a
  cross-sweep subtraction the standing rule forbids; retiring the cross-file
  readings, which gives up the finding the three files exist for.
- **Inputs share a sweep wherever anything read off their reps subtracts one
  input's reading from another's**, rep by rep or median against median — a
  table row, a sentence or heading, or another figure consuming them — not
  only the figure's own rows. A reading normalised within its own sweep — a
  shape against its own floor, a share against a seed floor — may be set
  beside another sweep's, not subtracted from it; a raw absolute is quoted
  within its own sweep. `measurements.md`'s "Re-take a comparison table whole"
  states it.
- **The rule has a check**: a figure declares each cross-input subtraction it
  reads, a consumer's included (`Figure.subtracts`, `measure.Subtraction`),
  `test_measure.py` asserts each declared pair lies in one of its source
  figure's sweeps, and `subtraction_sweep` refuses an undeclared pair for
  `_per_row_diffs`. The second sweep's control is its own reading, keyed
  `…/sweep=1` (`RunSpec.sweep`, `in_sweep`); the first's key names no sweep,
  and it is what `cross-file-floor` and the allocator table read. Declared beside the
  nested pair: `peak-rss`'s rows against its pivot, `rss-attribution`'s
  slopes, and the series `per-block-quadratic` and `map-only` read row
  against row. A leg read against its own floor or one-worker row is
  normalised within its sweep and declares nothing.
- **`cross-file-floor`'s two rows stay in separate sweeps**, each paired
  within its own: setting the share beside the floor is a calibration, not a
  subtraction, and all three files together exceed the bound; its prose says
  so.

## `KD17` struck: the cap is `pgdt query`'s merge

Reviewed with the maintainer. `KD17` said the library's replay sub-streams gain
little from running concurrently, for no identified reason. They do run
concurrently: `parallel-scan-throughput`'s typed-query columns time
`pgdt query`, whose in-order merge refills only the slot it printed from, so
past the first round one sub-stream reads (`pgdt/src/main.rs`, `Slot`). The
2026-09-09 entry recorded that steady state; nothing connected it to `KD17`.
Evidence, one-off probes and not figures, in `runs/kd57-probe-20261001/`: the
same `.xz` scan through `datafusion-cli-pgdump` reads 16.45 s at one partition
and 1.91 s at twenty-four, plain 0.63 s to 0.35 s, while `pgdt query` over the
same plain columns is flat; the sweep's own `.xz` query legs climb from 19.4 s
to 30 s of user CPU at a flat wall clock.

- **`KD17` is struck**, its subject false. `KD57` files `pgdt query`'s serial
  steady state, **(c) unowned**, its marker on `Slot`.
- **`M187` moves `parallel-scan-throughput`'s query legs to the provider**,
  `target_partitions` the axis, so the strike rests on a figure; until it
  lands those columns time the merge, as the figure's prose says.
- **`M188` takes the concurrency clause off `ParallelismBudgetLimited`**, the
  library being unable to know how its caller drains the sub-streams; until it
  lands, `stream.rs` still cites `KD17` there. `pgdt query`'s manual states its
  own limit, and P22's inbox entry is reduced to its `KD32` fact.
- **`pgdt query` keeps planning N sub-streams**; the reason is at `KD57`'s
  marker.
