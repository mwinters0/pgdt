# 2026-09-12

## The reserve entry closes on 384, and the grilling found the charge wrong below four readers

`19.16`'s pick stands: **`MEMORY_RESERVE` ships at 384 MiB**, and `19.13`
carries it. Reconstructed against the criterion rather than read off the
probe's verdict, the pick is forced on this grid — at `1g` the criterion caps
the count at 11 (816.9 MiB passes, 827.3 fails), which needs a reserve in
(322, 381]; at `1536m` it caps at 19 (1224.4 passes, 1279.5 fails), needing
(367, 425]. 384 is the only candidate on the grid inside both. 512 was the
alternative the entry raised and it is declined: the criterion was registered
before the sitting, and choosing differently after reading the grid is what
this phase has twice been burned by.

**What the reserve is covering is not what `19.18` said it was.** Over the
block-path regime the worst-resident slope is 57.28 MiB a reader against the
58.03 [`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs)'s `block_reader_bytes` bills —
**0.987**, so the per-reader charge is right to 1.3% and glibc's arena
retention is already inside it. What sits above the charge is flat: the excess
`worst − 58.03 × jobs` is 135.7 MiB at two readers and 145.4 at twenty-four,
wandering 83.6–214.6 with no trend across jobs 2…24. `19.18`'s reading that
retention "rises with the count the allowance affords" is true of `fordblks`
and does not reach the reserve.

### The charge under-bills the pool floor, and the record says it does not

[`roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md)'s Future item "A block cache whose
retention floor tracks the reader count" states that P19 "charges the floor
honestly". **It does not.** `BufferPool::slots` clamps the block pool at
`POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` ([`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs), the
`set_limits` call in `XzSource::hint_parallelism`'s block-cache sibling), so
the pool holds at least four units however few readers there are, while
`block_reader_bytes` bills `2 × unit` a reader — which assumes `slots == jobs`.
Below four readers we hold units nobody charged for.

The size of it is `(POOL_DEPTH − jobs) × unit`, and `19.16`'s own readings
confirm it to 1.4 MiB on the 128 MiB-block file:

| jobs | worst RSS | budget | excess | `(4−jobs) × unit` | residual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 802.1 | 532.1 | 270.0 | 256.0 | 14.0 |
| 3 | 939.9 | 798.1 | 141.8 | 128.0 | 13.8 |
| 4 | 1077.7 | 1064.1 | 13.6 | 0.0 | 13.6 |
| 5 | 1342.6 | 1330.2 | 12.4 | 0.0 | 12.4 |
| 6 | 1607.5 | 1596.2 | 11.3 | 0.0 | 11.3 |

Five cells, residual flat at 11–14 MiB. That is an account, not a fit, and it
is derivable from `readings.json` with no new sitting.

**It is unbounded in the block size** — 96 MiB on koji's 24 MiB blocks, 384 MiB
at 128 MiB, 2 GiB on a 512 MiB-block file — which is why no constant can absorb
it and why it is a charge defect rather than a reserve one. It is also *not*
what 384 pays for: on the 24 MiB file the term is 48/24/0 MiB and the mid-range
failures the reserve was raised for are all at eleven readers and above, where
it is zero.

### The 24-worker cap is load-bearing and nothing said so

`2g` clears the criterion only because `available_parallelism` clamps 28
readers to this machine's 24. On a 64-core host the same limit resolves 28,
costing 1637 MiB of a 1664 MiB budget and predicting ~1774 MiB resident against
a 1638 MiB ceiling — **13.3%, a failure**, on a machine no leg ran. 384 is
validated to 2 GiB *on 24 cores*, and the phase's own grid cannot see the
difference.

### The arena question, answered against the record

Whether the reserve should be configured from the arena count was raised and is
declined, on grounds already written: the spec's "The arena cap is a deployment
setting, not a mechanism this binary ships" refuses `mallopt(M_ARENA_MAX, …)`
because a cap keyed to the resolved worker count is unreachable — glibc seeds an
arena per thread at that thread's first allocation, and the source-dependent
count arrives after source recognition, which is I/O. Nothing in the new
readings reopens it.

Introspecting the *maximum* is separately the wrong number. Observed arenas are
`readers + 2` (6/15/24/26 at 4/13/22/24 readers) against a glibc ceiling of
`8 × ncores` = 192 here, so our own thread count binds and the maximum never
does; there is no getter for `M_ARENA_MAX` in any case. The lever that exists
is setting it, which the spec section above already refuses.

## A `parse` reports what was asked for and never what ran

Grilling the "Decisions worth another look" entry `19.13` left open — a
declined block path is reported on `query` and not on `parse` — found the
entry's framing too narrow in two places and its coverage claim true in only
one arrangement. The entry is closed; the work it implies is `M87` and `19.25`.

**The decline costs `parse` throughput, not only `query`.** The entry leaned on
`PlanNoteKind::CompressedBlockPathDeclined`'s own doc, which says the mapping
pass "only reads forward, costs the same either way". That holds for *backward*
reads and is not the whole cost: `leader::scan_region` returns
`RegionScan::Declined` when `advice.max_partitions() == Some(1)`, which is
exactly the shape a declined source advertises and is how
`stream::compressed_block_path_declined` detects it. So a declined `.xz`
`parse` is serial whatever `--jobs` says.

**The mode report covered the decline in one arrangement only.**
`ParallelArgs::resolve_in`'s stated-budget arm returned
`Parallelism::workers(jobs, stated)` without consulting
`Parallelism::discover_in`, so a recommended count was never lowered when
`--parallel-memory` was stated. `pgdq parse --parallel-memory 33554432` against
a large-block `.xz` on a 24-core host reported `24 (recommended by the
source)`, ran one serial reader with the block path declined, and said nothing
else — a count that would not be delivered, printed as a recommendation. That
also falsified `Resolved::jobs_display`'s doc comment, which claimed a
recommendation "is lowered to what the allowance affords and printed lowered".
`M87` closes it, below.

**So the gap is the delivered count, not the decline.** On `parse` nothing ever
reports that the delivered worker count differs from the announced one, and the
only mechanism that corrects the announcement is a plan note `parse` cannot
see; `query` is covered on both counts. Printing a decline beside a `jobs=24`
line that is itself wrong is a worse end state than today's silence, and the
general statement is the one a future source inherits — "the block path" is one
container format's word for it.

**The report is the library's, and provenance stays the CLI's.** The entry
offered two routes and priced them; a third exists that neither considered.
`stream::map_forward` already emits `scan started` on both commands
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Status
output"), so the fact can be stated where it is known — `leader::scan_region`
is the only party that sees the advice — without a new public channel and
without the CLI re-deriving the source's arithmetic, which
`compressed_block_path_declined` refuses by name. The CLI keeps
`Resolved::plan_note_origin`'s clause: the library says what bound, the CLI
says where the number came from.

**A stated budget does not exempt a recommended count from the allowance.** The
standing rule is "never allocate workers there is no memory for"
([`../../design/roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md), "A default runs as fast
as the allocation permits"), and `resolve_in` scopes it to "the absence of a
flag and never its presence" — but the flag whose presence is respected there
is `--jobs`, and in this arrangement `--jobs` is absent. Nothing states that
stating a budget should make a recommended count stop answering to it, so the
asymmetry is an oversight rather than a decision. `M87` removes it.

## `M87`: a recommended count answers to a stated budget too

`ParallelArgs::resolve_in` now lowers a **recommended** worker count on both
arms. Where `--parallel-memory` is typed and `--jobs` is not, the source's
recommendation is cut to what those bytes afford, through
`Parallelism::recommended_within` — `discover_for`'s own `fit`, over a budget
that arrived typed rather than discovered. So `pgdq parse --parallel-memory
67108864` against a 24 MiB-block `.xz` on a 24-core host reports `jobs=1`
instead of the `24 (recommended by the source)` it announced beside one serial
reader.

**Only the count moves.** Discovery hands back the bytes the lowered count
spends, because it chose them and must not name a budget the allowance never
granted; a stated budget is taken whole, a stated flag winning outright. The
asymmetry with `--jobs` is unchanged: a count somebody typed is printed as
typed, and what it delivers stays `stream::worker_count`'s to decide.

**The lowering clause names which budget did it.** `by the allocation` where
the number came off the environment, `by the stated budget` where it was typed
— the recourse is a different number in each case, and pointing at a cgroup
nobody set would be the new arm's own way of misleading. `Resolved` already
carries `budget_stated`, so the discriminator is exact: a stated budget wins
over a discovered limit, so it is what lowered the count whenever it is
present.

It is an out-of-band row rather than a slice because no spec'd decision moves:
the standing rule scopes the lowering to the absence of `--jobs`, which is
absent on this arm, so what landed is the rule already written applied where it
had not been reaching.

## The library default declines the block path, and the charge is why

`19.22` made `BlockCache::affordable` bill one reader's pool floor, so the line
a budget must clear went from `2 × unit + chunk + decode` to `5 × unit + chunk +
decode` — 130 MiB at koji's 24 MiB blocks against 58, and 650 at 128 MiB blocks
against 266. Under `DEFAULT_MEMORY_BUDGET`'s 64 MiB that declines every ordinary
`.xz` dump, where a 24 MiB-block file previously took the block path. The call
to leave the constant where it is was reviewed and stands; the reasoning is
beside the gate ([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md),
"Execution model and API surface").

**The constant was never the thing that changed.** Two units alone is 48 MiB and
the chunk and decoder take ~10.5 more, so a 64 MiB grant has 4.6 MiB of headroom
and no pooling rule fits inside it — `slots = jobs + 1`, the least that does not
drain the retention list before every decode, still wants 82.5 MiB at one reader.
The path was reachable only while the charge billed a floor of zero. So the
default did not regress; the charge stopped concealing what the default had
always granted.

**The entry's own argument for leaving it was backwards and is not what was
filed.** It held that moving the constant would make one number answer two
unrelated questions. `affordable` compares against the budget in force and that
budget's fallback *is* this constant, so it already decides the compressed gate
— `MEMORY_RESERVE`'s doc comment says the same thing from the other end, that
the reserve and the decline are one knob. What keeps the number honest is that
it stays derived from the serial path, which is the smallest grant costing the
plain path nothing; sizing it to clear a block-path gate is what would pick it
for the second question.

**"The CLI is unaffected" was wrong in a narrow band.** The CLI's budget is
`limit − MEMORY_RESERVE`, so the block path needs an allocation of about
515 MiB. At 512 MiB — the size `19.16` took its 400 runs in — `Parallelism::fit`
now returns one worker at a 128 MiB budget and the gate then refuses at 130.5,
where before `19.22` the same cgroup ran two block readers at 118.8. This is the
right answer rather than a defect: the sweep at that limit already says the
block path buys nothing there, two readers being 6% slower than declining. Its
consequence is for the apparatus, not the mechanism, and is `M88`.

## `M88`: the path a leg ran is read off the budget it reported

Every flagless `reserve` cell names `*block path*` or `*streaming*`, and the
harness asks that question in one place: `block_path_afforded` mirrors
`BlockCache::affordable` as `charge_bytes(unit, 1)` — the per-reader term plus
that one reader's pool floor, which `19.22` put inside it.

Three of the renderer's four sites compared the reported budget against
`reader_bytes` alone; the fourth, `19.24`'s model check, did not. The line
`19.22` moved is 58.03 MiB → **130.0** at 24 MiB blocks and 266 → **650** at
128, so a stale mirror mislabels whole cells rather than getting an edge case
wrong:

- `-m 512m` grants `512 − 384 = 128 MiB`, below 130.0, so **both** inputs read
  the streaming fallback on the axis's bottom row. The 24 MiB cell there is one
  of the two the old mirror called block path.
- `-m 1g` grants 640 MiB, below the 128 MiB leg's 650 and above the 266 the old
  mirror compared against, so that is the other one. Nothing else moved: at
  512 MiB the 128 MiB leg was already declined, and `1536m`/`2g` clear both
  lines. The 128 MiB fit is therefore over two legs where it was over three.
- The path step's two budgets straddled `reader_bytes`, which both legs now
  clear and neither reaches, so the pair would have run the streaming decoder
  twice and published its difference as the cost of a path neither took. They
  are `charge_bytes(unit, 1)` and one byte below it.

Two apparatus claims said 512 MiB is the smallest allocation reaching the block
path. Both are corrected where they stand, in `RESERVE_LIMITS` and
`RESERVE_MECHANISM_LIMIT`: that row is kept as the bottom of the axis because
the rule's own worst headroom is there and a fallback leg is still a leg the
allocation must hold, and the path step beside it is the one pair that states a
budget, precisely so the block path can be reached inside that container.

**The row's own mechanism is wrong, and the correction is narrower rather than
wider.** It reads that the leg already knows, `stream::compressed_block_path_declined`
being a `PlanNote` — but `parse` announces no plan notes at all
([`pgdump_query-cli/src/main.rs`](../../../pgdump_query-cli/src/main.rs),
`announce_plan_notes`, reached only from the `query` arm), and
`scripts/measure.py`'s own `RESERVE_FLAGLESS_INPUTS` comment already said so
before the row was written. What the run *does* report is the budget it
resolved, on its own `scan started` line, and the path follows from it by the
library's arithmetic — so this stays bookkeeping over a reading rather than
becoming the library change `19.25` owns.

## `POOL_DEPTH` is the plain path's depth, and the block pool inherits it

Filed against `KD21` and the `roadmap.md` Future item on the retention floor,
which share the question and neither of which was carrying this. `POOL_DEPTH` is
the free-list depth a *chunk* pool needs so that a flushed batch's release does
not find an empty list, sized against a chunk of at most 8 MiB. The block pool
takes it as a floor while its unit is whatever block the file was written with —
up to 512 MiB — so the constant governing that floor was never chosen against
that pool's unit. `XzSource::apportion` defends only the minimum, that a block
pool of one drains before every decode, and that argues for two rather than
four. Neither has been measured against what block retention buys a seeking
query, which is the reading both the entry and the Future item wait on.

## The gate never reaches the pool floor, and the 128 MiB family was never a fit

`M88` corrected the harness's mirror of `BlockCache::affordable` and the
consequence was that two of the eight flagless cells changed mechanism without
moving. Grilling that consequence found a larger one underneath it.

**The arithmetic, recomputed from the harness's own mirrors.** `POOL_DEPTH` is
4 and `MEMORY_RESERVE` 384 MiB, so `charge_bytes(unit, 1)` — the line
`BlockCache::affordable` compares a budget against — is **130.03 MiB** at 24 MiB
blocks and **650.03** at 128, and the smallest container limit affording one
block-decoding reader is **514.03** and **1034.03 MiB**:

| `-m` | budget | 24 MiB blocks | 128 MiB blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| `512m` | 128 MiB | streaming | streaming |
| `1g` | 640 | block, 11r | streaming — 650.03 against 640 |
| `1536m` | 1152 | block, 19r | block, 4r |
| `2g` | 1664 | block, ≤24r on this box's clamp | block, 6r |

The 11 and 19 agree with what `19.16` read off the sitting, which is the
independent check on the mirror.

**No published cell bills a pool floor.** `pool_floor_bytes` is
`max(0, 4 − jobs) × unit`, so it is clamped off at four readers, and every
block-path leg above resolves 4, 6, 11, 19 or 24. The model check's "of which
pool floor" column therefore reads `—` in all five rows it prints. That is the
term `19.22` added, `19.24` gave a named column, and `M88` spent a round
correcting the mirror of, and `19.11`'s amended acceptance — the verdict holds
"at every evaluated cell" — is satisfiable without the criterion ever evaluating
it. The over-bill side is what a clamped term stresses, and it is the side a grid
search over reserve constants cannot report at all.

The windows where a flagless cell *would* bill the term are disjoint, so no
single limit covers both block sizes:

| readers | 24 MiB: limit ≥ | floor | 128 MiB: limit ≥ | floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 514.03 MiB | 72 MiB | 1034.03 MiB | 384 MiB |
| 2 | 548.07 | 48 | 1172.07 | 256 |
| 3 | 582.10 | 24 | 1310.10 | 128 |
| 4 | 616.13 | 0 | 1448.13 | 0 |

**Raising the bottom of the axis is the wrong remedy, and so is extending it
past `2g`.** Both were the shapes the closed entry offered. More allowance means
more readers means the term stays clamped at zero, so neither reaches it; and
`2g` is already where the 24 MiB count saturates on the source's recommendation
rather than on the allowance, so nothing above it is informative. What reaches it
is a limit *inserted* under the clamp — `544m` and `1088m`, `M89`, now
registered. Additive, so `512m` keeps the axis's worst headroom and no existing
reading moves; the axis is 12 flagless legs and 7 instrument legs where it was 8
and 5, which is 38 legs against 32.

**The 128 MiB family was never owed a fit.** The spec already says so — "the
128 MiB flagless family is the repair's acceptance gate, not a source of
numbers" — and its acceptance is that every leg either takes the block path and
survives its allocation or declines it, none killed. So the two-point line the
closed entry worried about is not a thin deliverable; it is a fit the renderer
publishes over a family whose spec says it produces none. The guard is the fix,
and it refuses a fit below **3** distinct reader counts where it refused below 2:
a two-term model has no residual at two points, and `±0 MiB` printed beside a
real residual cannot be told from one. `measure.RESERVE_FIT_MIN_COUNTS` is that
bound; `_least_squares` keeps its own floor of two, which is where the arithmetic
rather than the publication stops. `1088m`'s value is the floor cell and the
gate, not the third point — the reason the closed entry gave for it was the
weaker half.

That also makes `544m` the more valuable of the two for the fit, since the
24 MiB family *is* the fit family: a 1-reader point against a window of 11 to 24
is the smallest arrangement the mechanism has, which is where a fit is checked
(`.claude/skills/evidence/SKILL.md`, rule 2 — a term fixed at one end of an axis
and per-unit at the other is a clamped term, and this one is a `max(0, …)`).

**The axis is checked rather than remembered.** `reserve_floor_problems` is a
`--check` refusal beside the worker-count ones: a block size bills the floor
exactly where some registered limit's discovered budget — `limit −
MEMORY_RESERVE`, the library's own rule mirrored — affords one block-decoding
reader and cannot afford `POOL_DEPTH` of them, and an axis reaching neither
window fails naming the window to register. It argues from the registered limits
rather than from a sitting, which is what lets it run before any reading exists;
the resolved count is `min(recommendation, fit)`, so a machine recommending fewer
readers lowers the count and *raises* the floor, making the window a sufficient
condition everywhere rather than a prediction of this box.

**Also corrected**: `pool_floor_bytes`' docstring illustrated the term as
"96 MiB at koji's 24 MiB blocks, 384 at 128, 2 GiB at 512". No reader count
makes all three true — 96 and 2048 are the values at zero readers, 384 the value
at one. It now states them at one reader, where the term is largest, and names
the two limits that bill the term against every other cell, which is zero.

## The three-count fit guard belongs to the model, and below three counts a secant is publishable

`M89` raised the flagless family's fit guard to three distinct reader counts and
named the constant `RESERVE_FIT_MIN_COUNTS`. Reviewing that choice settled where
the guard lives and what it withholds. `M90` is the work.

**The guard is a property of two-term least squares, not of either family.**
`resident = fixed + readers × per_reader` passes exactly through two points, so
at two distinct abscissae the residual printed beside it is zero by
construction — true wherever the model is fitted. `scripts/measure.py` has three
`_least_squares` call sites across two families: the flagless fit, its min/max
band, and the instrument account's `live_fit`. `M89` guarded one. The guard
therefore moves to the `_least_squares` boundary, where a fourth call site
cannot skip it, and the constant stops naming one family.

**The second site was never a decision.** `live_fit` admits a fit at two
distinct counts, and its refusal prose — *a line through one point is an
intercept asserted as a measurement* — restates `_least_squares`' own docstring.
That is the arithmetic floor copied inline, so there was no considered asymmetry
between the two families to defend.

**What narrows the instrument axis is a kill, not an axis edit.**
`RESERVE_INSTRUMENT_LIMITS` is derived from `RESERVE_LIMITS`, so that axis cannot
be shrunk on its own, and `M89` widened it to six limits spanning one to
twenty-four readers. `account_points` admits a leg only when it is neither killed
nor unarranged, and a kill removes the high-memory end — so the instrument
family's coverage is a **per-sitting** property that degrades under exactly the
condition `19.17.1` built the censoring machinery for. Two killed legs and the
instrument fit publishes in the same sitting where the flagless one refuses.

**Below three counts the slope survives and the intercept does not.** Both traps
behind the rule (the `evidence` skill, rule 2) are statements about the
intercept: curvature outside the window folds into it, and a clamped term reads
as a constant of the process. A difference between two distinct reader counts is
a measured secant and carries no model claim, so refusing the whole fit withholds
a number that is sound. Both families therefore print the **secant** below three
counts, naming the two legs it connects and offering no intercept and no
residual. That keeps `19.18`'s `98% of reader_bytes` comparison — the one reading
that says whether the charge the budget rule divides by is the charge a reader is
— alive in a censored sitting, where refusing the fit withdraws it silently.

**The registered axis is checked for fit-ability**, folded into
`reserve_floor_problems` rather than given a walk of its own: it argues from the
same registered limits and the same discovered budgets. It passes on both
families today, so what it buys is a later axis edit failing before a sitting
rather than after; the censoring case is covered by the secant, which is why the
static check is not where the guarantee comes from.

**It points the other way from the floor half it sits beside**, which is worth
saying because the two live in one function. The floor's window is *sufficient*
on every machine: the resolved count is `min(recommendation, fit)`, so a smaller
recommendation lowers the count and raises the floor. Fit-ability is only
*necessary*: the same cap collapses two distinct fits onto one count, so a host
with fewer cores than the axis has distinct fits publishes a secant on an axis
the check passed. The registered limits afford `[1, 11, 12, 19, 28]` readers at
24 MiB blocks and `[1, 4, 6]` at 128, so the second family sits exactly on the
bound.

## The margin applies the criterion twice, because `MEMORY_RESERVE` already contains one

`19.23` put the 20% criterion in front of the worker count and predicted
resident as `charge(n) + MEMORY_RESERVE`. That prediction re-subtracts a margin
the reserve already carries, and the review closing the entry it raised settled
that it is a double application rather than a conservative choice.

**The whole effect of the margin, at every evaluated leg, is `0.2 × limit`:**

| leg | count before `19.23` | after | drop | `0.2 × limit ÷ 58.03 MiB` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xz24 `1g` | 11 | 7 | 4 | 204.8 ÷ 58.03 = 3.5 |
| xz24 `1536m` | 19 | 14 | 5 | 307.2 ÷ 58.03 = 5.3 |
| xz24 `2g` | 28 | 21 | 7 | 409.6 ÷ 58.03 = 7.1 |

A modelling refinement does not reproduce the margin term to within the
discreteness of a reader at three limits; a second application of the same rule
does.

**The reason is how `19.16` defined the constant.** 384 MiB is the *smallest*
reserve whose worst rep leaves ≥20% under the cap rule `charge ≤ limit −
reserve`, so at the leg that decided it the two constraints are the same one.
At xz24 `1g`, r384's worst rep left 20.2% of 1024 MiB — resident 817.2 MiB — and
`11 × 58.03 = 638.3` of that is charge, putting the flat term at **178.9 MiB**.
The criterion's charge bound is then `819.2 − 178.9 = 640.3 MiB` against a cap
of exactly **640**. So `MEMORY_RESERVE = 0.2 × 1 GiB + flat term`, to within
0.3 MiB, and feeding it back in as the predicted flat term subtracts the
`0.2 × limit` it already holds.

**This refutes the reason the call was made.** The entry, and
[`architecture.md`](../design/architecture.md)'s paragraph beside the
mechanism, argued that the alternative was "a number picked off a probe to
loosen a safety rule". Separating the two terms does not loosen the criterion —
it stops enforcing it twice — and the arithmetic above needs no new reading:
`19.16` states that its account sections "were derived from `readings.json` by
arithmetic — no reading in them needs re-taking".

**What the margin is still for is the shape mismatch**, which is sound and
unchanged: a constant reserve leaves a shrinking *share* as the limit grows, so
a 64-core host resolves 28 readers at 2 GiB and is predicted to breach at 13.3%.
Fixing the large end by subtracting a constant margin at every limit is what
over-corrects.

**Two facts the entry did not weigh.** The measured flat range 83.6–214.6 MiB
predates `19.19` and `19.22`, which moved the pool floor into the charge, so
today's unnamed remainder is smaller than that range at low counts and any bound
taken from it is conservative in the safe direction. And the margin cannot reach
`M89`'s pool-floor gate legs whatever constant it uses: `Parallelism::fit` takes
`cap.min(ceiling)`, and at `544m` the cap is 160 MiB — below any allowance a
smaller constant produces — so `19.11`'s acceptance cells are not at risk.

**Admitted as `19.26`**, a numbered slice rather than a ledger row: it changes a
decision the spec records, so it takes a spec amendment and runs before `19.11`,
whose flagless axis it moves.

**The harness keeps two fault lines.** `charge_model_problem` faults a cell
whose unnamed remainder exceeds `MEMORY_RESERVE`. Moving that threshold to the
new constant alone would make a bound that is slightly low read as a failed
rule; keeping both, registered before the sitting, separates the two — over the
inner line is a finding about the bound, over the reserve is the rule not
holding. And the bound itself is **re-derived from `readings.json` under
today's charge model** rather than quoted from the 83.6–214.6 MiB range, which
was computed before the pool floor moved into the charge.

## The gate reads one fault line where the harness now has two

`19.26` gave `charge_model_problem` a second, tighter threshold at
`MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND` precisely so that *the number the count is predicted
against is wrong* could be told from *the discovery cannot keep this
arrangement inside its allocation*. The renderer it feeds does not tell them
apart: any fault at all emits `**The model is refuted, and by these cells:**`
([`measure.py`](../../../scripts/measure.py), the model verdict in the
`reserve` renderer), and that verdict is the whole of `19.11`'s amended
acceptance. So a cell between the two lines — which the constant's own doc
comment, the harness's own docstring and
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md) all describe as leaving the
allocation intact — blocks the sweep's box exactly as a breach of the rule
does.

**It is an ordering artifact, not a disagreement.** The acceptance clause was
written at `8833ed2`, when the check had one threshold and "the model holds"
could only mean the reserve; `19.26` added the inner line at `2e7e172` and
nothing re-read the clause that consumes the verdict. The lesson is narrow and
mechanical: a check that gains a fault line owes a pass over whatever reads its
verdict, because the verdict is where a distinction gets spent.

**The exposure is real rather than theoretical.** The worst remainder in hand
is 214.6 MiB against a 256 MiB bound, that apparatus scatters 32–43 MiB, and
the sweep resolves counts `19.16` never measured — 9/10/16/23 readers at
24 MiB blocks against a grid whose worst cell was at seventeen. A fresh
worst-rep in the 256–300 MiB band is an ordinary outcome and would bar
publication of a figure whose own cells say the allocation held.

So `19.11`'s acceptance is amended a third time to **no evaluated cell above
`MEMORY_RESERVE`**, and the verdict is to name which band each faulting cell is
in. `M91` is the work, and it blocks `P19`: sizing the library constant upward
to buy the gate clearance was the alternative and is refused — that chooses a
constant by what a harness threshold tolerates rather than by what the readings
say, and the readings say 256 (beside the mechanism, in
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Execution model and API
surface", where the same round's review of the bound's value is filed).

**What the harness does, since `M91` landed.** `charge_model_problem` returns a
`ChargeFault` carrying the band it faulted in — `over-bill`, `bound` or `rule`
— beside the sentence it already wrote, and `ChargeFault.bars_acceptance` is
true of the outer band alone. The `Criterion` column names that band where it
read `**refuted**`, and the verdict is written in two: the cells above
`MEMORY_RESERVE` under *The rule is refuted*, which says in those words that the
sweep's box does not tick, and the cells under it as *Inside the rule, and so
not a bar on the box*.

**A sitting re-derives the bound only where nothing bars.** Where every fault is
inside the reserve, the verdict prints the smallest 64 MiB step covering the
worst remainder over the evaluated cells — `19.26`'s own arithmetic, re-done
over the sitting in hand, which is what makes a bound finding publishable rather
than a re-take. Where a cell *is* above the reserve, the re-derivation is
withheld and says so: that cell's remainder is a reading of an arrangement the
rule did not keep inside its allocation, so sizing a constant to it would take
the number from the run that should not have happened.

**The over-bill band is not released, and `M92` is what says so.** `M91` gave
`bars_acceptance` the shape `band == BAND_RULE`, which released the over-bill
side along with the bound side; the review below reverses that half. The band
still travels with the fault — that part of `M91` stands and is what the repair
is written on.

## The gate names bands, and an over-bill is not one of the ones it releases

`charge_model_problem` has three fault lines, not two. The over-bill line — the
unnamed remainder must be non-negative — entered at `19.24` (`faf9f2d`), whose
notes register the criterion as two-sided *before the sitting* and give the
over-bill side its own paragraph: a charge that is too large shows up in a
headroom sweep as headroom, which reads as the constant being adequate, and
that asymmetry is why the row exists. `19.26` then split the upper side in two.

**The third amendment miscounted them**, saying the acceptance clause "was
written when `charge_model_problem` had one threshold; `19.26` gave it two" —
it was counting upper lines. Its operative sentence releases exactly one case,
*a cell above the bound alone*; its summary sentence is the shorthand *no
evaluated cell above `MEMORY_RESERVE`*, and `M91` implemented the shorthand. So
the over-bill side left the gate without anyone arguing it.

**The bound band's argument does not transfer.** That release rests on the
fault being plausibly apparatus: 214.6 MiB observed against a 256 MiB bound,
with 32–43 MiB of scatter. An over-bill has to exceed the *entire* rest of the
process's footprint — code, stacks, glibc arenas, everything `MEMORY_RESERVE`
covers — before the arithmetic can even report it, so it is never noise. The
"allocation intact" rationale, applied on its own, releases every fault that is
not a breach, which is the collapse `19.26` split the lines to prevent, run in
the other direction.

**A released band carries a remedy the sitting discharges; an over-bill has
none.** A bound fault re-derives `MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND` on `19.26`'s own
64 MiB grid, so the sitting publishes *and* the constant is repaired by
arithmetic already in hand. An over-bill prints its sentence and nothing is
owed. That asymmetry is the test for any further band: a band is released from
the gate only where the allocation is intact **and** the sitting itself
discharges the finding.

**One confound is known and deliberately not built against.** `charge_bytes` is
a capacity — `jobs × reader_bytes(unit) + pool_floor_bytes`, mirroring
`WorkerMemory::at` — while `held` is an observed worst-rep resident, so a run
whose readers never fill their pools produces a negative remainder too, and the
arithmetic alone cannot tell that from a rule that over-charges. Both are
findings the sweep should stop for, so the discriminator is not worth building
ahead of a fault that has never fired; the sitting that fires one is the
evidence for building it.

So `19.11`'s acceptance is amended a fourth time, and the amendment **names the
bands** rather than a threshold: the `bound` band alone is released, and a band
the code defines with no stance recorded bars by default. The clause and
`ChargeFault.bars_acceptance` drifting apart with nothing reading both is the
same shape as the stale `reader_bytes` mirror `M88` found and the collapsed
verdict `M91` found, so `--check` asserts the enumeration covers every band the
code defines. `M92` is the work, and it blocks `P19`.

**The withheld re-derivation stands, on a stronger reason than the one in the
code.** `M91` withholds the bound's re-derivation from any sitting carrying a
barring cell, which the round that wrote it justified as *that cell's remainder
is a reading of an arrangement the rule did not hold for*. True, but it invites
a per-family refinement — one rule-faulting cell at 128 MiB withholds a
re-derivation the 24 MiB family's cells would have supported. The reason that
settles it is that a sitting carrying a barring cell does not publish at all, so
withholding a number from it strands nothing: the re-derivation is a property of
a publishable sitting. Filed beside the mechanism in
[`measure.py`](../../../scripts/measure.py).

**What the harness does, since `M92` landed.** `BAND_STANCE` is the acceptance
clause written out: a band maps to `None` where `19.11` releases it and to the
sentence saying *why* it bars otherwise, `band_bars` reads it with barring as
the default for a band nobody listed, and `ChargeFault.bars_acceptance` is that
answer. The verdict's barring stanza names the bands it is reporting and prints
each one's clause, where it had asserted the `rule` band's reason over every
cell in it — which is the sentence that went wrong the moment the over-bill
band joined that half — and its opening reads *The model is refuted* rather than
*The rule is*, the rule being one of the three lines rather than all of them.
`charge_band_problems` is a `--check` refusal beside `reserve_floor_problems`:
every `BAND_*` string constant the module defines must carry a stance, so a
fourth fault line is reported until somebody records one. The criterion prose
above the table states the released band by name for the same reason.

## `19.25`'s gate is right and its reason was false; the leader over-reads small blocks

The open "Decisions worth another look" entry asked whether `scan arrangement`
should report the third way a `parse` is left serial — stated there as "the
region is smaller than one of the source's partitions", and "how a dump of
small tables reads at any `--jobs`". The call is affirmed and the reason is
refuted.

**The third refusal is an end-of-file condition.** `scan_region`'s floor
compares `size - data_offset` against one `partition_bytes`, and `size` is the
**file's** size: the region's extent is not known there, because finding it is
the work, so what remains of the file is the only bound available. It fires on
a tail shorter than one reader's charge and on nothing else. A dump of small
tables clears it at every block but the last, so the cost the entry, the
`Shortfall` doc comment, the notes doc, `architecture.md` and the manual all
stated — a silent serial scan — does not exist. Being serial on such a tail is
the right arrangement anyway, and a line saying so would fire on the last block
of every file. The gate stands; the five sites are corrected.

**What a dump of small tables actually does is over-read, and that is `KD22`.**
The leader cuts `workers × partition_bytes` from the block's data start,
drains every piece, and `merge` discards everything past the first terminator,
after which `map_forward` re-reads those bytes serially. Each added reader
widens the stretch that is thrown away, so the waste grows with `--jobs`.
Measured on a 57.6 MiB synthetic dump of 2,000 `COPY` blocks of ~29 KB — a
probe, not a figure:

| `--jobs` | wall | CPU (u+s) | bytes read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.04 s | 0.03 s | 57.6 MiB |
| 4 | 0.76 s | 1.76 s | 8,594 MiB (149×) |
| 8 | 1.37 s | 3.89 s | 15,462 MiB (268×) |

`--jobs 8` is 34× the wall time and 130× the CPU of a serial scan of the same
file. It is reached with no flag typed on a compressed dump, since
`XzSource::default_workers` is one worker per core and the boundaried cut takes
`workers` whole blocks — one block decode per reader per small `COPY` block.
Every fixture is immune because each is smaller than one partition end to end,
which is exactly the size at which the floor swallows the whole file; that is
why the 4000-block fixture reads as serial and why `architecture.md`'s
speculative-splitting rejection concluded the shape costs nothing.

**`scan arrangement` is left as `19.25` shipped it.** Its contract is a
correction of `scan started`'s `jobs=`, and here the announced count genuinely
ran — silence is accurate about the count and says nothing about whether the
count was wise. Widening it to carry an over-read ratio would cost an
accumulator across regions whose only consumer is a diagnostic, and would not
help the person it is aimed at, who is helped by the leader not doing this. The
limit is recorded beside the mechanism instead.

`KD22` is `(c) unowned`: no phase in the index takes up leader scheduling, and
the fix is neither the cut width (`19.20` measured and settled it) nor the
floor (which is a memory charge) but the leader learning a region's extent
before it commits a window — a design question nobody has grilled.

## The charge over-bills the pool floor at every count

`19.11`'s closing sweep is gated on a `--figure reserve --alone` sitting, because
a sitting that publishes `reserve` refuses while any leg is killed and `M92`
releases the `bound` band alone — both of which an hour-long `--all` sweep would
otherwise discover after the machine was spent. That gate ran on 2026-09-12
against `880d09e` (`runs/measure-20260912T170937/tables.md`, NOT PUBLISHABLE, as
every `--alone` sitting's tables are) and **refused the sweep**.

**Half the gate passed outright: no leg was killed.** The whole flagless axis
survived its own allocation, including `128 MiB blocks, flagless in 1g`, which is
where `19.18`'s sitting died on 2026-09-11 at `maxrss_kib=1049016` inside a 1 GiB
limit. The instrument legs reconciled with the black-box ones to within 2 MiB at
every allocation, so the two apparatus halves agree.

**What refused it is the charge, at one reader, on both block sizes:**

| Leg | Readers | Billed | of which floor | Worst rep held | Unnamed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 MiB blocks, `-m 544m` | 1r | 130.0 MiB | 72.0 MiB | 111.1 MiB | **-18.9 MiB** |
| 128 MiB blocks, `-m 1088m` | 1r | 650.0 MiB | 384.0 MiB | 526.9 MiB | **-123.2 MiB** |

Both are over-bills — bytes the rule charged that nothing holds — so the rule
admits fewer readers than the allocation affords, and `19.11` releases neither
that band nor anything above the bound.

**The error is exactly one block unit, and the source names it.** The two
over-bills differ by 104.3 MiB for a 104 MiB difference in unit, so it scales
with the unit rather than being a margin; and at both cells the arrangement holds
four units plus the base the `-m 512m` streaming legs read directly — 4 × 24 +
15.1 = 111.1 measured, 4 × 128 + 14.9 = 526.9 — the same ≈5 MiB by which the
charge under-states that base, agreeing between the two families to 0.3 MiB. The
instrument legs confirm it from the other side: Rust's live high-water at `544m`
is **98.1 MiB**, four 24 MiB units with `liblzma`'s ~9.5 MiB invisible to a
`GlobalAlloc` counter by construction.

**What the pool holds is `slots − 1 + workers`, where `WorkerMemory` states
`slots + workers`.** `BlockCache::slot` evicts the retention list to
`pool.slots() − 1` *before* it obtains a buffer, and `BlockCache::retain` then
pushes that same buffer's block — so a reader's in-flight block **becomes** one
of the retained ones rather than sitting beside them. The `+ workers` term is
real all the same: the block pool is left at `WaitPolicy::NeverWait` on purpose,
a retained block being normally the block some reader holds a view into, so a
drain frees no slot for a caller waiting on one and `obtain` allocates rather
than blocking — guarded by `a_permitted_wait_never_reaches_the_block_pool`,
which fails rather than hanging if anything starts charging block slots.

**So the excess is uniform in the count, not a property of the low-count
regime.** The charge is `(workers + max(workers, POOL_DEPTH)) × unit` against a
true `… − 1`, at every count. It is visible only at one reader because above
that glibc arena retention is larger than a unit: +127.2, +129.4, +76.3 and
+114.5 MiB across this sitting's 24 MiB legs at 9 to 23 readers. The five cells
of `19.16`'s readings that "confirm" the floor to 1.4 MiB are all at two readers
and more, so they do not confirm the premise — they cannot resolve a term this
size.

**One unit off every cell's bill lands the whole criterion met, which is
arithmetic over readings already in hand rather than a re-take.** The two
faulting cells become +5.1 and +4.9 MiB; the six that read met rise by one unit
to 151.2 / 153.4 / 100.3 / 138.5 MiB at 24 MiB blocks and 4.8 / 141.9 / 133.1 at
128. The worst is 153.4 against a 256 MiB `MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND`, so neither
that constant nor `MEMORY_RESERVE` moves — the bound was never tight.

**`M93` landed the repair, and it was not a change to the floor.** What was
wrong is that the per-reader term billed **two** units for a reader whose
decoding buffer and retained block are the same buffer. The block term now
states what the mechanism holds — one unit a reader, and
`(POOL_DEPTH.max(workers) − 1) × unit` shared for the retention list — which is
the form that makes every consumer read a true sentence, `bytes_per_worker()`
included. `WorkerMemory`'s second term is renamed with it: `pooling(unit,
depth)` and `pool_bytes(workers)` where `flooring`/`floor_bytes` stood, because
a term that grows with the count past `POOL_DEPTH` is not a floor. Keeping two
units and carrying an explicit negative unit was the alternative: it gives the
same `at(n)` and fixes the gate, which is asked at `at(1)`, while leaving
`bytes_per_worker()` overstating for its own consumers —
`leader::scan_region`'s region floor and `stream::plan_partitions`' choice of
the costliest advice.

**Two consequences the harness carries.** `reserve_axis_problems` (formerly
`reserve_floor_problems`) drops its floor-window half: the pool term is now
billed at **every** cell, so there is no window to register a limit inside, and
what is left is the fit-ability check `M90` folded in. `M89`'s two limits stay
on the axis — they are additive legs and the reader counts they add (two at
24 MiB blocks, one at 128) are counts no other limit resolves. And `512m` at
24 MiB blocks now reads the **block path**, where the over-bill put it on the
streaming fallback: 128 MiB granted against a line that moved 130.0 → 106.03.

*Rejected:* that the repair is blocked because `slots` is a function of the
budget being solved. `slots` is `(budget / unit).clamp(1, POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs))`,
so the budget enters only as the branch that **lowers** it, and the charge has
been a bound rather than an exact count since `19.7` — charging the depth
argument is budget-independent, and wherever the budget's branch wins the pool
holds less than charged. There is no fixed point to restate.

*Rejected:* weakening the gate to publish the sweep. `M92` set that acceptance
four amendments deep, and an over-bill is a reader the allocation would have
afforded — the defect the phase exists to remove.

**It is out-of-band rather than a slice**, by the ledger's admission rule: it
reverses no decision a spec records. `19.22`'s `WorkerMemory` shape, `19.24`'s
criterion and `19.26`'s two constants all stand. What it moved is consequence
rather than decision — `BlockCache::affordable`'s decline line (130.0 MiB → 106
at 24 MiB blocks, 650.0 → 522 at 128), `19.26`'s flagless axis, which resolves
1/2/10/11/17/24 readers on the registered limits at 24 MiB blocks where it read
9/10/16/23 on four of them, and `leader::scan_region`'s region floor, which is
`partition_bytes` and so halves on a compressed source. The **cut** width is not
among them: `Partitioning::partition_bytes` is read only on the
`PartitionBoundaries::Anywhere` arm, while a compressed source cuts on `At` at
`BOUNDARIED_PARTITION_UNITS` boundaries, so `19.20`'s measured "one unit stays"
is untouched.

**No figure changed staleness class.** Every published figure that names
`pgdump_query/src/io.rs` was already red on it before this change, so `M93`
spends no acknowledgement and owes none (`uv run measure.py --stale`).

**The chunk pool has the same defect one pool over, and it is `M94`.**
`XzSource::apportion` gives that pool `POOL_DEPTH` slots while
`charged_chunk_bytes` bills one chunk a reader, so at one reader it holds 4 MiB
against 1 billed — most of the ≈5 MiB base discrepancy measured above. It is an
**under**-bill, so it inflates the unnamed remainder rather than declining a
path and cannot bar the gate, which is why it is queued separately rather than
folded into `M93`. It scales with what a caller announces, `POOL_DEPTH ×
announced_chunk` — 64 MiB held against 16 billed at a 16 MiB `hint_read_size` —
and pricing it at an announced chunk is what decides whether it is a fix or a
`KD<k>`.

**Nothing else of `19.11` landed.** The sweep was not launched; `reserve`'s
`Shared` edge onto `peak-rss`, the `Session.borrow` change that lets an RSS
reading cross a share, `rss-attribution`'s publication and `M74`'s closure, and
both `parallel-*` re-takes all remain ahead of it, now behind `M93`.

## The fit is a straight line across a kink the charge now states

`M93` restated the block charge as what the pool holds:
`(POOL_DEPTH.max(workers) − 1) × unit` retained, plus one unit a reader in
flight. Written out, held memory is `w + 3` units below four readers and
`2w − 1` at or above — **piecewise linear, with a kink at `POOL_DEPTH`**. The
bend is in the measured resident and not only in the charge: `BufferPool::slots`
clamps the block pool at `POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)`, so a one-reader arrangement
really does retain three blocks it will not use again. `io.rs` says so in those
words ("Two regimes, because the cost is piecewise linear"); `M93` is what made
the harness state it too.

**The harness fits one straight line across it.** `_least_squares` publishes
`resident = fixed + readers × per_reader` over every block-path leg of a family,
and both registered families straddle the kink:

| family | afforded counts | below `POOL_DEPTH` | at or above |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 MiB blocks | 1, 2, 11, 12, 20, 29 | 1, 2 | 11, 12, 20, 29 |
| 128 MiB blocks | 1, 2, 4, 6 (`512m`/`544m` decline) | 1, 2 | 4, 6 |

Least squares over the charge's own held-unit values, in units of one block:
the 24 MiB family gives slope 1.903 and intercept **+1.04 units (+25 MiB)**
where the upper branch's own intercept is −1 unit, so ≈49 MiB of intercept bias
and a worst residual of ≈+25 MiB at one reader; the 128 MiB family gives slope
**1.373** against a true 2.0 — a 31% understatement — and intercept **+2.29
units (+293 MiB)** against a true −128, so ≈**421 MiB** of bias. That is the
model's shape, before any measurement noise, and the bound it sits beside is
256 MiB with 32–43 MiB of apparatus scatter.

**It does not reach the gate.** `charge_model_problem` is per cell — billed
against held — and never reads a fitted line, so `19.11`'s acceptance is
unaffected. What it reached is the pair of numbers the table publishes and
`19.18`'s `98% of reader_bytes` comparison, which reads the slope. No figure
publishes them yet, which is why this was a blocker on the sitting rather than a
correction to a published table.

**`M95` landed the repair, and it is `19.24`'s own principle one table over.**
That slice decided the harness checks a stated model rather than searching for a
constant; the pool term is not an unknown to be recovered from data but
arithmetic over quantities all known before the sitting, and already held to the
library's constants by `test_the_mirrored_pool_depth_and_constants_are_the_librarys_own`.
So `_depooled` subtracts it and the fit is over the remainder, which is the
genuinely unknown fixed and per-reader cost and has no kink in it. Fitting per
regime was the alternative: honest, but it leaves the below-depth regime with
two counts on both families and therefore a secant and no intercept, and it
makes the two families' slopes incomparable. Fitting only the at-or-above-depth
legs was refused outright — the one- and two-reader legs are the ones closest to
their own ceiling, which is what the constant was chosen against.

**The subtraction sits at `_fit_or_secant`, not in a renderer**, on
`RESERVE_FIT_MIN_COUNTS`' own argument: every fitted line in the harness crosses
that boundary, and a de-pooling any of the three call sites could forget is a
de-pooling one of them will. A test parses the module back out and holds
`_least_squares` to exactly one call site, inside `_fit_or_secant`, so a fourth
line cannot be fitted around it. `RESERVE_FIT_MIN_COUNTS` is unchanged at three:
the remainder still has two terms.

**Two things the subtraction made load-bearing, both corrected with it.** A leg
on the streaming fallback holds no block pool, so subtracting a retention list
from one would invent a negative term; the instrument account now drops a
declined leg from its line by name, as the flagless family always has — no
registered limit declines at that block size, so it is a guard rather than a
filter. And the `98% of reader_bytes` comparison is commensurable again:
`reader_bytes` is the per-worker term *without* the list, so a slope still
carrying the list was being compared against a bill that does not — a whole unit
a reader of disagreement above `POOL_DEPTH`, which is what `M93` opened when it
took the second unit out of `reader_bytes`.

**One sentence of the model table's prose was still the pre-`M93` spelling** —
"below four readers the pool holds `(POOL_DEPTH − jobs) × unit`" — describing a
term that decayed to nothing at four readers. It reads
`(POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs) − 1) × unit`, billed at every count, which is what the
column beside it has printed since `M93`.

## The window check has no successor, and a second property was riding on it

`M89`'s floor-window refusal failed the axis unless some registered limit
granted a budget in `[charge_bytes(unit, 1), charge_bytes(unit, POOL_DEPTH))`,
the only band in which the old decaying term was billed at all. `M93` made the
term unconditional, so the window is gone and the check cannot fail; deleting it
was right, because a refusal that can never fire is a signal that is always
green.

The reason recorded for the deletion was incomplete, though. The window was
doing two jobs, and only the first became unconditional: it also forced a cell
onto the **below-depth side of the kink**, which is coverage the fit needs and
nothing else stated. Under `M95` the fit subtracts the kink rather than fitting
across it, so that coverage stops being a requirement and no successor check is
owed — but it is why `544m` and `1088m` stay, and that is now written where the
limits are registered rather than left to be re-derived.

**One detail in the closed entry was wrong**: it justified those two limits as
adding "two at 24 MiB blocks, one at 128". Under the repaired charge `1088m`
resolves **two** readers at 128 MiB blocks and `544m` declines the block path
there entirely. The conclusion survives — each adds a count no other limit
resolves — but not for the reason given.

## Five model defects, none of which needed a run

`M87`, `M88`, `M93`, `M94` and `M95` are all the same kind of thing — the
charge model, or the harness's mirror of it, disagreeing with what the process
holds — and **not one of them was found by a measurement**. `M93` was arithmetic
over a sitting's own table, `M94` and `M95` were read straight off the source,
`M88` was a stale mirror, `M87` a bypassed call. The stream is gated on somebody
reading the account carefully, not on taking another reading.

That matters because of what it cost. `19.11`'s acceptance criterion was
amended four times — `M89` registered limits and a floor window, `M90` moved the
fit guard to the model, `M91` split the verdict into bands, `M92` said which
band releases — and then `M93` falsified a premise underneath them: `M89`'s
floor window was deleted the same day it was added, and `M91`'s `bars_acceptance`
shape was changed one commit after it landed. None of those amendments was
wrong. They were written against a model that kept moving, which is
`.claude/skills/evidence/SKILL.md`'s rule 1 — *account before you fit* — failing
one level up, at the gate rather than at the fit.

**So the order is inverted deliberately from here.** `M96` enumerates the whole
account in one pass — every pool and buffer, billed against held — before `M94`
and `M95` are fixed, and the gate is not amended again until that list is
complete. Fixing the two already-diagnosed items first and then looking for more
is the same one-per-round pattern continuing; enumerating first is what stops
it. The remaining work on `19.11` is unchanged, and no constant has moved.

## Billed against held, in one pass

`M96` is landed and the account is
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Billed against held: one row
per buffer the process keeps". It enumerates every pool, buffer and retained
structure a scan holds against what `WorkerMemory` bills for it, on all three
read paths — a block-decoding `XzSource`, its streaming fallback, and
`LocalFileSource` — plus the terms above the source that `MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND`
is the bound over. No reading was taken for it; every number in it is a constant
in `io.rs` or a `size_of` over a vendored struct.

**Three discrepancies came out of the pass, and none of them blocks `19.11`.**

`M97` is the largest. `LocalFileSource::partitions` still bills
`PLAIN_PARTITION_CHUNKS × chunk` — 8 MiB a reader — because that number is the
plain path's *cut* size and has been its memory charge since before there were
two. `19.20` made the read shape the source's own statement and a plain source
states `PartitionRead::Chunked`, so a worker reads one announced chunk at a
time, and the interior split's `WaitPolicy::MayWait` caps what is outstanding at
`BufferPool::slots` — the path holds `POOL_DEPTH` chunks flat in the reader
count against a bill that grows by 8 MiB a reader. It is the same value-with-two-consumers
defect `19.19` repaired one source over, and `19.20`'s own probe already
measured it: plain at `--jobs 24 --parallel-memory 256m` held **9.4 MiB** where
the charge bills 192. Over-billing declines readers rather than overrunning an
allocation, which is why nothing failed; `--jobs 24 --parallel-memory 64m`
delivers eight readers for an arrangement that holds under 8 MiB.

`M98` is the one that is an under-bill. `XzSource::partition_advice` states
`RetainedUnit::Partition`, which drops `max_source_span` from the charge on the
ground that a batch pins the block already charged for — sound while the span
fits one block, and the default span is 64 MiB against koji's 24 MiB blocks, so
a sub-stream's `RetainedChunks` pins up to four blocks and one is billed. Query
path only: `pgdq parse` builds no batches, so no `reserve` leg can reach it, and
that is why the sitting which priced the block path did not see it.

`M99` is small and is a row because of its shape rather than its size: the seek
table is held twice, billed nowhere, and grows with the file's stream count —
6.65 MiB on koji's 31,150-stream download — inside a bound that is a constant
read off a grid of 3 GiB fixtures.

**What the pass corrected rather than queued** is prose that stated the held
side wrongly. `architecture.md` still said a block-decoding reader holds the
block unit *twice* and cost 32 MiB against koji's blocks, both of which `M93`
and `19.14` had already moved; three `io.rs` doc comments still described the
plain path's partition-length body read, which `19.20` removed; and
`BufferPool::held_bytes` claimed to be a true bound without saying that a
checked-out buffer and a block evicted while a view is live are on neither of
the two lists it counts.

**The enumeration's own claim is that it is complete, and nothing checks that.**
It was built by walking every `vec!`, `obtain`, `Arc::new` and retained `Vec` on
the three read paths rather than from a list somebody kept, so the honest
statement is that it is a careful reading and not a mechanical one. What would
make it mechanical is the counting `#[global_allocator]` the `introspect`
feature already carries, reporting live bytes per site rather than in total —
which is a slice, not a row.

## A query partition is not one unit wide

`M96`'s pass found that a `pgdq query` sub-stream pins more decoded blocks than
the charge bills, and filed it as `M98`. The finding is right and the filing was
not.

**Where the defect actually sits.** `RetainedUnit::Partition` drops the
`max_source_span` term for a block-decoding source, and the reason it records is
that a batch is confined to a partition, which `partition_bytes` has already
charged for. That reason is true wherever a partition is one unit wide, and
`leader::run_region` guarantees it — its window comes from
`Partitioning::window_end`, which is held to `BOUNDARIED_PARTITION_UNITS` by
`a_block_decoding_partition_spans_at_most_the_cut_width`. **The query path never
crosses that window.** `plan_partitions` calls `stream::cut` over the whole
`CopyBlock` with `want = min(workers, max_partitions)`, and `max_partitions` is
every block boundary in the region, so a piece spans as many units as the region
holds over the worker count. So the two paths disagree about what a partition
is, and the charge was written against one of them.

The magnitude the row gave stands — up to four of koji's 24 MiB blocks pinned
against one billed, `QueryOptions::max_source_span` being 64 MiB by default —
and so does its judgement that `19.11` is untouched, now for a stronger reason
than it gave: the cold path genuinely does honour the one-unit cut, and the
`reserve` figure's legs are `pgdq parse`.

**Three consequences, and the filing is the first.** Every repair reverses
`RetainedUnit::Partition`'s rationale, so by the ledger's admission rule this is
not out-of-band: `M98`'s row is withdrawn, its number spent, on the `M82`
precedent. Second, `BlockCache::affordable`'s doc asserted the stated budget is
"true rather than nearly true" without qualification; that is false on the query
path and is corrected in place rather than deferred, the manual rule for a
falsified claim applying to a shipped doc comment as much as to prose. Third,
the defect outlives both corrections and has no owning phase, so it is `KD23`,
`(c) unowned`, filed beside the mechanism.

**The repair is not chosen here.** Charging the span on the block path undoes
the reason the variant exists for this source and widens the decline on every
compressed query, which `19.22` has already shown is the expensive direction;
giving `plan_partitions` the leader's window loop leaves the charge alone and
makes the rationale true as written. The second looks better and neither is
settled — that is the slice's grilling, not this closure's.

## The chunk pool's floor is priced, and 4 MiB does not buy a third term

`M94` was admitted as a pricing, not a repair: the chunk pool's free list is
billed nowhere, and what the row asked for is the number at a caller-announced
chunk before anyone decides between a fix and a register entry. The number says
entry, and the defect is `KD24`.

**What is unbilled is a different population from what is billed.**
`BlockCache::reader_bytes` charges one chunk *a reader* for the buffer a
straddling read is assembled into, which `leader::scan_partition` keeps exactly
one of per worker. The free list holds what previous reads released, and
`XzSource::apportion` sizes it with `set_limits(budget, POOL_DEPTH)` — a depth
that never rises with `jobs`, unlike the block pool's `POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)`. So
the unbilled ceiling is `⌊budget/chunk⌋.clamp(1, POOL_DEPTH) × chunk`, flat in
the reader count and linear in the chunk.

**Priced at the two chunks that exist:**

| Arrangement | Chunk pool ceiling | Billed | Path |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped default, 64 MiB budget | 4 MiB | 1 MiB a reader | block or streaming |
| `--chunk-size 16m`, 64 MiB budget | 64 MiB | 16 MiB | streaming; `affordable` asks 121.5 MiB of a 64 MiB allowance |

Against the 106.0 MiB the block path bills one reader on koji's 24 MiB blocks
the default case is **3.8%**, and it is a sixty-fourth of
`MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND` — the constant that exists to bound exactly the bytes no
charge names. The 16 MiB case is the whole default budget, which reads worse
than it is: `BufferPool::slots` divides the same budget by the same chunk, so
`slots × chunk ≤ budget` holds by construction and the term can never exceed the
stated allowance. The one arrangement that can is a chunk larger than the whole
budget, where `slots` floors at one and the pool keeps a buffer bigger than the
allowance — the deliberate floor, not this entry.

**The fix is a third term in `WorkerMemory`, and that is what decides it.** The
type has a per-worker term that grows with the count and a pooling term that
grows with it past `POOL_DEPTH`; a pool whose depth never moves needs a
count-independent term, and neither existing one can stand in — `pooling` would
over-bill above four readers, `per_worker` at every count but one. Adding it
changes `at`, `affords`, `is_zero` and `plus_per_worker`, and every consumer
reads one of those: `Parallelism::fit`, `stream::worker_count`,
`stream::plan_partitions`, `leader::scan_region`, `BlockCache::affordable`, the
CLI's mirror and `measure.py`'s `charge_model`. `MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND` survives
either way — a larger bill only shrinks the remainder it bounds — but the
decline line and `19.26`'s flagless axis move again, for 4 MiB at the shipped
default, the third such move in a day and the second inside the run-up to a
gated sitting. That is a slice's worth of rework on a tested core path bought
with a term the bound already covers, so the term stays unbilled and named.

**The resident cost is already disclosed; the accounting is not.**
`--chunk-size`'s help text and `docs/manual/dump-inspection.md` both say the
pool keeps four buffers of the announced size or the budget's worth, whichever
is fewer, and work the 16 MiB case out in the same words. So no user-facing
claim is falsified and no one is surprised in resident bytes — what is wrong is
that the charge picking the worker count and the block path does not carry the
term, which is what keeps this an entry rather than a property.

**`19.11` is what the row was blocking, and the block is discharged rather than
removed.** An under-bill cannot decline a path or fault the gate; what it does
is inflate the unnamed remainder the sitting measures, and unlike `M97`, `KD23`
and `M99` this one *is* reachable by `reserve`'s legs — every one of them runs
at the default chunk, so 4 MiB of every cell's remainder is `KD24`. Saying so is
what the row owed: the sitting now knows what that 4 MiB is instead of carrying
it as scatter.

*Rejected:* folding it into the per-reader chunk term by raising
`charged_chunk_bytes` to the pool's ceiling. `BlockCache::affordable` compares
that same number against the budget to decide the block path, and the doc
comment there already records what raising it costs — the path declined on files
that fit, a reader lost at every allocation when the unannounced pool's
`POOL_MAX_BYTES` was charged instead. It also bills a flat term `w` times, which
is the error `M93` had just removed one pool over.

**No figure changed staleness class**, as with `M93`: every published figure
naming `pgdump_query/src/io.rs` was already red on it, and the only change to
that file here is a doc comment carrying the marker. No acknowledgement is spent
and none is owed (`uv run measure.py --stale`).

**The hole is the apparatus, and it is admitted as `M101`.** The harness already
refuses to time a `runs/pgdq-nocensus` whose stamp is not an ancestor of the

third passage is the sharpest: `ensure_allocator_binary` rebuilds every leg once
