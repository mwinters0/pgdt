# 2026-09-12

## The reserve entry closes on 384, and the grilling found the charge wrong below four readers

`19.16`'s pick stands: **`MEMORY_RESERVE` ships at 384 MiB**, and `19.13`
carries it. Reconstructed against the criterion rather than read off the
probe's verdict, the pick is forced on this grid — at `1g` the criterion caps
the count at 11 (816.9 MiB passes, 827.3 fails), which needs a reserve in
(322, 381]; at `1536m` it caps at 19 (1224.4 passes, 1279.5 fails), needing
(367, 425]. 384 is the only candidate on the grid inside both. 512 was the
alternative the entry raised and it is declined: the criterion was registered
before the sitting, and choosing differently after reading the grid is what
this phase has twice been burned by.

**What the reserve is covering is not what `19.18` said it was.** Over the
block-path regime the worst-resident slope is 57.28 MiB a reader against the
58.03 [`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs)'s `block_reader_bytes` bills —
**0.987**, so the per-reader charge is right to 1.3% and glibc's arena
retention is already inside it. What sits above the charge is flat: the excess
`worst − 58.03 × jobs` is 135.7 MiB at two readers and 145.4 at twenty-four,
wandering 83.6–214.6 with no trend across jobs 2…24. `19.18`'s reading that
retention "rises with the count the allowance affords" is true of `fordblks`
and does not reach the reserve.

### The charge under-bills the pool floor, and the record says it does not

[`roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md)'s Future item "A block cache whose
retention floor tracks the reader count" states that P19 "charges the floor
honestly". **It does not.** `BufferPool::slots` clamps the block pool at
`POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` ([`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs), the
`set_limits` call in `XzSource::hint_parallelism`'s block-cache sibling), so
the pool holds at least four units however few readers there are, while
`block_reader_bytes` bills `2 × unit` a reader — which assumes `slots == jobs`.
Below four readers we hold units nobody charged for.

The size of it is `(POOL_DEPTH − jobs) × unit`, and `19.16`'s own readings
confirm it to 1.4 MiB on the 128 MiB-block file:

| jobs | worst RSS | budget | excess | `(4−jobs) × unit` | residual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 802.1 | 532.1 | 270.0 | 256.0 | 14.0 |
| 3 | 939.9 | 798.1 | 141.8 | 128.0 | 13.8 |
| 4 | 1077.7 | 1064.1 | 13.6 | 0.0 | 13.6 |
| 5 | 1342.6 | 1330.2 | 12.4 | 0.0 | 12.4 |
| 6 | 1607.5 | 1596.2 | 11.3 | 0.0 | 11.3 |

Five cells, residual flat at 11–14 MiB. That is an account, not a fit, and it
is derivable from `readings.json` with no new sitting.

**It is unbounded in the block size** — 96 MiB on koji's 24 MiB blocks, 384 MiB
at 128 MiB, 2 GiB on a 512 MiB-block file — which is why no constant can absorb
it and why it is a charge defect rather than a reserve one. It is also *not*
what 384 pays for: on the 24 MiB file the term is 48/24/0 MiB and the mid-range
failures the reserve was raised for are all at eleven readers and above, where
it is zero.

### The 24-worker cap is load-bearing and nothing said so

`2g` clears the criterion only because `available_parallelism` clamps 28
readers to this machine's 24. On a 64-core host the same limit resolves 28,
costing 1637 MiB of a 1664 MiB budget and predicting ~1774 MiB resident against
a 1638 MiB ceiling — **13.3%, a failure**, on a machine no leg ran. 384 is
validated to 2 GiB *on 24 cores*, and the phase's own grid cannot see the
difference.

### The arena question, answered against the record

Whether the reserve should be configured from the arena count was raised and is
declined, on grounds already written: the spec's "The arena cap is a deployment
setting, not a mechanism this binary ships" refuses `mallopt(M_ARENA_MAX, …)`
because a cap keyed to the resolved worker count is unreachable — glibc seeds an
arena per thread at that thread's first allocation, and the source-dependent
count arrives after source recognition, which is I/O. Nothing in the new
readings reopens it.

Introspecting the *maximum* is separately the wrong number. Observed arenas are
`readers + 2` (6/15/24/26 at 4/13/22/24 readers) against a glibc ceiling of
`8 × ncores` = 192 here, so our own thread count binds and the maximum never
does; there is no getter for `M_ARENA_MAX` in any case. The lever that exists
is setting it, which the spec section above already refuses.

## A `parse` reports what was asked for and never what ran

Grilling the "Decisions worth another look" entry `19.13` left open — a
declined block path is reported on `query` and not on `parse` — found the
entry's framing too narrow in two places and its coverage claim true in only
one arrangement. The entry is closed; the work it implies is `M87` and `19.25`.

**The decline costs `parse` throughput, not only `query`.** The entry leaned on
`PlanNoteKind::CompressedBlockPathDeclined`'s own doc, which says the mapping
pass "only reads forward, costs the same either way". That holds for *backward*
reads and is not the whole cost: `leader::scan_region` returns
`RegionScan::Declined` when `advice.max_partitions() == Some(1)`, which is
exactly the shape a declined source advertises and is how
`stream::compressed_block_path_declined` detects it. So a declined `.xz`
`parse` is serial whatever `--jobs` says.

**The mode report covered the decline in one arrangement only.**
`ParallelArgs::resolve_in`'s stated-budget arm returned
`Parallelism::workers(jobs, stated)` without consulting
`Parallelism::discover_in`, so a recommended count was never lowered when
`--parallel-memory` was stated. `pgdq parse --parallel-memory 33554432` against
a large-block `.xz` on a 24-core host reported `24 (recommended by the
source)`, ran one serial reader with the block path declined, and said nothing
else — a count that would not be delivered, printed as a recommendation. That
also falsified `Resolved::jobs_display`'s doc comment, which claimed a
recommendation "is lowered to what the allowance affords and printed lowered".
`M87` closes it, below.

**So the gap is the delivered count, not the decline.** On `parse` nothing ever
reports that the delivered worker count differs from the announced one, and the
only mechanism that corrects the announcement is a plan note `parse` cannot
see; `query` is covered on both counts. Printing a decline beside a `jobs=24`
line that is itself wrong is a worse end state than today's silence, and the
general statement is the one a future source inherits — "the block path" is one
container format's word for it.

**The report is the library's, and provenance stays the CLI's.** The entry
offered two routes and priced them; a third exists that neither considered.
`stream::map_forward` already emits `scan started` on both commands
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Status
output"), so the fact can be stated where it is known — `leader::scan_region`
is the only party that sees the advice — without a new public channel and
without the CLI re-deriving the source's arithmetic, which
`compressed_block_path_declined` refuses by name. The CLI keeps
`Resolved::plan_note_origin`'s clause: the library says what bound, the CLI
says where the number came from.

**A stated budget does not exempt a recommended count from the allowance.** The
standing rule is "never allocate workers there is no memory for"
([`../../design/roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md), "A default runs as fast
as the allocation permits"), and `resolve_in` scopes it to "the absence of a
flag and never its presence" — but the flag whose presence is respected there
is `--jobs`, and in this arrangement `--jobs` is absent. Nothing states that
stating a budget should make a recommended count stop answering to it, so the
asymmetry is an oversight rather than a decision. `M87` removes it.

## `M87`: a recommended count answers to a stated budget too

`ParallelArgs::resolve_in` now lowers a **recommended** worker count on both
arms. Where `--parallel-memory` is typed and `--jobs` is not, the source's
recommendation is cut to what those bytes afford, through
`Parallelism::recommended_within` — `discover_for`'s own `fit`, over a budget
that arrived typed rather than discovered. So `pgdq parse --parallel-memory
67108864` against a 24 MiB-block `.xz` on a 24-core host reports `jobs=1`
instead of the `24 (recommended by the source)` it announced beside one serial
reader.

**Only the count moves.** Discovery hands back the bytes the lowered count
spends, because it chose them and must not name a budget the allowance never
granted; a stated budget is taken whole, a stated flag winning outright. The
asymmetry with `--jobs` is unchanged: a count somebody typed is printed as
typed, and what it delivers stays `stream::worker_count`'s to decide.

**The lowering clause names which budget did it.** `by the allocation` where
the number came off the environment, `by the stated budget` where it was typed
— the recourse is a different number in each case, and pointing at a cgroup
nobody set would be the new arm's own way of misleading. `Resolved` already
carries `budget_stated`, so the discriminator is exact: a stated budget wins
over a discovered limit, so it is what lowered the count whenever it is
present.

It is an out-of-band row rather than a slice because no spec'd decision moves:
the standing rule scopes the lowering to the absence of `--jobs`, which is
absent on this arm, so what landed is the rule already written applied where it
had not been reaching.

`measurements.md` able to carry only tables that agree with the library, which
inverts what the document is for. The check therefore reports and does nothing

spec row is amended to a two-sided acceptance, the shape `19.19` already used:
the sweep publishes, and the verdict reads that the model holds at every

## The library default declines the block path, and the charge is why

`19.22` made `BlockCache::affordable` bill one reader's pool floor, so the line
a budget must clear went from `2 × unit + chunk + decode` to `5 × unit + chunk +
decode` — 130 MiB at koji's 24 MiB blocks against 58, and 650 at 128 MiB blocks
against 266. Under `DEFAULT_MEMORY_BUDGET`'s 64 MiB that declines every ordinary
`.xz` dump, where a 24 MiB-block file previously took the block path. The call
to leave the constant where it is was reviewed and stands; the reasoning is
beside the gate ([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md),
"Execution model and API surface").

**The constant was never the thing that changed.** Two units alone is 48 MiB and
the chunk and decoder take ~10.5 more, so a 64 MiB grant has 4.6 MiB of headroom
and no pooling rule fits inside it — `slots = jobs + 1`, the least that does not
drain the retention list before every decode, still wants 82.5 MiB at one reader.
The path was reachable only while the charge billed a floor of zero. So the
default did not regress; the charge stopped concealing what the default had
always granted.

**The entry's own argument for leaving it was backwards and is not what was
filed.** It held that moving the constant would make one number answer two
unrelated questions. `affordable` compares against the budget in force and that
budget's fallback *is* this constant, so it already decides the compressed gate
— `MEMORY_RESERVE`'s doc comment says the same thing from the other end, that
the reserve and the decline are one knob. What keeps the number honest is that
it stays derived from the serial path, which is the smallest grant costing the
plain path nothing; sizing it to clear a block-path gate is what would pick it
for the second question.

**"The CLI is unaffected" was wrong in a narrow band.** The CLI's budget is
`limit − MEMORY_RESERVE`, so the block path needs an allocation of about
515 MiB. At 512 MiB — the size `19.16` took its 400 runs in — `Parallelism::fit`
now returns one worker at a 128 MiB budget and the gate then refuses at 130.5,
where before `19.22` the same cgroup ran two block readers at 118.8. This is the
right answer rather than a defect: the sweep at that limit already says the
block path buys nothing there, two readers being 6% slower than declining. Its
consequence is for the apparatus, not the mechanism, and is `M88`.

## `M88`: the path a leg ran is read off the budget it reported

Every flagless `reserve` cell names `*block path*` or `*streaming*`, and the
harness asks that question in one place: `block_path_afforded` mirrors
`BlockCache::affordable` as `charge_bytes(unit, 1)` — the per-reader term plus
that one reader's pool floor, which `19.22` put inside it.

Three of the renderer's four sites compared the reported budget against
`reader_bytes` alone; the fourth, `19.24`'s model check, did not. The line
`19.22` moved is 58.03 MiB → **130.0** at 24 MiB blocks and 266 → **650** at
128, so a stale mirror mislabels whole cells rather than getting an edge case
wrong:

- `-m 512m` grants `512 − 384 = 128 MiB`, below 130.0, so **both** inputs read
  the streaming fallback on the axis's bottom row. The 24 MiB cell there is one
  of the two the old mirror called block path.
- `-m 1g` grants 640 MiB, below the 128 MiB leg's 650 and above the 266 the old
  mirror compared against, so that is the other one. Nothing else moved: at
  512 MiB the 128 MiB leg was already declined, and `1536m`/`2g` clear both
  lines. The 128 MiB fit is therefore over two legs where it was over three.
- The path step's two budgets straddled `reader_bytes`, which both legs now
  clear and neither reaches, so the pair would have run the streaming decoder
  twice and published its difference as the cost of a path neither took. They
  are `charge_bytes(unit, 1)` and one byte below it.

Two apparatus claims said 512 MiB is the smallest allocation reaching the block
path. Both are corrected where they stand, in `RESERVE_LIMITS` and
`RESERVE_MECHANISM_LIMIT`: that row is kept as the bottom of the axis because
the rule's own worst headroom is there and a fallback leg is still a leg the
allocation must hold, and the path step beside it is the one pair that states a
budget, precisely so the block path can be reached inside that container.

**The row's own mechanism is wrong, and the correction is narrower rather than
wider.** It reads that the leg already knows, `stream::compressed_block_path_declined`
being a `PlanNote` — but `parse` announces no plan notes at all
([`pgdump_query-cli/src/main.rs`](../../../pgdump_query-cli/src/main.rs),
`announce_plan_notes`, reached only from the `query` arm), and
`scripts/measure.py`'s own `RESERVE_FLAGLESS_INPUTS` comment already said so
before the row was written. What the run *does* report is the budget it
resolved, on its own `scan started` line, and the path follows from it by the
library's arithmetic — so this stays bookkeeping over a reading rather than
becoming the library change `19.25` owns.

## `POOL_DEPTH` is the plain path's depth, and the block pool inherits it

Filed against `KD21` and the `roadmap.md` Future item on the retention floor,
which share the question and neither of which was carrying this. `POOL_DEPTH` is
the free-list depth a *chunk* pool needs so that a flushed batch's release does
not find an empty list, sized against a chunk of at most 8 MiB. The block pool
takes it as a floor while its unit is whatever block the file was written with —
up to 512 MiB — so the constant governing that floor was never chosen against
that pool's unit. `XzSource::apportion` defends only the minimum, that a block
pool of one drains before every decode, and that argues for two rather than
four. Neither has been measured against what block retention buys a seeking
query, which is the reading both the entry and the Future item wait on.

## The gate never reaches the pool floor, and the 128 MiB family was never a fit

`M88` corrected the harness's mirror of `BlockCache::affordable` and the
consequence was that two of the eight flagless cells changed mechanism without
moving. Grilling that consequence found a larger one underneath it.

**The arithmetic, recomputed from the harness's own mirrors.** `POOL_DEPTH` is
4 and `MEMORY_RESERVE` 384 MiB, so `charge_bytes(unit, 1)` — the line
`BlockCache::affordable` compares a budget against — is **130.03 MiB** at 24 MiB
blocks and **650.03** at 128, and the smallest container limit affording one
block-decoding reader is **514.03** and **1034.03 MiB**:

| `-m` | budget | 24 MiB blocks | 128 MiB blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| `512m` | 128 MiB | streaming | streaming |
| `1g` | 640 | block, 11r | streaming — 650.03 against 640 |
| `1536m` | 1152 | block, 19r | block, 4r |
| `2g` | 1664 | block, ≤24r on this box's clamp | block, 6r |

The 11 and 19 agree with what `19.16` read off the sitting, which is the
independent check on the mirror.

**No published cell bills a pool floor.** `pool_floor_bytes` is
`max(0, 4 − jobs) × unit`, so it is clamped off at four readers, and every
block-path leg above resolves 4, 6, 11, 19 or 24. The model check's "of which
pool floor" column therefore reads `—` in all five rows it prints. That is the
term `19.22` added, `19.24` gave a named column, and `M88` spent a round
correcting the mirror of, and `19.11`'s amended acceptance — the verdict holds
"at every evaluated cell" — is satisfiable without the criterion ever evaluating
it. The over-bill side is what a clamped term stresses, and it is the side a grid
search over reserve constants cannot report at all.

The windows where a flagless cell *would* bill the term are disjoint, so no
single limit covers both block sizes:

| readers | 24 MiB: limit ≥ | floor | 128 MiB: limit ≥ | floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 514.03 MiB | 72 MiB | 1034.03 MiB | 384 MiB |
| 2 | 548.07 | 48 | 1172.07 | 256 |
| 3 | 582.10 | 24 | 1310.10 | 128 |
| 4 | 616.13 | 0 | 1448.13 | 0 |

**Raising the bottom of the axis is the wrong remedy, and so is extending it
past `2g`.** Both were the shapes the closed entry offered. More allowance means
more readers means the term stays clamped at zero, so neither reaches it; and
`2g` is already where the 24 MiB count saturates on the source's recommendation
rather than on the allowance, so nothing above it is informative. What reaches it
is a limit *inserted* under the clamp — `544m` and `1088m`, `M89`, now
registered. Additive, so `512m` keeps the axis's worst headroom and no existing
reading moves; the axis is 12 flagless legs and 7 instrument legs where it was 8
and 5, which is 38 legs against 32.

**The 128 MiB family was never owed a fit.** The spec already says so — "the
128 MiB flagless family is the repair's acceptance gate, not a source of
numbers" — and its acceptance is that every leg either takes the block path and
survives its allocation or declines it, none killed. So the two-point line the
closed entry worried about is not a thin deliverable; it is a fit the renderer
publishes over a family whose spec says it produces none. The guard is the fix,
and it refuses a fit below **3** distinct reader counts where it refused below 2:
a two-term model has no residual at two points, and `±0 MiB` printed beside a
real residual cannot be told from one. `measure.RESERVE_FIT_MIN_COUNTS` is that
bound; `_least_squares` keeps its own floor of two, which is where the arithmetic
rather than the publication stops. `1088m`'s value is the floor cell and the
gate, not the third point — the reason the closed entry gave for it was the
weaker half.

That also makes `544m` the more valuable of the two for the fit, since the
24 MiB family *is* the fit family: a 1-reader point against a window of 11 to 24
is the smallest arrangement the mechanism has, which is where a fit is checked
(`.claude/skills/evidence/SKILL.md`, rule 2 — a term fixed at one end of an axis
and per-unit at the other is a clamped term, and this one is a `max(0, …)`).

**The axis is checked rather than remembered.** `reserve_floor_problems` is a
`--check` refusal beside the worker-count ones: a block size bills the floor
exactly where some registered limit's discovered budget — `limit −
MEMORY_RESERVE`, the library's own rule mirrored — affords one block-decoding
reader and cannot afford `POOL_DEPTH` of them, and an axis reaching neither
window fails naming the window to register. It argues from the registered limits
rather than from a sitting, which is what lets it run before any reading exists;
the resolved count is `min(recommendation, fit)`, so a machine recommending fewer
readers lowers the count and *raises* the floor, making the window a sufficient
condition everywhere rather than a prediction of this box.

**Also corrected**: `pool_floor_bytes`' docstring illustrated the term as
"96 MiB at koji's 24 MiB blocks, 384 at 128, 2 GiB at 512". No reader count
makes all three true — 96 and 2048 are the values at zero readers, 384 the value
at one. It now states them at one reader, where the term is largest, and names
the two limits that bill the term against every other cell, which is zero.

## The three-count fit guard belongs to the model, and below three counts a secant is publishable

`M89` raised the flagless family's fit guard to three distinct reader counts and
named the constant `RESERVE_FIT_MIN_COUNTS`. Reviewing that choice settled where
the guard lives and what it withholds. `M90` is the work.

**The guard is a property of two-term least squares, not of either family.**
`resident = fixed + readers × per_reader` passes exactly through two points, so
at two distinct abscissae the residual printed beside it is zero by
construction — true wherever the model is fitted. `scripts/measure.py` has three
`_least_squares` call sites across two families: the flagless fit, its min/max
band, and the instrument account's `live_fit`. `M89` guarded one. The guard
therefore moves to the `_least_squares` boundary, where a fourth call site
cannot skip it, and the constant stops naming one family.

**The second site was never a decision.** `live_fit` admits a fit at two
distinct counts, and its refusal prose — *a line through one point is an
intercept asserted as a measurement* — restates `_least_squares`' own docstring.
That is the arithmetic floor copied inline, so there was no considered asymmetry
between the two families to defend.

**What narrows the instrument axis is a kill, not an axis edit.**
`RESERVE_INSTRUMENT_LIMITS` is derived from `RESERVE_LIMITS`, so that axis cannot
be shrunk on its own, and `M89` widened it to six limits spanning one to
twenty-four readers. `account_points` admits a leg only when it is neither killed
nor unarranged, and a kill removes the high-memory end — so the instrument
family's coverage is a **per-sitting** property that degrades under exactly the
condition `19.17.1` built the censoring machinery for. Two killed legs and the
instrument fit publishes in the same sitting where the flagless one refuses.

**Below three counts the slope survives and the intercept does not.** Both traps
behind the rule (the `evidence` skill, rule 2) are statements about the
intercept: curvature outside the window folds into it, and a clamped term reads
as a constant of the process. A difference between two distinct reader counts is
a measured secant and carries no model claim, so refusing the whole fit withholds
a number that is sound. Both families therefore print the **secant** below three
counts, naming the two legs it connects and offering no intercept and no
residual. That keeps `19.18`'s `98% of reader_bytes` comparison — the one reading
that says whether the charge the budget rule divides by is the charge a reader is
— alive in a censored sitting, where refusing the fit withdraws it silently.

**The registered axis is checked for fit-ability**, folded into
`reserve_floor_problems` rather than given a walk of its own: it argues from the
same registered limits and the same discovered budgets. It passes on both
families today, so what it buys is a later axis edit failing before a sitting
rather than after; the censoring case is covered by the secant, which is why the
static check is not where the guarantee comes from.

**It points the other way from the floor half it sits beside**, which is worth
saying because the two live in one function. The floor's window is *sufficient*
on every machine: the resolved count is `min(recommendation, fit)`, so a smaller
recommendation lowers the count and raises the floor. Fit-ability is only
*necessary*: the same cap collapses two distinct fits onto one count, so a host
with fewer cores than the axis has distinct fits publishes a secant on an axis
the check passed. The registered limits afford `[1, 11, 12, 19, 28]` readers at
24 MiB blocks and `[1, 4, 6]` at 128, so the second family sits exactly on the
bound.

## The margin applies the criterion twice, because `MEMORY_RESERVE` already contains one

`19.23` put the 20% criterion in front of the worker count and predicted
resident as `charge(n) + MEMORY_RESERVE`. That prediction re-subtracts a margin
the reserve already carries, and the review closing the entry it raised settled
that it is a double application rather than a conservative choice.

**The whole effect of the margin, at every evaluated leg, is `0.2 × limit`:**

| leg | count before `19.23` | after | drop | `0.2 × limit ÷ 58.03 MiB` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xz24 `1g` | 11 | 7 | 4 | 204.8 ÷ 58.03 = 3.5 |
| xz24 `1536m` | 19 | 14 | 5 | 307.2 ÷ 58.03 = 5.3 |
| xz24 `2g` | 28 | 21 | 7 | 409.6 ÷ 58.03 = 7.1 |

A modelling refinement does not reproduce the margin term to within the
discreteness of a reader at three limits; a second application of the same rule
does.

**The reason is how `19.16` defined the constant.** 384 MiB is the *smallest*
reserve whose worst rep leaves ≥20% under the cap rule `charge ≤ limit −
reserve`, so at the leg that decided it the two constraints are the same one.
At xz24 `1g`, r384's worst rep left 20.2% of 1024 MiB — resident 817.2 MiB — and
`11 × 58.03 = 638.3` of that is charge, putting the flat term at **178.9 MiB**.
The criterion's charge bound is then `819.2 − 178.9 = 640.3 MiB` against a cap
of exactly **640**. So `MEMORY_RESERVE = 0.2 × 1 GiB + flat term`, to within
0.3 MiB, and feeding it back in as the predicted flat term subtracts the
`0.2 × limit` it already holds.

**This refutes the reason the call was made.** The entry, and
[`architecture.md`](../design/architecture.md)'s paragraph beside the
mechanism, argued that the alternative was "a number picked off a probe to
loosen a safety rule". Separating the two terms does not loosen the criterion —
it stops enforcing it twice — and the arithmetic above needs no new reading:
`19.16` states that its account sections "were derived from `readings.json` by
arithmetic — no reading in them needs re-taking".

**What the margin is still for is the shape mismatch**, which is sound and
unchanged: a constant reserve leaves a shrinking *share* as the limit grows, so
a 64-core host resolves 28 readers at 2 GiB and is predicted to breach at 13.3%.
Fixing the large end by subtracting a constant margin at every limit is what
over-corrects.

**Two facts the entry did not weigh.** The measured flat range 83.6–214.6 MiB
predates `19.19` and `19.22`, which moved the pool floor into the charge, so
today's unnamed remainder is smaller than that range at low counts and any bound
taken from it is conservative in the safe direction. And the margin cannot reach
`M89`'s pool-floor gate legs whatever constant it uses: `Parallelism::fit` takes
`cap.min(ceiling)`, and at `544m` the cap is 160 MiB — below any allowance a
smaller constant produces — so `19.11`'s acceptance cells are not at risk.

**Admitted as `19.26`**, a numbered slice rather than a ledger row: it changes a
decision the spec records, so it takes a spec amendment and runs before `19.11`,
whose flagless axis it moves.

**The harness keeps two fault lines.** `charge_model_problem` faults a cell
whose unnamed remainder exceeds `MEMORY_RESERVE`. Moving that threshold to the
new constant alone would make a bound that is slightly low read as a failed
rule; keeping both, registered before the sitting, separates the two — over the
inner line is a finding about the bound, over the reserve is the rule not
holding. And the bound itself is **re-derived from `readings.json` under
today's charge model** rather than quoted from the 83.6–214.6 MiB range, which
was computed before the pool floor moved into the charge.

## The gate reads one fault line where the harness now has two

`19.26` gave `charge_model_problem` a second, tighter threshold at
`MEMORY_UNPOOLED_BOUND` precisely so that *the number the count is predicted
against is wrong* could be told from *the discovery cannot keep this
arrangement inside its allocation*. The renderer it feeds does not tell them
apart: any fault at all emits `**The model is refuted, and by these cells:**`
([`measure.py`](../../../scripts/measure.py), the model verdict in the
`reserve` renderer), and that verdict is the whole of `19.11`'s amended
acceptance. So a cell between the two lines — which the constant's own doc
comment, the harness's own docstring and
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md) all describe as leaving the
allocation intact — blocks the sweep's box exactly as a breach of the rule
does.

**It is an ordering artifact, not a disagreement.** The acceptance clause was
written at `8833ed2`, when the check had one threshold and "the model holds"
could only mean the reserve; `19.26` added the inner line at `2e7e172` and
nothing re-read the clause that consumes the verdict. The lesson is narrow and
mechanical: a check that gains a fault line owes a pass over whatever reads its
verdict, because the verdict is where a distinction gets spent.

**The exposure is real rather than theoretical.** The worst remainder in hand
is 214.6 MiB against a 256 MiB bound, that apparatus scatters 32–43 MiB, and
the sweep resolves counts `19.16` never measured — 9/10/16/23 readers at
24 MiB blocks against a grid whose worst cell was at seventeen. A fresh
worst-rep in the 256–300 MiB band is an ordinary outcome and would bar
publication of a figure whose own cells say the allocation held.

So `19.11`'s acceptance is amended a third time to **no evaluated cell above
`MEMORY_RESERVE`**, and the verdict is to name which band each faulting cell is
in. `M91` is the work, and it blocks `P19`: sizing the library constant upward
to buy the gate clearance was the alternative and is refused — that chooses a
constant by what a harness threshold tolerates rather than by what the readings
say, and the readings say 256 (beside the mechanism, in
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Execution model and API
surface", where the same round's review of the bound's value is filed).
