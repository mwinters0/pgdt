# 2026-09-12

## The reserve entry closes on 384, and the grilling found the charge wrong below four readers

`19.16`'s pick stands: **`MEMORY_RESERVE` ships at 384 MiB**, and `19.13`
carries it. Reconstructed against the criterion rather than read off the
probe's verdict, the pick is forced on this grid — at `1g` the criterion caps
the count at 11 (816.9 MiB passes, 827.3 fails), which needs a reserve in
(322, 381]; at `1536m` it caps at 19 (1224.4 passes, 1279.5 fails), needing
(367, 425]. 384 is the only candidate on the grid inside both. 512 was the
alternative the entry raised and it is declined: the criterion was registered
before the sitting, and choosing differently after reading the grid is what
this phase has twice been burned by.

**What the reserve is covering is not what `19.18` said it was.** Over the
block-path regime the worst-resident slope is 57.28 MiB a reader against the
58.03 [`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs)'s `block_reader_bytes` bills —
**0.987**, so the per-reader charge is right to 1.3% and glibc's arena
retention is already inside it. What sits above the charge is flat: the excess
`worst − 58.03 × jobs` is 135.7 MiB at two readers and 145.4 at twenty-four,
wandering 83.6–214.6 with no trend across jobs 2…24. `19.18`'s reading that
retention "rises with the count the allowance affords" is true of `fordblks`
and does not reach the reserve.

### The charge under-bills the pool floor, and the record says it does not

[`roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md)'s Future item "A block cache whose
retention floor tracks the reader count" states that P19 "charges the floor
honestly". **It does not.** `BufferPool::slots` clamps the block pool at
`POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` ([`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs), the
`set_limits` call in `XzSource::hint_parallelism`'s block-cache sibling), so
the pool holds at least four units however few readers there are, while
`block_reader_bytes` bills `2 × unit` a reader — which assumes `slots == jobs`.
Below four readers we hold units nobody charged for.

The size of it is `(POOL_DEPTH − jobs) × unit`, and `19.16`'s own readings
confirm it to 1.4 MiB on the 128 MiB-block file:

| jobs | worst RSS | budget | excess | `(4−jobs) × unit` | residual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 802.1 | 532.1 | 270.0 | 256.0 | 14.0 |
| 3 | 939.9 | 798.1 | 141.8 | 128.0 | 13.8 |
| 4 | 1077.7 | 1064.1 | 13.6 | 0.0 | 13.6 |
| 5 | 1342.6 | 1330.2 | 12.4 | 0.0 | 12.4 |
| 6 | 1607.5 | 1596.2 | 11.3 | 0.0 | 11.3 |

Five cells, residual flat at 11–14 MiB. That is an account, not a fit, and it
is derivable from `readings.json` with no new sitting.

**It is unbounded in the block size** — 96 MiB on koji's 24 MiB blocks, 384 MiB
at 128 MiB, 2 GiB on a 512 MiB-block file — which is why no constant can absorb
it and why it is a charge defect rather than a reserve one. It is also *not*
what 384 pays for: on the 24 MiB file the term is 48/24/0 MiB and the mid-range
failures the reserve was raised for are all at eleven readers and above, where
it is zero.

### The 24-worker cap is load-bearing and nothing said so

`2g` clears the criterion only because `available_parallelism` clamps 28
readers to this machine's 24. On a 64-core host the same limit resolves 28,
costing 1637 MiB of a 1664 MiB budget and predicting ~1774 MiB resident against
a 1638 MiB ceiling — **13.3%, a failure**, on a machine no leg ran. 384 is
validated to 2 GiB *on 24 cores*, and the phase's own grid cannot see the
difference.

### The arena question, answered against the record

Whether the reserve should be configured from the arena count was raised and is
declined, on grounds already written: the spec's "The arena cap is a deployment
setting, not a mechanism this binary ships" refuses `mallopt(M_ARENA_MAX, …)`
because a cap keyed to the resolved worker count is unreachable — glibc seeds an
arena per thread at that thread's first allocation, and the source-dependent
count arrives after source recognition, which is I/O. Nothing in the new
readings reopens it.

Introspecting the *maximum* is separately the wrong number. Observed arenas are
`readers + 2` (6/15/24/26 at 4/13/22/24 readers) against a glibc ceiling of
`8 × ncores` = 192 here, so our own thread count binds and the maximum never
does; there is no getter for `M_ARENA_MAX` in any case. The lever that exists
is setting it, which the spec section above already refuses.
