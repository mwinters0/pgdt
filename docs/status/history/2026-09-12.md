# 2026-09-12

## The reserve entry closes on 384, and the grilling found the charge wrong below four readers

`19.16`'s pick stands: **`MEMORY_RESERVE` ships at 384 MiB**, and `19.13`
carries it. Reconstructed against the criterion rather than read off the
probe's verdict, the pick is forced on this grid — at `1g` the criterion caps
the count at 11 (816.9 MiB passes, 827.3 fails), which needs a reserve in
(322, 381]; at `1536m` it caps at 19 (1224.4 passes, 1279.5 fails), needing
(367, 425]. 384 is the only candidate on the grid inside both. 512 was the
alternative the entry raised and it is declined: the criterion was registered
before the sitting, and choosing differently after reading the grid is what
this phase has twice been burned by.

**What the reserve is covering is not what `19.18` said it was.** Over the
block-path regime the worst-resident slope is 57.28 MiB a reader against the
58.03 [`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs)'s `block_reader_bytes` bills —
**0.987**, so the per-reader charge is right to 1.3% and glibc's arena
retention is already inside it. What sits above the charge is flat: the excess
`worst − 58.03 × jobs` is 135.7 MiB at two readers and 145.4 at twenty-four,
wandering 83.6–214.6 with no trend across jobs 2…24. `19.18`'s reading that
retention "rises with the count the allowance affords" is true of `fordblks`
and does not reach the reserve.

### The charge under-bills the pool floor, and the record says it does not

[`roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md)'s Future item "A block cache whose
retention floor tracks the reader count" states that P19 "charges the floor
honestly". **It does not.** `BufferPool::slots` clamps the block pool at
`POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` ([`io.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/io.rs), the
`set_limits` call in `XzSource::hint_parallelism`'s block-cache sibling), so
the pool holds at least four units however few readers there are, while
`block_reader_bytes` bills `2 × unit` a reader — which assumes `slots == jobs`.
Below four readers we hold units nobody charged for.

The size of it is `(POOL_DEPTH − jobs) × unit`, and `19.16`'s own readings
confirm it to 1.4 MiB on the 128 MiB-block file:

| jobs | worst RSS | budget | excess | `(4−jobs) × unit` | residual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 802.1 | 532.1 | 270.0 | 256.0 | 14.0 |
| 3 | 939.9 | 798.1 | 141.8 | 128.0 | 13.8 |
| 4 | 1077.7 | 1064.1 | 13.6 | 0.0 | 13.6 |
| 5 | 1342.6 | 1330.2 | 12.4 | 0.0 | 12.4 |
| 6 | 1607.5 | 1596.2 | 11.3 | 0.0 | 11.3 |

Five cells, residual flat at 11–14 MiB. That is an account, not a fit, and it
is derivable from `readings.json` with no new sitting.

**It is unbounded in the block size** — 96 MiB on koji's 24 MiB blocks, 384 MiB
at 128 MiB, 2 GiB on a 512 MiB-block file — which is why no constant can absorb
it and why it is a charge defect rather than a reserve one. It is also *not*
what 384 pays for: on the 24 MiB file the term is 48/24/0 MiB and the mid-range
failures the reserve was raised for are all at eleven readers and above, where
it is zero.

### The 24-worker cap is load-bearing and nothing said so

`2g` clears the criterion only because `available_parallelism` clamps 28
readers to this machine's 24. On a 64-core host the same limit resolves 28,
costing 1637 MiB of a 1664 MiB budget and predicting ~1774 MiB resident against
a 1638 MiB ceiling — **13.3%, a failure**, on a machine no leg ran. 384 is
validated to 2 GiB *on 24 cores*, and the phase's own grid cannot see the
difference.

### The arena question, answered against the record

Whether the reserve should be configured from the arena count was raised and is
declined, on grounds already written: the spec's "The arena cap is a deployment
setting, not a mechanism this binary ships" refuses `mallopt(M_ARENA_MAX, …)`
because a cap keyed to the resolved worker count is unreachable — glibc seeds an
arena per thread at that thread's first allocation, and the source-dependent
count arrives after source recognition, which is I/O. Nothing in the new
readings reopens it.

Introspecting the *maximum* is separately the wrong number. Observed arenas are
`readers + 2` (6/15/24/26 at 4/13/22/24 readers) against a glibc ceiling of
`8 × ncores` = 192 here, so our own thread count binds and the maximum never
does; there is no getter for `M_ARENA_MAX` in any case. The lever that exists
is setting it, which the spec section above already refuses.

### What was admitted

Three slices, allocated in discovery order and scheduled in another —
[`STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md) carries both. **`19.13` ships the 384 MiB constant
ahead of all three**, being the row that makes discovery work at all; re-taking
a number after `19.22` is cheaper than leaving the budget rule unshipped behind
it. Then `19.24`, a reading over data already in the tree and what would have
caught the under-bill; then `19.22`, which corrects the charge; then `19.23`,
which removes the core-count dependence.

## A `parse` reports what was asked for and never what ran

Grilling the "Decisions worth another look" entry `19.13` left open — a
declined block path is reported on `query` and not on `parse` — found the
entry's framing too narrow in two places and its coverage claim true in only
one arrangement. The entry is closed; the work it implies is `M87` and `19.25`.

**The decline costs `parse` throughput, not only `query`.** The entry leaned on
`PlanNoteKind::CompressedBlockPathDeclined`'s own doc, which says the mapping
pass "only reads forward, costs the same either way". That holds for *backward*
reads and is not the whole cost: `leader::scan_region` returns
`RegionScan::Declined` when `advice.max_partitions() == Some(1)`, which is
exactly the shape a declined source advertises and is how
`stream::compressed_block_path_declined` detects it. So a declined `.xz`
`parse` is serial whatever `--jobs` says.

**The mode report covered the decline in one arrangement only.**
`ParallelArgs::resolve_in`'s stated-budget arm returned
`Parallelism::workers(jobs, stated)` without consulting
`Parallelism::discover_in`, so a recommended count was never lowered when
`--parallel-memory` was stated. `pgdq parse --parallel-memory 33554432` against
a large-block `.xz` on a 24-core host reported `24 (recommended by the
source)`, ran one serial reader with the block path declined, and said nothing
else — a count that would not be delivered, printed as a recommendation. That
also falsified `Resolved::jobs_display`'s doc comment, which claimed a
recommendation "is lowered to what the allowance affords and printed lowered".
`M87` closes it, below.

**So the gap is the delivered count, not the decline.** On `parse` nothing ever
reports that the delivered worker count differs from the announced one, and the
only mechanism that corrects the announcement is a plan note `parse` cannot
see; `query` is covered on both counts. Printing a decline beside a `jobs=24`
line that is itself wrong is a worse end state than today's silence, and the
general statement is the one a future source inherits — "the block path" is one
container format's word for it.

**The report is the library's, and provenance stays the CLI's.** The entry
offered two routes and priced them; a third exists that neither considered.
`stream::map_forward` already emits `scan started` on both commands
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Status
output"), so the fact can be stated where it is known — `leader::scan_region`
is the only party that sees the advice — without a new public channel and
without the CLI re-deriving the source's arithmetic, which
`compressed_block_path_declined` refuses by name. The CLI keeps
`Resolved::plan_note_origin`'s clause: the library says what bound, the CLI
says where the number came from.

**A stated budget does not exempt a recommended count from the allowance.** The
standing rule is "never allocate workers there is no memory for"
([`../../design/roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md), "A default runs as fast
as the allocation permits"), and `resolve_in` scopes it to "the absence of a
flag and never its presence" — but the flag whose presence is respected there
is `--jobs`, and in this arrangement `--jobs` is absent. Nothing states that
stating a budget should make a recommended count stop answering to it, so the
asymmetry is an oversight rather than a decision. `M87` removes it.

## `M87`: a recommended count answers to a stated budget too

`ParallelArgs::resolve_in` now lowers a **recommended** worker count on both
arms. Where `--parallel-memory` is typed and `--jobs` is not, the source's
recommendation is cut to what those bytes afford, through
`Parallelism::recommended_within` — `discover_for`'s own `fit`, over a budget
that arrived typed rather than discovered. So `pgdq parse --parallel-memory
67108864` against a 24 MiB-block `.xz` on a 24-core host reports `jobs=1`
instead of the `24 (recommended by the source)` it announced beside one serial
reader.

**Only the count moves.** Discovery hands back the bytes the lowered count
spends, because it chose them and must not name a budget the allowance never
granted; a stated budget is taken whole, a stated flag winning outright. The
asymmetry with `--jobs` is unchanged: a count somebody typed is printed as
typed, and what it delivers stays `stream::worker_count`'s to decide.

**The lowering clause names which budget did it.** `by the allocation` where
the number came off the environment, `by the stated budget` where it was typed
— the recourse is a different number in each case, and pointing at a cgroup
nobody set would be the new arm's own way of misleading. `Resolved` already
carries `budget_stated`, so the discriminator is exact: a stated budget wins
over a discovered limit, so it is what lowered the count whenever it is
present.

It is an out-of-band row rather than a slice because no spec'd decision moves:
the standing rule scopes the lowering to the absence of `--jobs`, which is
absent on this arm, so what landed is the rule already written applied where it
had not been reaching.
