## 7.16's residual is admitted as 7.17, and its stated closure path was wrong

The review of 7.16's "Decisions worth another look" entry settled three things:
the +3.8% array-render residual is closed rather than filed as a property, the
closure named in 7.16's notes was aimed at the wrong structure, and the work is
scoped to the array arm alone.

**The residual is larger than the number it was reported as.** The entry read
the +3.8% as the wrapper split — an element passing through `render_field` and
then `render_field_into` where one function used to do. Reading the arm itself
says the split is the smaller part of it. `render_field_into`'s `Array` arm is
`out.push_str(&render_array(column, row, child)?)`, so an array-bearing row pays
a `String` per element, the `Vec` collecting them, a whole-array `String` that
`nested::render_array` starts at `String::new()` and grows by `push_str`, and
then a copy of the finished text into the caller's buffer. That
un-presized-`String` growth is the same pathology 7.16 diagnosed and fixed in
`push_padded` and in `render_field`'s wrapper; the array path kept it. What
makes it worth a row rather than a property is where the mass is:
`projection-widths` prices the two array columns at **+13.21 µs a row**, 93% of
what all three nested columns cost together and more than every scalar column in
that table.

**The `Cow` is not the lever, and reshaping it would have cost the decode
reading.** 7.16's notes named the closure as reworking `nested::ArrayLiteral` so
an element need not own its text. `batch::collect_array` fills `elements` with
`render_field(..)?.map(Cow::Owned)` — unconditionally owned, because it renders
an Arrow value rather than slicing a literal — so the lifetime that arm carries
does no work on the render path at all. It does work on the decode path, which
is what `measurements.md`'s "Nested decode costs what it copies" measures at the
77 ns / 48 ns per-element split, so a session following the notes as written
would have reworked the structure holding a published reading in order to buy
nothing. Both the notes and `architecture.md`'s "The nested literal codec" now
say the borrow serves decode only. The shape that does close it is to not build
an `ArrayLiteral` on the render side: write each element through
`render_field_into` while walking the Arrow list, against a scratch buffer
reused across elements — a scratch is required rather than incidental, because
`push_token` decides quoting from the finished element text.

**The scope is the array arm, on evidence rather than symmetry.** All five
nested arms stop at the same boundary — `Multirange` builds a `Vec<String>` and
joins it, `Record` and `Range` build their literals and push the rendered text,
`Int2Vector` likewise — so the fix has an obvious generalisation and the
generalisation is not admitted. `projection-widths` prices the whole composite
column at **+0.98 µs a row**, decode, build and render together, and the other
three render one value a row against the arrays' fifty. 7.17 takes whichever of
the four fall out of the same shape at no extra risk and promises a measurement
for arrays only; "push the sink through every nested form" would be a refactor
with a measured win on one arm and hope on four.

7.17 runs ahead of `7.12` under the standing rule already in the phase spec —
any row admitted after spec time against a typed-query profile does — and owes a
control that renders nothing, which is `parse`: 7.16 established that a
`strings` query reaches `render_field` for its sixteen text columns, so 7.15's
control cannot serve a render-path row.

## `quoted_by` names a document and cannot say a sentence went false

P7's wrap audit found four things the slices had learned that the subject-filed
docs did not carry, and **three of the four were the `af15eac` fold-in's own
consumer re-read stopping short**. The `allocator` figure declares
`architecture.md` in `quoted_by`, the fold-in re-read that file, and the
section it points at still said "nothing beats the platform allocator
anywhere" — the sentence the sweep had just falsified — under the *previous*
stamp's ranking. The census reconciliation number, three of the projection
table's per-column deltas and the whole closing paragraph of the
`query-profile` section were stale the same way.

**The mechanism did what it promises and the promise is narrower than it
reads.** `quoted_by` names a *document*; a fold-in then has to find, inside a
6,700-line file, every sentence that a number moved. The three misses are all
sentences that quote a figure without naming it: "nothing beats it anywhere" is
a claim licensed by the allocator table and contains none of its numbers, and
the projection deltas sit in a paragraph the fold-in did update — it changed
the three numbers that appear in the figure's own table and left the three that
are arithmetic over it. So the failure is not laziness; it is that the unit of
the edge is a file and the unit of the error is a clause.

**No check is proposed, and the reason is the same one that already applies to
the manual.** A mechanical version would reconcile numerals in a consumer
against the figure's cells, and measured against these four instances it would
have caught the projection deltas and missed the two that carry no digits.
Keying on the claim rather than on the number is what a second attempt has to
do. Until one exists this is discipline, and the honest statement of it is:
**a `quoted_by` re-read is a re-read of the argument, not of the numerals**, and
the sentences most likely to go false are the ones that state a conclusion the
figure licenses rather than a figure it quotes.

## `M55`: the pool keeps the chunk size the caller asked for

The read path's buffer pool no longer decides what to keep by length alone.
**`ByteRangeSource::hint_read_size` is a third, advisory method**, defaulted to
doing nothing, through which each of the three read loops — `scan::scan`,
`stream::map_forward` and the replay loop in `stream::table_stream` — announces
its `ScanOptions::chunk_size` once before it starts. `io::BufferPool` keeps a
released buffer that is under the 8 MiB ceiling *or* exactly the announced
length, so a raised `--chunk-size` is pooled like any other and the cliff behind
the flag is gone. The cost is the one the admitting entry priced: `POOL_SLOTS`
is 4, so a 16 MiB chunk can hold 64 MiB against the flat ~9 MiB a scan
otherwise sits at.

**Exactly the announced length, not "up to" it.** A span read smaller than a
large chunk is still the one-off allocation the ceiling exists to drop, so it is
dropped; `attach_text`'s coalesced read is unaffected whatever `chunk_size` is.

**The hint is on the trait because `ScanOptions` is where the size is
configured.** A `LocalFileSource`-only setter called by the CLI would leave a
library embedder configuring one thing in two places and losing pooling
silently, which is the failure being closed. It is deliberately outside the
`object_store` surface the other two methods mirror, and an `object_store`
backend that takes the default behaves exactly as it would without it — which
is also the thing P14 must not inherit by accident, and is filed in that
phase's inbox. Both alternatives, including inferring the chunk size from
repeated lengths inside the pool, are beside the mechanism
([`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Execution model and API
surface").

**The admission rule was re-tested against the work rather than the
description**, and it holds: the fix is the one the architecture doc already
named, it changes no decision any live spec records, and it fits one session.

**Sixteen figures went red and no sweep was launched.** The diff adds
executable lines to `io.rs`, `scan.rs`, `stream.rs` and the CLI, so neither
mechanical oracle reaches it and no acknowledgement is written. One published
number actually moves — the `chunk-size` table's 16 MiB row, taken when a chunk
that large missed the pool — and it is called out both in that table's prose and
beside the mechanism, rather than being replaced by a partial sitting that would
spend the `af15eac` stamp's one-sitting property for one row. A stale figure
obliges no sweep; the next full one re-takes them
([`measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md), "A stale figure does not
oblige a sweep"). The 1.83× itself is kept as what a pool miss costs on a real
file, which nothing else in the register states.

**Five documents said the old thing and were corrected in the same change**:
the manual's `--chunk-size` section, `parse --chunk-size`'s help,
`DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE`'s doc comment, `STATUS.md`'s CLI row, and the fact entries
in P14's and P16's inboxes, whose numbers were also two stamps old.

## Both `M55` decisions reviewed: the trait keeps its advisory method, and the stale row keeps its place

The two entries `M55` left under "Decisions worth another look" were put to the
maintainer and both calls stand. Their reasoning is filed beside the mechanisms
they govern — the trait's in
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Execution model and API
surface", as a third rejected alternative; the figure's in the `chunk-size`
section of [`measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md) — and the entries
are deleted. What the review found that the entries had not is below.

**The `object_store` mirror is an argument about the three *required*
methods.** A defaulted method sits outside it by construction: a ranged backend
inherits the do-nothing body and is exactly where it would be without the
method, so refusing one buys a trait describable in a sentence and costs the
announcement its only home outside a single implementation's inherent API —
which is the setter already rejected. Deleting the method later is mechanical,
where finding that a remote backend wanted the caller's read length and having
nowhere to put it is not; P14's and P16's inboxes already carry entries that
depend on it existing.

**`BufferPool::keeps` is a disjunction, and its comment claimed an exactness
the code has only above the ceiling.** The comment said a span read smaller
than a large chunk is still dropped; `len <= POOL_MAX_BYTES || len == hinted`
keeps *any* buffer under 8 MiB, hint or no hint, and `take` picks the smallest
buffer that fits — so a sub-ceiling span read can be pooled and then handed to
a chunk read and retained. That is unchanged from before `M55` and bounded, but
it makes the pool's real ceiling `4 × max(8 MiB, announced)` rather than four
chunks. Corrected in the comment, in `POOL_SLOTS`'s bound, and beside the
mechanism, where the flat-RSS claim can see it — `M58`, still queued, re-homes
that claim into a registered figure and inherits the corrected bound. The code
was left alone: making the hint authoritative would drop pooling for the map
path's own reads and move behaviour a second time, for a hazard no RSS reading
has shown.

**The stale 16 MiB row was carrying an argument, not just a number.** Besides
the 1.83× price of a pool miss, which is deliberately kept as history, the
`fadvise` refusal rested on that row being the slowest cold-NVMe reading by a
clear margin. Its cold penalty (0.54 s) is the same order as its warm one
(0.42 s), so plausibly the whole of it is the per-chunk `calloc` and a re-take
could bring it back to a tie. The refusal survives either way, but the sentence
carrying it would not have, so the paragraph now rests on the 4 MiB and 8 MiB
rows — which run at or below the pool ceiling and cannot have moved — and says
what a tie would and would not change. That is the third option neither entry
considered: leave the row stale *and* repair what leans on it, which costs
nothing and needed no sitting.

**No partial sitting was taken, and none is admitted.** `chunk-size` declares
no `shares` and no derived consumer, so a sitting of it alone would strand
nobody — the cost is only that `measurements.md` stops being one sitting, which
a full sweep restores anyway and which sixteen red figures make likely enough
to wait for.
