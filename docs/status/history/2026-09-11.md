# 2026-09-11

## `M80`: help text is not wrapped, and nothing looked

`clap` wraps help text at **no width at all** without its `wrap_help` feature —
not the terminal's, not `COLUMNS`. Every flag's help here is the rustdoc above
it and most of those are paragraphs
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "A flag's help
is its doc comment, citations included"), so the four commands printed single
lines up to **748** columns. A terminal hard-wraps those mid-word with no
hanging indent, which destroys the option-list alignment a help page is read by.
The feature is now on; the width is the terminal's, falling back to `COLUMNS`
and then to `clap`'s own 100 columns.

It survived every session that wrote one of those comments because the rendering
is invisible from the source: nothing under `pgdump_query-cli/tests/` rendered a
help page, so no artifact anywhere showed what a reader sees. `tests/help_text.rs`
now holds all eight pages — both depths of the root command and of the three
subcommands — at a stated `COLUMNS`, so a wording change puts the rendered
result in front of a reviewer.

**Two properties are asserted beside the snapshots rather than left to them**,
because a snapshot of a broken page is exactly as plausible as a snapshot of a
good one: no line exceeds the width, and no flag renders with nothing beside it.
Both were verified to fail by reintroducing the defect. The second found one:
`pgdq info --detail` had no doc comment at all and printed as a bare spec in the
option list. Its help is written from the manual's own description of the flag.

**It went ahead of `19.11`.** `pgdump_query-cli/Cargo.toml` is a declared
`depends` path of the `allocator` and `rss-attribution` figures, so landing a
feature change after the closing sweep would put `rss-attribution` — the figure
that sweep exists to publish, closing `M74` — stale on its first outing. Landed
here it adds no red that is not already there: `uv run measure.py --stale`
reports both paths this change touches as already held red by earlier commits,
and `19.11`'s sweep republishes them.

## Closed: whether `--help` should be split from rustdoc

Raised by `19.10`, reviewed the same day, and the call was affirmed: a flag's
help stays its doc comment, citations and emphasis included. The reasoning is
filed beside the mechanism ([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md),
"A flag's help is its doc comment, citations included"), including both
rejected alternatives — a `long_help` per flag, and help rewritten as the
manual's abstract.

Three facts the entry had not established, and the first reframed it:

- **`clap_derive` already splits the text by depth.** The first paragraph of a
  doc comment is the short help `-h` prints; the whole comment is the long help
  `--help` prints. The unserved split was help against rustdoc, not short
  against long.
- **The leakage is not confined to `--help`.** `-h` already prints a citation on
  `--preamble-only` and `--chunk-size`, and `pgdq --help`'s subcommand list
  prints `**bold**`.
- **Help-and-manual duplication was already the accepted shape**, on the record:
  a raised chunk size's whole cost is deliberately stated in the flag's help, in
  the manual and in `DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE`'s doc comment. So the alternative's
  duplication cost was not a new one being introduced — which is what made
  `19.10`'s own three falsified manual sentences the decisive argument against
  paying it twice over.

One rider considered and dropped: citing a measurement by figure id rather than
by heading. `citations.py`'s id form is `<!-- section: <id> -->`, which exists
only in `architecture.md` and only on four profile sections;
`measurements.md`'s `<!-- figure: <id> -->` markers are a different register
that citations never address. No help-text citation names a finding-quoting
heading in any case.

## The budget rule's headroom fails at 512 MiB, and not where it was predicted to

`19.15` ran the shipped rule in real cgroups and the gate did not pass. A
flagless `.xz` `parse` inside a **512 MiB** allocation resolves three readers
and a 195 MiB budget, and holds **474 MiB median, 509 MiB at its worst of
thirteen reps** — 0.57% of the limit at the top of the distribution, against a
per-rep spread of 19.5%. Nothing was killed in thirteen attempts, which bounds
the tail rather than clearing it. Readings, apparatus and the rest of the legs:
[`../../design/roadmap-P19.15-budget-probe-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P19.15-budget-probe-notes.md).

**The band the reserve review named is comfortable, and the band nobody looked
at is the thin one.** 1.5 GiB leaves 33% and 1 GiB leaves 9%; 512 MiB leaves
1.6% of three reps and 0.6% of ten. The review's headroom table was computed
from `19.12`'s `180 MiB + 59.4` a sub-stream, which was fitted across a *stated*
budget with the count pinned at twenty-four. A flagless run brings the count
down with the budget, so that line was extrapolated onto an axis it never
measured: refitted against what a discovered allowance actually resolves,
resident is **403 MiB + 31.2 MiB a reader** (±36 MiB). The intercept more than
doubled and the slope halved, and the errors cancel at twenty-four readers and
compound at three.

**So the fixed term, not the per-reader one, is what exceeds the reserve** —
403 MiB against `MEMORY_RESERVE`'s 256 — and the smallest allocation that
reaches the block path at all is where that bites. **Corrected the same day:
that intercept is an artifact of fitting a line over three to twenty-four
readers, and there is no fixed term** — see "`M81` and `M82` are both withdrawn,
and the account they were guarding is wrong", below. The refit was right that
`19.12`'s line was extrapolated onto an axis it never measured; it was wrong
that the replacement was a better line.

**The reopening the spec named is refused by the same reading.** "A proportional
term is what it reopens to" was written when the thin band was believed to be
large-limit; a proportional reserve is *smaller* at a small limit, so any
fraction leaving a 1.8 GiB allocation 20% would hand a 512 MiB allocation more
budget than 256 MiB does. What replaces it is below, in "Closed: what
the reserve reopens to": two of the three candidates that looked open are one
knob, the margin the constant must leave is now stated, and `19.16` takes the
reading it is chosen from. `19.13`'s box stays open until it does.

**It is not arena retention, which was the standing account.**
`MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` buys 61.6 MiB at 512 MiB and 145.4 MiB at 1 GiB — a sixth
of the fixed term, not the bulk of it — lifting the 512 MiB median headroom to
19.4% and leaving ~340 MiB unattributed. What the pools can hold accounts for
about 134 MiB of the leg — the block pool's ceiling is `POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` =
4 slots at 24 MiB, so 96 MiB and not the 192 a per-reader reading of the charge
suggests — and the rest is not yet named. The probe does not attribute it, and
`rss-attribution` does not either: its inputs are plain files.

**Two readings worth keeping beyond the gate.** The unlimited arm works at
scale: at 8 GiB the *source's* recommendation binds (24 readers, 1560.8 MiB
asked for) and resident is 1137 MiB, so the charge is a bound rather than an
occupancy and no unbounded run was needed to see it. And `KD18`'s shape costs
80.5 MiB inside a 1 GiB container rather than its 209 MiB, because the
discovered 64 MiB budget admits eight of the twenty-four workers stated — the
deficiency is bounded by the budget as well as by the flag.

## Closed: what the reserve reopens to

The reserve's reopening is settled, and the entry that held it is gone. Three
things the failing gate did not by itself decide, and one correction to how it
was read.

**The rule had no margin to lose.** `budget = limit − MEMORY_RESERVE` aims
resident *at* the limit by construction, the reserve being meant to equal the
fixed term, so every margin the rule has ever left was an accident of two
over-estimates: `Parallelism::fit` grants only whole readers (195.1 MiB of a
256 MiB cap at 512 MiB), and the per-reader charge bills 65.03 MiB against a
marginal 31.2 MiB measured. Both scale with the reader count, which is why the
thin point is the *smallest* limit reaching the block path and why the headroom
predicted from `19.12`'s pinned twenty-four-worker fit came out inverted along
the axis. So the criterion is stated rather than inferred — **worst observed rep
leaves ≥20% of the limit**, median as context — and 20% because the spec had
already been reasoning with that number implicitly, which makes stating it cost
no argument already made.

**The constant and the floor are one knob.** `BlockCache::affordable` compares
`reader_bytes` against the budget, and the budget is `limit − reserve`, so a
reserve near the measured 403 MiB fixed term declines block decode below roughly
`reserve + 65 MiB` with nothing else written. A named `BLOCK_PATH_MIN_LIMIT` was
refused: two numbers deriving one boundary is the drift `19.14` had just removed
from the adjacent function by collapsing the divisor and the decline into one
`reader_bytes`. What the implicit floor owes is a report, which `19.13` gains —
a compressed scan going serial is a throughput cliff, and an operator should not
have to infer its cause from a status line that says only what was resolved.

**The constant waits on one reading, which is `19.16`.** Every candidate near
the fixed term resolves a *one-reader block path*, and the sitting holds n=1
*streaming* (15.1 MiB) and n=3 block (474 MiB) with nothing between — so
choosing the number now would extrapolate across the discontinuity that had
just broken the previous extrapolation, to save the four minutes a probe on
`19.15`'s own apparatus takes.

**One correction to the notes' arithmetic.** `roadmap-P19.15-budget-probe-notes.md`
attributes the 512 MiB leg partly to "eight coupled block slots at 24 MiB".
`XzSource::apportion` sets the block pool's depth to `POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` =
`max(4, 3)` = **4**, and `BufferPool::slots` clamps there, counting free and
retained together — so that pool's ceiling is 96 MiB, not 192. The unattributed
remainder the notes report is unchanged at ~340 MiB; only the route to it was
wrong. `rss-attribution`, the figure whose name suggests it would account for
this, runs on `blocks500`/`blocks4000` and reaches the compressed path nowhere.

Two decisions filed with it. The criterion is **not** promoted to a standing
rule over future sources: `MEMORY_RESERVE` is one shared constant, so there is
no second site to govern, and binding ungrilled phases to 20% calibrated on one
file's distribution is the move this sitting's own finding argues against. And
the unexplained remainder becomes **`KD19`**, `(c)` unowned — the deficiency
being the decline it forces on a small allocation rather than the bytes
themselves, since `--parallel-memory` and the arena cap reach only part of it.

No figure moved: the only code hunk is `MEMORY_RESERVE`'s doc comment, and every
figure declaring `pgdump_query/src/io.rs` is already red from this phase's
substantive commits, with `19.11`'s sweep the sitting that replaces them. An
acknowledgement was not filed because it would be inert on all of them — the
shape `--stale` prints as "excused here but inert".

## The compressed path never got the account the plain path got

`19.15`'s unexplained ~280 MiB is a hole in `P19`'s spec, not a finding of
`19.15`'s. The spec carries a section and a prerequisite slice — `19.2` — for
"The plain path is accounted for before its default is chosen", and wrote no
compressed counterpart, while the compressed path is the one whose default the
phase then could not set. Its own binding precondition says no default is
defensible "until the resident set is a bounded function of the stated budget",
and on that path it is not: 403 MiB sits outside the budget. So `19.16` as first
written would have chosen a constant in the state the spec called indefensible,
which is why the account now runs ahead of it.

**`reserve` gains flagless legs and keeps its stated ones.** The figure states
both the budget and `--jobs`, which is `19.12`'s arrangement — the one `19.15`
refuted as a predictor — so as registered the closing sweep would publish a
table that does not describe the shipped default. The stated legs nevertheless
stay: under discovery `budget = jobs × per_worker` exactly, so the budget axis
and the count axis are the same axis, and no fit over flagless legs alone can
say whether a resident byte is owed to a reader or to a budget byte. Flagless
legs say what the default holds; stated legs are what make the number
decomposable.

**One hypothesis worth recording because no reading has tested it.**
`BlockCache::slot` documents that a block evicted while a `Bytes` still views it
stays alive until that view drops — a live-block count nothing bounds, where
every other term is bounded by `slots()`. The allocator legs are what would
separate it from glibc fragmentation, fragmentation moving under jemalloc where
retained views do not.

The work is `19.17` (the instrument, reviewable cold) and `19.18` (the sitting,
which owns `KD19` and re-homes it from `(c) unowned` to `(b)`).

The account's sitting is **diagnostic**, and the closing sweep still publishes.
`reserve` is untaken, so there is no table to invalidate, and the trap in
publishing early is that `MEMORY_RESERVE` lives in a path the figure declares:
a table taken before `19.16` picks the constant goes stale the moment `19.13`
ships it, paying for a `warm-parallel` sitting twice to publish a number about
to move. So `19.11` keeps the publication, the `Shared` edge onto `peak-rss`,
and the `Session.borrow` change that edge requires — `borrow` copies wall clock
only, so no RSS reading can cross a share today, which is why no sweep has
declared it yet.

## The reserve shares a sitting with two figures and a reading with one

`19.17` registered `reserve`'s new families and found the spec's account of its
borrow graph false. The spec said all three resident-set figures "read resident
on shared shapes, so it declares `shares` edges"; the leg sets say otherwise, and
the maintainer settled it on review. The spec's paragraph and its `19.17` row now
state what is true, and `19.11` already carried the singular obligation.

**One edge, to `peak-rss`.** This figure's serial baseline is that figure's
`control` row spec for spec, so the two must borrow rather than measure it twice.

**No edge to `rss-attribution`, and that is a reading of the leg sets rather than
an omission.** Every leg of that figure runs `blocks500`/`blocks4000`, the two
block-count shapes whose axis it is; every leg here runs `control`, `control_xz`
or `control_xz128`. No two of their runs are the same run, so there is no reading
to borrow in either form a `Shared` takes — neither republished nor consumed. The
division is deliberate and already recorded beside the mechanism
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Execution model
and API surface"): `rss-attribution` instruments the *plain* path and publishes a
slope, which is why the compressed path gets an account of its own publishing an
intercept. `scripts/test_measure.py` computes both intersections over spec keys,
so a leg added later that does coincide fails rather than ages.

**Manufacturing the edge was refused.** The only way to create one is for
`reserve` to take a leg over a block-count input — a leg about `COPY`-block growth
inside a figure whose axis is the allocation. That would re-measure on the
compressed path what the plain path already publishes, and confound the intercept
`KD19` exists to attribute. It would also buy nothing at the scale in hand: koji
carries **74** `COPY` blocks, and per-block resident growth is already `KD14`,
unowned, naming "a dump with tens of thousands of tables" as what would promote
it. Measuring to satisfy a sentence inverts the relation between figure and spec;
the sentence was what was wrong.

## `19.18`'s sitting is killed by the default it was measuring

The compressed account's sitting ran (`measure.py --figure reserve --alone`,
`b06add3`, `runs/measure-20260911T055038/`) and **aborted on its fifth leg**:
`control_xz128` flagless in a 1 GiB container resolves two readers against a
546 MiB budget and is OOM-killed at 1024.4 MiB. `19.17` registered that
possibility as a reading rather than an apparatus failure, and this is it. The
figure fails fast, so the mechanism legs, the path step and the stated-budget
axis behind it were never reached and **no account was published**.

**What the four surviving flagless legs say.** At 24 MiB blocks, one rep each:
3 readers on a 195 MiB budget hold 447.8 MiB in `-m 512m`, 11 on 715 MiB hold
842.2 in `1g`, 19 on 1236 MiB hold 971.7 in `1536m`, and 24 on 1561 MiB hold
1136.9 in `2g` — a fit of **406 MiB + 31.1 MiB a reader**, which reproduces
`19.15`'s independent 403 + 31.2 on a different apparatus. The fifth leg,
128 MiB blocks in `512m`, declines the block path (one reader costs 266 MiB
against a 256 MiB budget) and holds **15.2 MiB**, which is the streaming
fallback and the other end of the path step.

**Why no container limit rescues that family.** A follow-up probe
(`runs/19.18-blocksize-charge-probe.py`, three reps, stated counts in an 8 GiB
container that never binds) reads what a reader actually holds at both block
sizes. Medians, and resident *above the stated budget* beside each:

| readers | 24 MiB blocks | above budget | 128 MiB blocks | above budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (serial) | 62.9 MiB | +4.9 | 270.8 MiB | +4.8 |
| 2 | 331.8 MiB | +215.7 | 1322.8 MiB | +790.7 |
| 4 | 499.5 MiB | +267.4 | 1826.4 MiB | +762.3 |
| 6 | 566.7 MiB | +218.5 | 2039.3 MiB | +443.1 |

So the quantity the reserve is a reserve *for* is a function of the block
size, not a constant of the process: 216–267 MiB at koji's block size and
443–791 MiB at the size one `xz --block-size=128MiB` flag produces. A flagless
run spends `limit − 256 MiB` on readers and then holds that much on top, so at
128 MiB blocks it overshoots every allocation whose block path it takes until
the reader count saturates — which is above ~2.4 GiB, off the end of the
registered axis. The 512 MiB leg survives only by declining the block path.

Two shapes inside that reading are worth keeping. **One reader is the serial
arrangement** — `Parallelism::workers(1, …)` collapses to `Serial` — so the
1→2 step costs +269 MiB (24 MiB blocks) and +1052 MiB (128 MiB), about four
times what one reader is charged, and it is the parallel machinery switching
on rather than a reader. And resident is **concave** in the count above that:
the marginal reader costs 83.9 then 33.6 MiB at 24 MiB blocks, 251.8 then
106.5 at 128 MiB. `fixed + readers × per_reader`, the pair the figure
publishes, is therefore a description of the flagless axis and not of the
mechanism; it fits the 3–24-reader legs and would mislead over 2–6.

**What this changes.** The spec's binding order — the compressed account, then
the constant — holds for the stated families and is backwards for the flagless
one: those legs measure the shipped default, and the shipped default is what
kills them. `KD19` is rewritten to what is now measured (the detail is beside
the mechanism, [`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md),
"Execution model and API surface"). What the reading means for the reserve
itself is the section below.

## A unit-independent fixed term is refuted, so the reserve cannot be one constant

The reading above was filed as a question about *ordering* — whether `19.16`
runs before `19.18`. Reviewed, it is a question about the rule: the quantity a
constant reserve has to cover scales with the **block unit**, and no ordering
fixes that.

**The refutation uses only the probe's medians.** The shipped arithmetic is
`resident = fixed + readers × reader_bytes`, with `reader_bytes = 2 × unit +
chunk + decode` and `fixed` — what the reserve is for — independent of the
file. If that held, then at equal reader counts `resident(128 MiB blocks) −
resident(24 MiB blocks)` would be exactly `readers × 2 × 104 MiB`, the two
files differing only in the unit. Measured against predicted:

| readers | measured difference | predicted | excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 991.0 MiB | 416 MiB | +575 |
| 4 | 1326.9 MiB | 832 MiB | +495 |
| 6 | 1472.6 MiB | 1248 MiB | +225 |

The excess is large and **falls as the reader count rises**, so it is not a
per-reader term that `reader_bytes` under-bills — it is a unit-scaled term that
is *not* per-reader. A constant reserve cannot cover it at any value, because
the quantity is a function of the file rather than of the process.

**The candidate named here is refuted the same day**, arithmetically and with
no run: at every measured cell the floor's unbilled share is zero at two readers
and negative above, so it cannot produce a positive excess at all. What the term
actually is — a partition read taking the copying arm — is below. The candidate
as it stood: the
block cache's slot count is `clamp(budget / unit, 1, POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs))`
(`io.rs`, `BufferPool::slots` and `XzSource::apportion`), so the cache retains
up to `POOL_DEPTH` *units* independently of the reader count — unit-scaled,
not per-reader, and unbilled by `reader_bytes`, which charges two units per
reader. Beside it sits `KD19`'s standing hypothesis, `BlockCache::slot`'s
evicted-but-still-viewed block, which is one more unit per live view and which
no pool bounds. Both have the measured shape. Separating them is what the three
mechanism legs were registered for, and they are the legs the abort skipped.

**The per-reader charge is not the fault.** Chord slopes over 2–6 readers are
58.7 MiB at 24 MiB blocks against a 58.0 MiB charge, and 179.1 MiB at 128 MiB
against 266.0 — exact at the small unit and conservative at the large one.
(Both are chords across a concave curve, so neither is a slope the rule should
take; what they rule out is the charge under-billing per reader, which was the
first reading of this data.)

## `M81` and `M82` are both withdrawn, and the account they were guarding is wrong

Reviewed with the maintainer. Both ledger rows go, and the review that filed
them turned out to have been arguing inside a frame that is itself false. Four
findings, in the order they bite.

**`M81` is withdrawn: its mechanism is false and the mirror is correct.**
`SeekTable::input_chunk()` is `.min(INPUT_CHUNK)`, and `xz --list -vv` gives max
compressed blocks of 4.6 MB / 24.5 MB / 1.7 MB for `control_xz` /
`control_xz128` / `koji_xz` — all three saturate the 1 MiB cap, so
`XZ_DECODE_FOOTPRINT` = 8,388,608 + 34,592 + 1,048,576 = 9,471,776 is exact for
every registered input and carries no per-file term. The 7,340,032 gap is
`POOL_MAX_BYTES − DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE`, identical at both block sizes: the
source's recommendation charges an 8 MiB chunk slot because it is asked before
the file is open for reading, where `BlockCache::affordable` charges the 1 MiB
steady-state slot. Doing what the row asked would substitute the recommendation
for the charge and make the `block_path` gate wrong.

**The over-ask does cost something, and it is a reader rather than idle bytes.**
`Parallelism::fit` divides the allowance by that same recommendation, so a
512 MiB allocation resolves `268,435,456 / 68,192,032` = three readers where
`/ 60,852,000` would fit four — a systematic under-count on every allocation.
Folded into `19.19` rather than filed as its own row: it is the same arithmetic
as the charge repair, and pricing it separately would price the charge twice.

**`M82` is withdrawn as misfiled.** It reverses a decision `19.17` registered —
`RESERVE_LIMITS`' fail-fast, which that slice's own docstring defends as "the
finding the constant is being chosen against" — so by the admission rule it
needs a slice, and it is re-filed as `19.17.1`. Two of its claims are also
false. The flagless family is not unrunnable at every registered limit: the
128 MiB leg at `512m` declines the block path and reads 15.2 MiB. And the
harness **cannot tell an OOM kill from any other failure** — `rss_wrapper`'s
`exit($st == 0 ? 0 : ($st >> 8) || 1)` collapses every signal death to exit 1,
verified directly, and `--rm` destroys the container before `inspect` could
say — so a licence to record kills as readings, written harness-wide as the row
wrote it, would have silenced the `parallel-peak-rss` near-miss that measured
3067 MiB in a 3072 MiB container. The oracle is the container reading
`/sys/fs/cgroup/memory.events`' `oom_kill` count after the timed command, and
the licence is per family.

**The `403 MiB` fixed term does not exist.** This is the expensive one, and it
stood for three sessions because two independent sittings agreed: `19.15` fitted
`403 + 31.2` a reader and `19.18` fitted `406 + 31.1` on a different apparatus.
Both fitted over three to twenty-four readers. Resident is **concave** in the
reader count, so the intercept is the concavity the window skipped — the same
file over one to six readers fits `62 MiB + 93.3`, and at one reader the process
holds **62.9 MiB** where `403 + 31.2` predicts 436. A term present in the process
at every arrangement cannot be absent at the smallest one. The concavity's
mechanism is `BufferPool::slots` running 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 6 over one to six
readers, which is the same clamp the design cites as the reason overhead is
*constant*. So `MEMORY_RESERVE` was being sized against an artifact, and
`19.15`'s "~340 MiB unattributed" was measuring the fitting window.

**The named candidate is refuted by arithmetic, with no run.** At every measured
cell `(budget − chunk_held)/unit` exceeds `POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)`, so slots are
2/4/4/6 against a bill of `2 × readers` = 2/4/8/12 — the retention floor's
unbilled share is zero at two readers and negative above, and cannot produce a
positive excess at any count. `19.18`'s three mechanism legs would have spent an
hour establishing that.

**What the excess actually is: a defect `19.14` introduced.** `partition_bytes`
is both the memory charge and `leader::run_region`'s cut size — `window_bytes =
workers × partition_bytes`, cut into `workers` pieces at block boundaries by
`stream::cut`, and `scan_partition`'s first read is the whole piece. Raising the
charge from `unit + chunk` to `reader_bytes` made a piece 2.42 blocks at 24 MiB
and 2.08 at 128 MiB, so the read spans blocks, takes `read_by_blocks`' copying
arm, and assembles into a fresh `vec![0u8; partition_len]` that
`BufferPool::keeps` refuses to pool and that is held through the parse.
`reader_bytes` bills one chunk for it. A charge that inflates what it charges
for. Three code comments and `architecture.md` all assert the read is one block
and zero-copy; all four were true before the change.

**With that term the account closes.** Modelling peak as `~15 MiB + slots ×
unit + min(readers, POOL_DEPTH) × partition` is exact at both serial cells —
62.9 and 270.8 MiB measured and predicted — and recovers 84 / 94 / 99% of the
measured cross-block-size difference at two / four / six readers, against the
shipped model's 42 / 63 / 85%. The remainder, 105 / 156 / 176 MiB at 24 MiB
blocks, tracks the measured ~31 MiB-a-reader glibc arena term, which is
unit-independent and so sits exactly where the unit-scaled terms leave a gap;
this machine runs an arena per core, which is why that term is large here.

**It is a throughput regression too.** Those partition buffers come from the
chunk pool, whose four slots then cap the fused workers: a stated-count `parse`
of a 24 MiB-block file runs 16.85 → 11.30 → 8.83 → 8.77 s at one, two, four and
six readers. `parallel-scan-throughput` publishes 5.82× at twenty-four workers
and was taken at `20fd77c`, two days before the change; `parallel-peak-rss` at
`e29939c` likewise. Neither describes the shipped partitioning. The flag is
written at the consumer (`architecture.md`, "What parallelism buys, and where it
stops") rather than in `measurements.md`, whose figure sections are the
harness's to write.

**What this reorders.** The repair is `19.19` and runs *first*, against the
spec's binding order, because its evidence is a failing unit test rather than a
sitting — build the four-block `SeekTable` fixture, call `partition_advice`,
feed `stream::cut`, assert every range spans one block. Every reading taken
before it prices the defect. Then `19.17.1`, then the re-taken `19.18`, then
`19.16`, `19.13` and the sweep. `KD19` is rewritten to the defect and
re-targeted from `19.18` to `19.19`; the spec carries it as a fifth amendment.

## `19.19` lands: the thin allocation moves, and the tail read's second decode is named

The repair is in the tree and `KD19` is struck. What it changes about the
*plan* is two things, neither of which was predictable from the defect.

**The thin allocation has moved off 512 MiB.** `19.15` and every argument built
on it read the smallest allocation that reaches the block path as the tight
one — 0.6% of the limit left at the worst rep — and reasoned that the margin
fails at the small end because the two over-estimates that produce it scale with
the reader count. Repaired, that leg leaves **30.1%**, and the cells that fail
the 20% criterion are 1 GiB (11.6%) and 1.5 GiB (10.3%). The reserve constant is
still `19.16`'s to choose and it is now chosen against a different shape: a
candidate taken off `19.15`'s curve is aimed at the end of the axis that no
longer binds. The readings are
[`../../design/roadmap-P19.19-per-file-term-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P19.19-per-file-term-notes.md);
the resulting decisions are in `STATUS.md`'s `19.13` and `19.16` rows and in
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Execution model
and API surface".

**The wall-clock column found a second term, and it is `KD20`.** A one-block
partition means a worker's chunk-sized tail read lands in the block its
*successor* owns, and `BlockCache` has no in-flight map — so both decode it, and
a parallel compressed scan does about twice the decode work of a serial one. The
speedup is therefore capped near half the reader count: 17.8 → 17.9 → 9.9 →
7.4 s over one, two, four and six readers, against a serial 17.8. At *stated*
low counts that is slower than the defect was, because a 2.42-block partition
amortised the tail over more blocks; at the flagless default, which resolves
thirteen readers in 1 GiB rather than two, it is 2.1–2.6× faster and the
four-worker ceiling is gone. The term is not new — it is what the arrangement
before `19.14` also did, and it is inside `parallel-scan-throughput`'s published
5.82×. What is new is that it has a name instead of being read as evidence that
widening a partition buys throughput.

**What this says about the next slices.** `19.18`'s three mechanism legs were
already dead arithmetically, and the axes that remain now measure a different
arrangement — both terms of the per-reader fit moved. `19.17.1`'s premise was
re-tested against this acceptance run and stands — see below.

## The route is judged on the repair, not on which phase it belongs to

`19.19` had two candidate repairs for the partition-length first read, and the
slice took the one-block partition. Reviewed: **a scope argument does not settle
which repair is right.** The spec's standing paragraph — that a repair reaching
`leader::scan_partition` is out of `P19` — and the `19.19` row's parenthetical
offering exactly that repair disagree with each other, and the disagreement is
bookkeeping. What decides the route is which repair is better, measured; the
phase a change gets filed under is chosen afterwards to fit the answer.

The facts that bear on it, established cold:

- **`scan_partition` has one caller** (`leader.rs:410`), the cold interior
  split. Partitioned replay does not reach it, so a change to its read shape
  touches the parallel *cold* scan only — and a plain source's cold default is
  `Serial`, so the plain arm it would alter is one a user reaches only by
  stating `--jobs`.
- **A chunk-sized first read is poolable where a partition-length one is not.**
  `BufferPool` keeps a returned buffer only when `len <= slot_bytes()`, so the
  copying arm of `XzSource::read_by_blocks` allocates and drops a fresh
  partition-length buffer per piece; at `DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE` (1 MiB) against
  `POOL_MAX_BYTES` (8 MiB) every read is a pooled one.
- **The cut width is what amortises `KD20`.** A worker decodes its successor's
  block for its tail read whatever the cut, so the wasted decode is one block
  *per piece*: a one-block piece pays 100%, `19.14`'s 2.42-block piece paid
  41%, and an eight-block piece would pay 12.5%. Pinning the cut to one block
  re-couples it to the source's retained unit, which is the memory-shaped
  number `window_end` was split apart from.

So the arrangement nobody has timed — the divisor kept, the first read made
chunk-sized, and the cut widened past one block — is not a number that cannot
change the decision. It is the one that decides it.

## Measurements are retained across the theories that motivated them

A reading taken for a theory since abandoned is still a reading, and this phase
has repeatedly re-derived arrangements it had already measured. **Retain every
potentially-relevant measurement, including those taken under a model no longer
held**, so that understanding accumulates and is continuously re-validated
rather than oscillating. Discarding a reading because its theory died is what
makes the next session re-take it.

**Reviewed and settled.** The one-block cut is **not** accepted as the right
repair — it is what is shipped while the deciding reading is taken. The
arrangement to time is the divisor kept, `scan_partition`'s first read made
chunk-sized, and the cut widened past one block, against both existing builds;
whichever wins is kept, and the outcome is genuinely open, because `19.14`'s
flat-above-four ceiling is still unexplained and may survive the read-shape
fix. `KD20` stays **(c) unowned**: widening the cut amortises the wasted decode
but does not remove it, and only the in-flight map would.

`w/2` is restated as a **ceiling** rather than an account, in both the notes doc
and `architecture.md`'s `KD20` paragraph, with the residual named as
unexplained — it allows 2.0 and 3.0 where 1.8 and 2.4 were measured, and 12×
where the published leg reaches 5.82×.

**How a retained measurement is kept.** A reading that argued a decision is
transcribed into a git-tracked doc — the slice's notes or the history entry — as
a table, at the moment it argues one; `runs/` holds the raw artifact and is
gitignored, so a reading left only there is a reading the next session re-takes.
Where raw readings are retained, prefer a **queryable** form over a log to be
grepped: `duckdb` is available globally here and a probe emitting JSON or a
small table can be selected against instead of parsed out of prose.

**The work admitted.** Not out-of-band — it changes a decision the spec
records — so it is a numbered slice, **`19.20`**, with the spec's paragraph
ruling `scan_partition` out of `P19` amended rather than worked around. It runs
**ahead of everything remaining**: `19.18`'s sitting attributes the compressed
arrangement and `19.16`/`19.13` choose the reserve constant, and the cut width
moves the per-reader charge, the resident curve and the thin allocation that all
three read. `19.17.1`'s premise was already undermined by `19.19`, so it is not
a slice to run ahead of a repair either.

It also re-pins the invariant. `a_block_decoding_partition_never_crosses_a_block_boundary`
and `window_end`'s "every piece lies within a single one of this source's units"
pin the arrangement under review, and a test reading as settled intent is how a
provisional choice hardens without anyone deciding it should. The property that
actually protects the read path is **no read exceeds `chunk_size`** — the
one-block piece was only ever a way of reaching it — so that is what `19.20`
pins, and it survives either outcome of the measurement.

## `19.20`: the cut width stays at one unit, and the read shape belongs to the source

The sitting the section above called "the one that decides it" has run, and it
decided against the arrangement it was written to test. Readings and the
account they confirm are
[`../../design/roadmap-P19.20-cut-width-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P19.20-cut-width-notes.md);
the decisions are beside their mechanisms
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "cut-width",
"The compressed source", "The interior split"). Three things changed the plan.

**The cut width's account is confirmed and the width is still one unit.** At two
stated readers the wall clock follows `w / (1 + 1/k)` within 10% at every width
tested, so `KD20`'s wasted decode is a priced term and widening does amortise
it — up to 1.64× at eight units. At the flagless default the ordering
*inverts*: thirteen readers in 1 GiB run 4.84 s at one unit against 9.2–9.7 s
at two, four and eight. A default twice as slow on the shape the phase is named
for is not bought by a stated-count win, so nothing about the compressed
arrangement moved. The inversion has no named mechanism; the arithmetic
available is that a window `workers × k` units wide asks the block cache for
`13 k` distinct blocks against thirteen slots, and that is recorded as a
candidate rather than a cause.

**The read shape is not one decision for all sources, which is the plan change.**
The row treated a chunk-sized body read as a repair that would ship whatever the
width turned out to be. Measured at equal count and equal budget it is worth
nothing on the compressed path and costs 76 MiB of median resident at thirteen
readers, killing one run in three in a 1 GiB allocation — while on the plain path
it is worth **22×**, 9.4 MiB at `--jobs 24` against 209.2, and is faster besides.
So `Partitioning` carries `PartitionRead` and the source says which shape it
wants. That is a mechanism the row did not name, taken because "keep whichever
wins" has two winners; it is flagged under `STATUS.md`'s "Decisions worth another
look" for that reason.

**`KD18` is struck by the plain reading**, and most of the roadmap's plain-source
Future item with it: the 8 MiB-a-worker arena retention was the un-pooled
partition buffer, and there is no longer a partition-length read to produce one.
What is left of that item is the `POOL_MAX_BYTES` cap on the partition product,
whose only remaining job is the cut width and whose removal is unmeasured. The
manual's claim that the arena cap wins back memory on a plain scan went false
with the reading and was corrected in the same change.

**One clause of the row could not be delivered.** It asked for the invariant to
be re-pinned on the read size — "no read exceeds `chunk_size`" — on the stated
ground that this survives either outcome. It does not: the winning arrangement
does not have that property on the compressed path. What is pinned instead is
the cut width, over a block table carrying more boundaries than the window asks
for, plus the chunked half of the read shape on the plain source. The spec row
is amended to say so — see "`19.20`'s refused clause, and the read shape as the
source's statement" below.

**No figure moved off red and none could.** `io.rs` and `leader.rs` are declared
by every scan figure and were already stale at this sitting's start; a library
change has no cheap oracle, so they stay stale until `19.11`'s sweep, which is
the standing rule rather than a new debt.

## `19.20`'s refused clause, and the read shape as the source's statement

The review of `STATUS.md`'s one open "Decisions worth another look" entry, which
asked two things: whether `19.20`'s box should be ticked with one clause of its
row refused, and whether `io::PartitionRead` — a mechanism the row did not name
— should stand.

**Both stand. What was wrong was that the spec row was left unamended.** The
entry framed the question as the box and the mechanism, and the actual defect
was neither: `roadmap-P19-efficient-defaults.md`'s `19.20` row still instructed
a chunk-sized body read and still asserted that "no read exceeds `chunk_size`"
"survives either outcome", which the sitting falsified. Four unticked slices
read that spec, and `19.18` has already cost three sessions to reviewing an
arrangement that had moved. The row is amended in place — the refused clause
struck, the decision that replaced it stated — which is a decision change and
not progress-tracking, and is the same disposition `19.15` and `19.12` took when
their readings refuted a spec'd decision.

**The box stays ticked, because unticking it would manufacture an obligation
nobody can discharge.** A ticked line is a record and an unticked one is a live
obligation; the refused clause cannot be delivered, since delivering it means
shipping the arrangement that was killed one run in three. A box that can never
be ticked is worse than a record read against an amended row. No `19.20.1`:
there is no deliverable left.

**`PartitionRead`'s hazard is real and is not the one the entry named.** The
entry offered a binary — keep the enum, or pick one shape for both sources and
pay 209 MiB on plain or a killed run on compressed. That binary is false: on a
one-unit piece, `PartitionRead::Whole` and a chunked read whose chunk is the
unit issue the identical single `read_range`, so the axis is a read *length*
rather than a two-valued shape. What the entry missed is that `Whole`'s
correctness argument is *"a piece is one unit"* — `BOUNDARIED_PARTITION_UNITS`
= 1's doing — while the same slice deliberately decoupled the width from the
read shape and **widened** the test that would have caught the pairing breaking,
from `19.19`'s `covering.len() == 1` to `<= k`. Raising the width, which this
slice's own notes say the evidence suggests, therefore re-creates the
un-poolable partition-length buffer `19.19` existed to remove, with every test
green. `M83` caps that buffer at one unit, which bounds it and does not make a
wider cut safe — a one-unit read is un-poolable at either width, and a
file-wide block maximum can still cross into a smaller successor block. The
width stays raisable only with the clip below.

**The end state is a read clipped to the next boundary**, which is safe at any
width by construction and is what the block path actually wants. It is not
taken now: it is unmeasured, and it only pays once the width varies with the
resolved count — which wants the `13 k`-wanted-blocks question answered first,
the same reason the notes doc refuses a varying width. Filed beside the
mechanism.

**Two facts that closed questions rather than opening them.**
`SeekTable::max_block_uncompressed()` already exists
([`vendor/xz-seek/src/table.rs`](../../../vendor/xz-seek/src/table.rs),
`max_block_uncompressed`), so `M83` is buildable as written rather than needing
a vendored change — which is the check `M81` and `M82` failed. And the growth
retry is **not** an asymmetry `19.20` introduced: `want` doubles to
`ScanOptions::max_line_bytes`, 64 MiB by default
([`pgdump_query/src/scan.rs`](../../../pgdump_query/src/scan.rs),
`ScanOptions::default`), so a long row reaches the same un-poolable ceiling from
either base — 24 → 48 → 64 on the compressed path, 8 → 16 → 32 → 64 on the
plain one. The ceiling is the serial path's documented bound, identical on both,
and no new deficiency hangs off it.

## `M83`: `Whole` carries the unit, and the chunked bound's prose is corrected

`io::PartitionRead::Whole` now carries the source's own unit and
`leader::scan_partition` reads `min(piece, unit)`; the block-decoding arm states
`BlockCache::unit`, which `BlockCache::for_table` sets from
`xz_seek::SeekTable::max_block_uncompressed()` and guards against zero. The
decisions are beside their mechanisms
([`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "cut-width",
"Execution model and API surface", "The compressed source", "The interior
split").

**Nothing observable moved, and that is checkable rather than measured.**
`BOUNDARIED_PARTITION_UNITS` is 1, so a piece spans at most one unit and
`min(piece, unit)` is `piece` — the identical single `read_range` at every
offset on every shipped input. The row said so and no re-measurement was taken.
What changes is what happens if the width is raised: the body buffer is capped
at one unit instead of growing with `k`.

**The bound needed a source that says `Whole` over a piece wider than a unit,
and no real one does.** The only source stating `Whole` also cuts its windows
into single units, so the bound is vacuous on it and a test written over it
would pass with the fix reverted. `leader.rs`'s
`no_read_a_worker_makes_exceeds_the_stated_unit` therefore restates a **plain**
source as `Whole` at a 24-byte unit against its eight-chunk (64-byte) piece —
verified to fail without the change, at 64 bytes read where 24 was stated.

**The `19.20` prose that called the chunked bound source-agnostic was false and
is corrected in the same change.** `no_read_a_worker_makes_exceeds_the_chunk_size`'s
own doc comment claimed the invariant held of every source, which is the clause
the sitting refused; it now says what it is — the bound on the `Chunked` shape —
and names the unit bound as the other half. That is the falsified-claim rule
applied where the claim sits, not a widening of the row.

**What it does not buy is unchanged**, and the row said this too: the copy
stays, because `BufferPool::keeps` admits only the announced chunk, so a
one-unit read is un-poolable at either width; and a file-wide block maximum can
still cross into a smaller successor block and take the copying arm. Raising the
cut width safely still wants the read clipped to the next boundary, which is
unmeasured and filed beside the mechanism. Admitted at
"`19.20`'s refused clause, and the read shape as the source's statement" above.

**No figure moved and none could.** `io.rs` and `leader.rs` are declared by
every scan figure and were already red before this change; a library change has
no cheap oracle, so they stay stale until `19.11`'s sweep, which is the standing
rule rather than a new debt. Behavioural identity at the shipped width is an
argument from the constant, not an acknowledgement.

## `19.17.1`'s premise stands, and the apparatus says so directly

`19.19`'s acceptance run killed no leg at any registered limit, which left the
kill licence looking like a solution to a problem the repair had removed. Tested
before building it, the premise holds, and the strongest of the three legs is a
verification rather than an argument.

**The exit-code collapse is now a measured fact, not a reading of the source.**
In `postgres:16` under `-m 64m`, `rss_wrapper` around a 200 MiB allocation and
the same wrapper around `false` both exit **1**. What separates them is one line
of the container's own `/sys/fs/cgroup/memory.events`: `oom_kill 1` against
`oom_kill 0`. The killed run still reports `maxrss_kib=68228` and a `real` line,
which is why a censored cell needs a *state* and not a missing number — the
number is there, and it is a bound on a peak the process never reached.

**The gate `19.11` is held behind could not be asked.** Its precondition is *a
`--figure reserve --alone` sitting completes with no killed leg*; under fail-fast
a sitting with a killed leg does not complete, so the two clauses were one and
the gate checked nothing. It is now answered in the run's banner, in `raw.json`
and in the exit code.

**The headroom argument is the weak leg and is not leaned on.** The thin cells
after `19.19` are 1 GiB at 11.6% and 1.5 GiB at 10.3% against a 20% criterion —
close rather than doomed. A slice resting on "a leg will probably die" would have
rested on a guess; the two above rest on the tree and on a container.
