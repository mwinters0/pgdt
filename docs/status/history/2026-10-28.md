# 2026-10-28

## 15.18.3's premise, reviewed: 15.18.4 earned ahead of it

With the maintainer, closing the "Decisions worth another look" entry that
stopped 15.18.3 at its premise. Two terms it was to set a charge on have no
bound D122 accepts, and both are now `gz-seek`'s, as **15.18.4**, before
15.18.3 (`KD149`, `KD150`). 15.18.3's row is otherwise unchanged.

- **A taken BGZF chunk's decode** records every block of its members
  (`crates/gz-seek/src/chunk.rs`, `Running::event`), and its guess, read off
  the chain (`speculate/members.rs`), counts none. At ten bits an empty fixed
  block and 24 bytes an event, a 1 MiB chunk holds about 1.7 M events, some
  40 MB. That is bounded by the chunk's bytes, at about forty times them, so
  it is the charge D122 refuses.
- **The bound is by construction, not a cut.** Inside a BGZF member
  `Placement::owes_block` is false and `Parallel::cut_at_static` skips its
  statics (D116, D118). So its block events, statics and per-block pieces
  serve nothing past moving the frontier's `at`, for which member
  granularity does. Dropping them leaves three events and one piece a member,
  and the chain read counts those against `EVENTS_A_GUESS`. Rejected:
  - handing back nothing past an event bound, which wastes the decode and
    sends the chunk to the frontier serially;
  - stopping at the last boundary under the bound, as `cut_at_static`
    truncates, which is still a cut where nothing need be recorded.
- **A member's header** holds every byte (`member.rs`, `Header::push`), only
  for `FHCRC`. Its one reader, `bgzf_member_len`, reads 18 bytes. Keeping
  those and a running CRC-32 bounds it at every site, the engine's serial
  pass and unit decodes included, so no separate `KD<k>` is wanted for the
  serial pass. A speculation also holds its `Input` from the header's start,
  checking its compressed bound only in `Phase::Deflate`, so 15.18.4 checks it
  in `Phase::Header` too.
- **15.18.3's opening reading is taken and stands**, at `ef5f5d4b` on both
  builds: `runs/p15.18.3/reading.sh`, `reading.txt`, `secant.txt`. Its leg,
  `repeated_gz`, holds no BGZF member and no long header, so 15.18.4 moves
  nothing it read. It bears `KD148` out: with each allocator's retention off,
  a worker's increment on the limit leg falls by about one freed decode
  buffer on both.

## 15.18.3's freed decode buffer, reviewed: pooled, not charged

With the maintainer, closing the "Decisions worth another look" entry that
charged one freed decode buffer a task rather than pooling the buffers. The
call is reversed, inside 15.18.3's row, which allowed either; the spec is
unchanged, and D124 and D117 are rewritten to the pool.

- **The term is the charge's one fit.** Every other term is a bound on what
  the code writes; this one is what mimalloc and glibc kept on one leg
  (`runs/p15.18.3/secant.txt`: each worker's increment falls 14.2 and
  18.7 MiB with retention off, a buffer being `SPECULATION_LIMIT`). The
  buffer is made on a worker (`chunk.rs`, `ChunkDecode::begin`) and freed on
  the caller's thread when the frontier is done serving it (`parallel.rs`,
  `Frontier::Serving`), so how many an allocator keeps is timing, not code.
- **A pool is not a rework of the handoff.** The frontier already makes each
  decode (`parallel/chain.rs`, `Chain::extend`), handing its window in, and
  takes its plaintext back in `Decoded.plain`. The finished buffer goes back
  in the same way, handed over at dispatch, not at queueing, or every queued
  decode holds one. Idle buffers and live decodes together stay under the
  live peak the charge already covers, so the freed term goes.
- **Only the plaintext buffer is pooled.** It is the one the reading names
  and is never outgrown; a decode's events and pieces are small and capped
  by its guess, and a speculation's input and sinks stay `KD148`'s.
- **Rejected: an anonymous mapping per buffer**, freed to the kernel on drop.
  It drops the allocator dependence without a pool, but faults and zeroes a
  limit's worth on every decode and needs `unsafe` or a dependency in
  `gz-seek`.

## D118's walk, reviewed

With the maintainer, closing the "Decisions worth another look" entry on
D118's walk being most of a level-1 file's serial pass. D118 stands; a
cheaper walk is admitted as `M256`, blocking nothing.

- **The walk does more work than it needs to.** 15.19's profile gives
  `Walk::run` about three fifths of the serial pass over `control_gz_quick`
  and zlib-rs's inflate a third, though the inflate decodes the same symbols
  and writes them too. The walk pays per symbol for an 8-byte, bounds-checked
  `peek` and keeps no bit buffer, so the excess is in the loop, not in
  walking at all.
- **The writer is any default-built zlib-ng or zlib-rs at level 1**, each
  ending its one static block only at a flush or `Z_FINISH`; Fedora's
  zlib-ng-compat writes dynamic blocks there
  (`runs/p15-level1-writers/reading.txt`).
- **A cheaper walk changes no decision**, so it is out-of-band. Rejected:
  another way to a symbol boundary, the engine's bit position where inflate
  stops, which no zlib API reports apart from a stop inside a match, the
  ground D118 already refused.
- **It blocks nothing.** The walk is flat across counts, so neither 15.19's
  gate nor `KD147` reads it, and 15.7.2's record states no throughput. No
  `KD` is filed: the row is the queue, and a `KD` is owned only by a phase.
