## The census's field split was the byte loop, not the census

P7.6 replaced the per-field `std::str::from_utf8` with one `simdutf8` pass per
chunk, which needed a splitter that yields **ranges** rather than slices so a
row can be taken out of the validated `&str`. `copy::split_fields` was
reimplemented on that splitter — `memchr` where it had been
`line.split(|&b| b == DELIMITER)`, a closure invoked per byte — and the only
hot caller of `split_fields` is the array-shape census.

**A whole-file `parse` of the `--arrays --composite` file falls from 17.781 G
user instructions to 2.919 G**, a factor of 6.1, with the cache it writes
byte-identical and its row count unchanged. `map::Builder::on_row`'s self share
falls 87.9–90.2% → 5.96–6.25% across three profiles each, and what is left of
that scan is three SIMD searches: the LF search 52.2%, the field split 20.1%,
the brace pre-filter 14.7%.

**What the decomposition had wrong** was not the share but the attribution.
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "parse-profile", read
`on_row` at 85.6% of that file's `parse` and called the field split behind the
pre-filter the thing a brace-bearing scan pays for the census. The split was
indeed the cost — but as a byte-at-a-time walk, not as census work, so five
sixths of it was never about deciding an array's shape at all. A profile
attributes to the function that ran; only replacing the function says which
half of it was intrinsic.

**Two things change downstream.** P7.7's row — "one field split per row, shared
by the predicate's terms, `push_row`, the census and the decoders" — keeps its
stake for the query path and loses most of the census half of it: what is left
to win by *sharing* the split is the walk, not the split. And
[`measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md)'s "The census on
array-bearing rows" is now wrong by more than any figure this phase has moved.
It was already stale on `copy.rs`, a stale figure obliges no sweep, and 7.12's
pair re-takes every table; until then the published cell is not to be quoted,
which is what [`STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md) says.

**Nothing here amends P7's lever table.** The splitter is not a lever the
profile found beside the phase's list; it is what 7.6's own mechanism required,
and its effect landed on a row the table already names. The spec records what
was believed when it was written, and the stake that moved is recorded here and
in the checklist.

## 7.7 is smaller and narrower than its rows said

Grilling the entry 7.6 left under "Decisions worth another look" found the
question was not whether the census clause had *shrunk*. It was never 7.7's.

**The splitter has three production callers**, and they do not share a pass.
`map::Builder::on_row` runs inside `map_forward`; `Predicate`'s per-term
`field_ranges(..).nth(i)` and `RowBatcher::push_row`'s inline walk run inside
the replay. [`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Query: mapping
and streaming are separate passes", makes that a hard boundary, and sharing one
split across it is the interleaved form recorded there as deliberately
deferred. So the slice row's fourth sharer was unreachable from the moment it
was written, and 7.6 is what made the mismatch visible rather than what caused
it.

**The fourth sharer named, "the decoders", is already discharged.**
`RawRow::decode` takes the range `push_row` computed — that is what 7.6
delivered — so the decoders share the split today.

**What is left is only the predicate's redundant walks, and nothing measures
them.** On a query with no predicate `push_row` splits once and everything
downstream uses that split, so the budget's split-and-walk row is irreducible,
not a prize: the "0.98 µs is 7.7's" line in the spec is an accounting claim
about *ownership*, which is what it was written to settle, and it was read
afterwards as a stake. The removable cost is one extra walk per predicate term.
`rg -- '--where|--filter' scripts/measure.py` returns nothing: no registered
figure passes a predicate at all, so the shape this lever pays off on is absent
from the apparatus.

**Both spec rows were amended and the slice gained a precondition** — a
predicated reading comes before the lever, since the phase's rule is that a row
lands when its *measured* prize is worth the change. Cutting 7.7 instead was
rejected: unmeasured is not zero, a five-term disjunction really does walk the
row five times, and this phase has now been surprised twice by attribution.

## A filter term costs mostly the walk to its field, and the lever is not monotone

`predicate-terms` is the first registered figure to pass a filter. Two
instruments, because a neighbouring session on this machine built throughout
the sitting: the harness's wall figure
([`../../design/measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md), "What a filter
term costs") and a deterministic instruction count beside it
(`runs/measure-7.7.tsv`), which is what 7.6 did and for the same reason.

The same five terms against the control's thirteenth column and against its
first differ by **3.4497 G user instructions, 49.2% of the deep query**, with
five reps agreeing to 0.0012%. A deep term is 0.896 G and a shallow one
0.206 G, so 77% of a deep term is the walk; one field boundary is 57.5 M

**What that settles about 7.7.1 is its sign, not only its size.** One shared
whole-row split is 16 boundaries where the predicate today pays *i* per term:
40% of a five-term deep query, and **5% worse** on a single deep term, 31%

upper bounds rather than typical costs, for the reason the review below sets

## The `predicate-terms` zero-row shape reviewed: it is the lever's worst case

The call was affirmed and its stated reasons were both replaced, which is why
this is written down rather than simply closed.

**The shape's defence is conservatism, not isolation.** `RowBatcher::push_row`
walks the whole row unconditionally — its own contract says the whole row is
walked whatever the projection is — so a row that survives the filter already
pays the whole-row split and sharing it is free. Every cell of the figure is
therefore a *rejected* row, and selectivity is the axis the lever turns on. A
zero-row figure sits at that axis's pessimal end, which makes the +5% and +31%
losses **upper bounds**: at any other selectivity they shrink toward zero while
the −40% many-term win does not. That is a better property than the entry
claimed for itself, and it converts 7.7.1's inheritance from an open trade into
a bounded one.

**The rejected keeps-everything shape was rejected for the wrong reason.** The
notes doc argued an N-term conjunction goes `Unknown` on 1 − 0.98^N of the rows,
which presumes one term per distinct column; `PREDICATE_SHAPES` puts all N terms
on **one** column, so the drop is a flat 2% whatever N is, and an all-true
conjunction does not short-circuit either. The term-count axis was never
threatened. What defeats the alternative is the **depth** axis: it needs two
columns of equal NULL-ness at different offsets, and the control's only NOT NULL
column is `id` at depth 1 — so the depth pair would differ by 2% of the rows
emitted, which dominates where the filter keeps everything. The obstruction is
in the input, not the predicate language, and a NULL-free deep column would
answer it.

**The contended sitting stands as published.** `measurements.md`'s rule that a
figure taken while local work ran is not a figure exists against *unauditable*
numbers; here the per-rep readings are published, the drift is visible in them,
the caveat states the supported precision, and the claim anything cites rests on
the deterministic instrument. 7.12's sweep re-takes it. The reasoning is filed
beside the figure, and `architecture.md`'s "Predicates" now says which of its
two numbers came from which instrument.

## The shared split is lazy, and a second entry point costs more than it saves

Two findings from 7.7.1 that its rows did not predict, both filed in
[`../../design/roadmap-P7.7.1-shared-field-split-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P7.7.1-shared-field-split-notes.md)
and beside the mechanism in
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Predicates".

**The term-count gate 7.7 left open is unnecessary, because the split is
lazy.** 7.7's arithmetic sized an *eager* whole-row split — 16 boundaries
whatever the filter reads — which is where its +5% and +31% losses came from.
`copy::RowSplit` extends only as far as it is asked to, so a row the filter
rejects is never walked past its deepest term and those shapes do not arise.
Measured, the worst rejecting shape is one deep term at **+4.5%**, against
**−39.7%** on the five-term disjunction the lever was proposed for and
**−11.7%** on the same five terms over rows that all survive.

**Adding a second `push_field` call site to `batch.rs` costs the unfiltered
query path 2.4% of its user instructions, whether or not the new site ever
runs.** Keeping a direct-walk `push_row` beside a split-reading one — the
arrangement that looks free — measured 24.65 G → 25.29 G on the 3.00 GiB
control; with the branch to the second entry point present but never taken it
was still 25.29 G, and with the call site deleted 24.70 G. `push_field` stops
being inlined into `push_row`, and `GenericByteViewArray::value` stops being
inlined into `render_field` in the same module. Neither `#[inline]` nor
`#[inline(always)]` recovered it, and a closure shared between two
monomorphized loops cost the same. **7.10 and 7.11 both rework `batch.rs`** and
inherit this: a refactor there that adds a call site to a function on the row
path is not free, and the unfiltered shape is the one to measure it on.

## The shared split's trade holds, and its buffer is bound to its row by discipline

The call was affirmed. Two of the entry's three supporting claims were replaced
and one piece of work was admitted, which is why this is written down rather
than simply closed.

**The entry weighed the trade against the wrong shape.** Its headline said the
cost falls on the unfiltered path, where the readings are +0.4% and +2.9%. The
largest measured loss is +4.5%, on `reject-deep-1` — one term against a late
column, rejecting every row — which is a *filtered* query and an ordinary
ad-hoc one. The notes doc had it right ("the losing shapes are all one term or
none"); the entry compressed it into a claim about the unfiltered path and so
understated its own worst case by a factor of one and a half.

**What makes it a bounded trade is selectivity, and the entry did not say so.**
`push_row` walks the whole row unconditionally, so on a row that survives the
filter the split is shared for nothing; every published loss is measured where
no row survives, which is the pessimal end of that axis. The losses are
therefore upper bounds reached only at zero selectivity, while the −39.7% and
−11.7% wins are not — the same property the `predicate-terms` review found for
that figure's zero-row shape, applied to the lever it sized. In absolute terms
on that instrument the worst loss is 0.155 G against wins of 2.78 G and 3.35 G.

**The regression is already published, which nothing had noticed.** Both losing
shapes are registered figures: `predicate-terms`' first row is
`--where 'v_bool=zzz1'`, which *is* `reject-deep-1`, and `projection-widths`'
first row is `--no-columns` with no filter, which *is* the +2.9% shape. Both
figures name `copy.rs` in `depends` and are stale now, so 7.12's sweep
republishes the cost side without anyone having to remember it. The *winning*
shape has no such cover: `keep-deep-5` — five terms over rows that all
survive — is absent from the apparatus, because `predicate-terms` is
deliberately a zero-row table and the obstruction to a keeps-rows companion is
the control file's lack of a NULL-free deep column.

**"No cheaper form without `unsafe`" was false as stated.** A safe one exists:
`restart` keeps capacity and a block's rows have one width, so from the second
row on, `Vec::push`'s capacity branch and its length store are both
known-redundant, and sizing `ends` once per block to write `ends[n] = end`
against a counter removes them. It is worth 2–3 of the 9 instructions — small,
and not a reversal — so it is filed as something 7.10 inherits rather than
given a slice. What *is* rejected outright, and now beside the mechanism, is a
`shared` flag turning memoization off for a single-term filter: it is a loss on
a rejected row and a win on a kept one, so it trades one selectivity regime for
the other rather than removing a cost.

**`RowSplit` is bound to its row by discipline, which the entry never
examined.** `field(row, i)` and `complete(row)` re-take the row on every call
and nothing checks it is the row the ends were found in, so a missed `restart`
yields in-range indices into the wrong row: wrong fields, wrong comparisons,
wrong rows emitted, no panic. The notes doc uses exactly that failure mode to
justify avoiding `unsafe` — "a wrong index costs an answer, never the process" —
and then leaves the answer unguarded. One production call chain restarts
correctly today; **7.10 and 7.11 both rework `batch.rs`**, which holds it. A
`#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` row length asserted in both accessors costs nothing
in release and turns the class into a `cargo test` failure. Admitted as `M54`,
blocking P7 so it lands ahead of 7.10 rather than after it.

## `M54`: the shared split is bound to its row

`copy::RowSplit` carries a `#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` row length, set by the
first accessor call after a `restart` and asserted by every one after it. A
split read against a row it was not restarted for panics in a debug build and
compiles to nothing in a release one; `copy::tests::a_missed_restart_panics_in_a_debug_build`
puts both accessors through it and then shows a `restart` between the two rows
is all the guard asks for. The mechanism's paragraph is
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "A `RowSplit` is
bound to its row by an assertion, not by a type", which is also where the two
rejected alternatives — `unsafe` on the borrow, and a `RowSplit<'a>` holding the
row — now live.

**A length is the whole check, and its hole is not the shape the mistake
takes.** Two rows of equal length are indistinguishable to it. But a missed
`restart` sits on a row path, so it runs on every row of a block, and a block
whose rows are all one length is not something a real dump or any fixture
produces — the first differing pair fails the run. The alternative that closes
the hole is binding on the row's address as well, which is sound today (every
call site passes `RawRow::bytes()` of one slice, and the validated and unchecked
forms of a row are the same bytes at the same address) and is one refactor away
from a false failure. The guard is worth having because it is unconditional and
cheap, not because it is a proof.

**No figure moves.** The change is `#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` on both sides:
`restart` clears a field that does not exist in a release build, and `bind`
compiles to `let _ = row`. `copy.rs` is a declared path of every figure that
reads a `COPY` row, so `measure.py --stale` reads them red — as it already did
before this change, for 7.6 and 7.7.1. Nothing is acknowledged here: an
acknowledgement spends against a stamp, and these figures are red for reasons an
acknowledgement of this commit would not clear.
