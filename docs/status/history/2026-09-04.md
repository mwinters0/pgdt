## `M51`: what the nine dangling citations meant

`scripts/citations.py` is green. The nine citations `M50` reported were read
one at a time and retargeted; none was deleted, because each was pointing at
something that still exists somewhere.

**Seven of the nine were one sweep's half-repair.** The keystone
([`2026-08-25`](2026-08-25.md)) deleted the phase specs and notes and re-filed
their content into `architecture.md`, and the sweep repointed each citation's
**document** while leaving its **section** naming a heading only the deleted doc
ever had. That shape is why they were invisible: the link resolves, the file
opens, and only a reader who looks for the quoted heading finds it missing.

The dead target is written below with an arrow rather than a comma, because a
citation's punctuation is what the check reads and today's entry is in scope.

The last two rows are the other two causes. **A drained inbox takes its citations
with it**: P13's grilling folded the xz probe numbers into that phase's spec and
deleted the inbox, which is the lifecycle working, but the entry P16 was holding
still named the file. And **a phase spec may rename a section the roadmap
sketched under another name**: `index.rs` cited "Cache: a sparse row index",
which was the roadmap's sketch heading; P7's spec settled the question the other
way and called the section "The sparse row index: not here either". That
citation's surrounding sentence claimed the spec "defines its real shape" — it
does not, it says the index belongs to P16 — so the sentence was corrected with
the pointer.

**Two of the repairs are not heading-for-heading.** Both are recorded here
because a later reader would otherwise read them as sloppy matches:

- The manual's large-objects "why" now points at a **table row** rather than a
  section, in the `` `doc.md`'s "Row name" row `` idiom the tree already uses.
  The question a manual reader is asking there is *why are contents out of
  scope*, and the answer — "decoding the contents stays permanently out of
  scope — opaque bytes belonging to no table" — is a coverage statement, so it
  lives in the compatibility matrix rather than beside a mechanism.
  `architecture.md`'s "Bulk regions: one span kind, three payloads" answers a
  different question, *how* the region is skipped. A row pointer carries no
  comma-plus-quotes, so the citation check does not watch it; that is the cost,
  and it is the same cost every other row pointer in the tree already pays.
- `map.rs`'s "Framing spans" was a bold lead in the deleted P3 material, and no
  section of `architecture.md` inherited that name. What the comment needs
  supported is the classification rule — a bare `SET` or
  `SELECT pg_catalog.set_config(` is `Framing` wherever it appears, from either
  producer — which is stated under "TOC enrichment", the section that owns
  `classify` and `governing_toc`.

**What this does not do** is change any inbox entry's content. The P16 inbox
quotes P13's decode probes and P13's spec says *no document may quote these
numbers*; both inbox entries label them "probes, not figures", which is what
that rule is guarding against, and rewriting an inbox's evidence is not a
citation repair. P16's grilling drains that file and will read the numbers
against the spec then.

## The census's field split was the byte loop, not the census

P7.6 replaced the per-field `std::str::from_utf8` with one `simdutf8` pass per
chunk, which needed a splitter that yields **ranges** rather than slices so a
row can be taken out of the validated `&str`. `copy::split_fields` was
reimplemented on that splitter — `memchr` where it had been
`line.split(|&b| b == DELIMITER)`, a closure invoked per byte — and the only
hot caller of `split_fields` is the array-shape census.

**A whole-file `parse` of the `--arrays --composite` file falls from 17.781 G
user instructions to 2.919 G**, a factor of 6.1, with the cache it writes
byte-identical and its row count unchanged. `map::Builder::on_row`'s self share
falls 87.9–90.2% → 5.96–6.25% across three profiles each, and what is left of
that scan is three SIMD searches: the LF search 52.2%, the field split 20.1%,
the brace pre-filter 14.7%.

**What the decomposition had wrong** was not the share but the attribution.
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "parse-profile", read
`on_row` at 85.6% of that file's `parse` and called the field split behind the
pre-filter the thing a brace-bearing scan pays for the census. The split was
indeed the cost — but as a byte-at-a-time walk, not as census work, so five
sixths of it was never about deciding an array's shape at all. A profile
attributes to the function that ran; only replacing the function says which
half of it was intrinsic.

**Two things change downstream.** P7.7's row — "one field split per row, shared
by the predicate's terms, `push_row`, the census and the decoders" — keeps its
stake for the query path and loses most of the census half of it: what is left
to win by *sharing* the split is the walk, not the split. And
[`measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md)'s "The census on
array-bearing rows" is now wrong by more than any figure this phase has moved.
It was already stale on `copy.rs`, a stale figure obliges no sweep, and 7.12's
pair re-takes every table; until then the published cell is not to be quoted,
which is what [`STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md) says.

**Nothing here amends P7's lever table.** The splitter is not a lever the
profile found beside the phase's list; it is what 7.6's own mechanism required,
and its effect landed on a row the table already names. The spec records what
was believed when it was written, and the stake that moved is recorded here and
in the checklist.
