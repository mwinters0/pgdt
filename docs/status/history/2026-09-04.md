## The census's field split was the byte loop, not the census

P7.6 replaced the per-field `std::str::from_utf8` with one `simdutf8` pass per
chunk, which needed a splitter that yields **ranges** rather than slices so a
row can be taken out of the validated `&str`. `copy::split_fields` was
reimplemented on that splitter — `memchr` where it had been
`line.split(|&b| b == DELIMITER)`, a closure invoked per byte — and the only
hot caller of `split_fields` is the array-shape census.

**A whole-file `parse` of the `--arrays --composite` file falls from 17.781 G
user instructions to 2.919 G**, a factor of 6.1, with the cache it writes
byte-identical and its row count unchanged. `map::Builder::on_row`'s self share
falls 87.9–90.2% → 5.96–6.25% across three profiles each, and what is left of
that scan is three SIMD searches: the LF search 52.2%, the field split 20.1%,
the brace pre-filter 14.7%.

**What the decomposition had wrong** was not the share but the attribution.
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "parse-profile", read
`on_row` at 85.6% of that file's `parse` and called the field split behind the
pre-filter the thing a brace-bearing scan pays for the census. The split was
indeed the cost — but as a byte-at-a-time walk, not as census work, so five
sixths of it was never about deciding an array's shape at all. A profile
attributes to the function that ran; only replacing the function says which
half of it was intrinsic.

**Two things change downstream.** P7.7's row — "one field split per row, shared
by the predicate's terms, `push_row`, the census and the decoders" — keeps its
stake for the query path and loses most of the census half of it: what is left
to win by *sharing* the split is the walk, not the split. And
[`measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md)'s "The census on
array-bearing rows" is now wrong by more than any figure this phase has moved.
It was already stale on `copy.rs`, a stale figure obliges no sweep, and 7.12's
pair re-takes every table; until then the published cell is not to be quoted,
which is what [`STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md) says.

**Nothing here amends P7's lever table.** The splitter is not a lever the
profile found beside the phase's list; it is what 7.6's own mechanism required,
and its effect landed on a row the table already names. The spec records what
was believed when it was written, and the stake that moved is recorded here and
in the checklist.

## The `predicate-terms` zero-row shape reviewed: it is the lever's worst case

The call was affirmed and its stated reasons were both replaced, which is why
this is written down rather than simply closed.

**The shape's defence is conservatism, not isolation.** `RowBatcher::push_row`
walks the whole row unconditionally — its own contract says the whole row is
walked whatever the projection is — so a row that survives the filter already
pays the whole-row split and sharing it is free. Every cell of the figure is
therefore a *rejected* row, and selectivity is the axis the lever turns on. A
zero-row figure sits at that axis's pessimal end, which makes the +5% and +31%
losses **upper bounds**: at any other selectivity they shrink toward zero while
the −40% many-term win does not. That is a better property than the entry
claimed for itself, and it converts 7.7.1's inheritance from an open trade into
a bounded one.

**The rejected keeps-everything shape was rejected for the wrong reason.** The
notes doc argued an N-term conjunction goes `Unknown` on 1 − 0.98^N of the rows,
which presumes one term per distinct column; `PREDICATE_SHAPES` puts all N terms
on **one** column, so the drop is a flat 2% whatever N is, and an all-true
conjunction does not short-circuit either. The term-count axis was never
threatened. What defeats the alternative is the **depth** axis: it needs two
columns of equal NULL-ness at different offsets, and the control's only NOT NULL
column is `id` at depth 1 — so the depth pair would differ by 2% of the rows
emitted, which dominates where the filter keeps everything. The obstruction is
in the input, not the predicate language, and a NULL-free deep column would
answer it.

**The contended sitting stands as published.** `measurements.md`'s rule that a
figure taken while local work ran is not a figure exists against *unauditable*
numbers; here the per-rep readings are published, the drift is visible in them,
the caveat states the supported precision, and the claim anything cites rests on
the deterministic instrument. 7.12's sweep re-takes it. The reasoning is filed
beside the figure, and `architecture.md`'s "Predicates" now says which of its
two numbers came from which instrument.

## The shared split is lazy, and a second entry point costs more than it saves

Two findings from 7.7.1 that its rows did not predict, both filed in
[`../../design/roadmap-P7.7.1-shared-field-split-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P7.7.1-shared-field-split-notes.md)
and beside the mechanism in
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "Predicates".

**The term-count gate 7.7 left open is unnecessary, because the split is
lazy.** 7.7's arithmetic sized an *eager* whole-row split — 16 boundaries
whatever the filter reads — which is where its +5% and +31% losses came from.
`copy::RowSplit` extends only as far as it is asked to, so a row the filter
rejects is never walked past its deepest term and those shapes do not arise.
Measured, the worst rejecting shape is one deep term at **+4.5%**, against
**−39.7%** on the five-term disjunction the lever was proposed for and
**−11.7%** on the same five terms over rows that all survive.

**Adding a second `push_field` call site to `batch.rs` costs the unfiltered
query path 2.4% of its user instructions, whether or not the new site ever
runs.** Keeping a direct-walk `push_row` beside a split-reading one — the
arrangement that looks free — measured 24.65 G → 25.29 G on the 3.00 GiB
control; with the branch to the second entry point present but never taken it
was still 25.29 G, and with the call site deleted 24.70 G. `push_field` stops
being inlined into `push_row`, and `GenericByteViewArray::value` stops being
inlined into `render_field` in the same module. Neither `#[inline]` nor
`#[inline(always)]` recovered it, and a closure shared between two
monomorphized loops cost the same. **7.10 and 7.11 both rework `batch.rs`** and
inherit this: a refactor there that adds a call site to a function on the row
path is not free, and the unfiltered shape is the one to measure it on.

## The shared split's trade holds, and its buffer is bound to its row by discipline

The call was affirmed. Two of the entry's three supporting claims were replaced
and one piece of work was admitted, which is why this is written down rather
than simply closed.

**The entry weighed the trade against the wrong shape.** Its headline said the
cost falls on the unfiltered path, where the readings are +0.4% and +2.9%. The
largest measured loss is +4.5%, on `reject-deep-1` — one term against a late
column, rejecting every row — which is a *filtered* query and an ordinary
ad-hoc one. The notes doc had it right ("the losing shapes are all one term or
none"); the entry compressed it into a claim about the unfiltered path and so
understated its own worst case by a factor of one and a half.

**What makes it a bounded trade is selectivity, and the entry did not say so.**
`push_row` walks the whole row unconditionally, so on a row that survives the
filter the split is shared for nothing; every published loss is measured where
no row survives, which is the pessimal end of that axis. The losses are
therefore upper bounds reached only at zero selectivity, while the −39.7% and
−11.7% wins are not — the same property the `predicate-terms` review found for
that figure's zero-row shape, applied to the lever it sized. In absolute terms
on that instrument the worst loss is 0.155 G against wins of 2.78 G and 3.35 G.

**The regression is already published, which nothing had noticed.** Both losing
shapes are registered figures: `predicate-terms`' first row is
`--where 'v_bool=zzz1'`, which *is* `reject-deep-1`, and `projection-widths`'
first row is `--no-columns` with no filter, which *is* the +2.9% shape. Both
figures name `copy.rs` in `depends` and are stale now, so 7.12's sweep
republishes the cost side without anyone having to remember it. The *winning*
shape has no such cover: `keep-deep-5` — five terms over rows that all
survive — is absent from the apparatus, because `predicate-terms` is
deliberately a zero-row table and the obstruction to a keeps-rows companion is
the control file's lack of a NULL-free deep column.

**"No cheaper form without `unsafe`" was false as stated.** A safe one exists:
`restart` keeps capacity and a block's rows have one width, so from the second
row on, `Vec::push`'s capacity branch and its length store are both
known-redundant, and sizing `ends` once per block to write `ends[n] = end`
against a counter removes them. It is worth 2–3 of the 9 instructions — small,
and not a reversal — so it is filed as something 7.10 inherits rather than
given a slice. What *is* rejected outright, and now beside the mechanism, is a
`shared` flag turning memoization off for a single-term filter: it is a loss on
a rejected row and a win on a kept one, so it trades one selectivity regime for
the other rather than removing a cost.

**`RowSplit` is bound to its row by discipline, which the entry never
examined.** `field(row, i)` and `complete(row)` re-take the row on every call
and nothing checks it is the row the ends were found in, so a missed `restart`
yields in-range indices into the wrong row: wrong fields, wrong comparisons,
wrong rows emitted, no panic. The notes doc uses exactly that failure mode to
justify avoiding `unsafe` — "a wrong index costs an answer, never the process" —
and then leaves the answer unguarded. One production call chain restarts
correctly today; **7.10 and 7.11 both rework `batch.rs`**, which holds it. A
`#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` row length asserted in both accessors costs nothing
in release and turns the class into a `cargo test` failure. Admitted as `M54`,
blocking P7 so it lands ahead of 7.10 rather than after it.

## `M54`: the shared split is bound to its row

`copy::RowSplit` carries a `#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` row length, set by the
first accessor call after a `restart` and asserted by every one after it. A
split read against a row it was not restarted for panics in a debug build and
compiles to nothing in a release one; `copy::tests::a_missed_restart_panics_in_a_debug_build`
puts both accessors through it and then shows a `restart` between the two rows
is all the guard asks for. The mechanism's paragraph is
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "A `RowSplit` is
bound to its row by an assertion, not by a type", which is also where the two
rejected alternatives — `unsafe` on the borrow, and a `RowSplit<'a>` holding the
row — now live.

**A length is the whole check, and its hole is not the shape the mistake
takes.** Two rows of equal length are indistinguishable to it. But a missed
`restart` sits on a row path, so it runs on every row of a block, and a block
whose rows are all one length is not something a real dump or any fixture
produces — the first differing pair fails the run. The alternative that closes
the hole is binding on the row's address as well, which is sound today (every
call site passes `RawRow::bytes()` of one slice, and the validated and unchecked
forms of a row are the same bytes at the same address) and is one refactor away
from a false failure. The guard is worth having because it is unconditional and
cheap, not because it is a proof.

**No figure moves.** The change is `#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` on both sides:
`restart` clears a field that does not exist in a release build, and `bind`
compiles to `let _ = row`. `copy.rs` is a declared path of every figure that
reads a `COPY` row, so `measure.py --stale` reads them red — as it already did
before this change, for 7.6 and 7.7.1. Nothing is acknowledged here: an
acknowledgement spends against a stamp, and these figures are red for reasons an
acknowledgement of this commit would not clear.

## `KD9`'s remainder is P8's, and the deferral rested on a false premise

**Two claims about the mechanism were sharpened while checking it.** The paren

`pgdq parse|query --chunk-size` stays, on both commands. The call was flagged
because P7's spec says the chunk-size lever's work is "measuring the right
defaults per device class rather than adding a knob", and 7.8.1 added a knob in
order to do the measuring. Three things decided it, and the first two reverse
the reasoning the slice itself gave.

**The instrument argument does not hold.** 7.8.1 argued a flag was the
better-provenanced instrument than a binary built per size, whose failure mode
is six identical binaries and a flat table. That failure mode is already closed
here twice over: the harness builds `pgdq-before-throttle` itself in a
temporary git worktree, and the `allocator` figure's three legs are
interrogated with `pgdq --version` before being timed. What the flag actually
saved is teaching `--version` to report the compiled chunk size — real, and
small. So the flag is not justified as a necessary instrument.

**Removal would delete the figure, not just its command shape.** A figure whose
regeneration command is gone is deleted rather than kept, and the flag *is*
that command. Removing it would take the published evidence that
`DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE = 1 MiB` is right — evidence the constant's own doc comment
and the manual both cite, and which cost a six-size, three-regime, nine-rep
sitting. Against that, the flag's operator value is thin but not zero: the null
result is scoped to the three device classes this project owns, and a lever
worth nothing on all of them is what someone on unlike hardware needs in order
to find out it is worth something there. Refusing values above the 8 MiB pool
ceiling was considered and rejected — it would delete the figure's 16 MiB row,
which is the row that shows the cliff.

**The 8 MiB cliff is a size proxy, not a property of the read path**, and
7.8.1 called it a property. `POOL_MAX_BYTES` exists to keep `attach_text`'s
coalesced span read — one allocation, once per map — out of a four-slot pool. A
chunk buffer at the configured size is the opposite kind of thing, and the pool
cannot tell them apart by length, so a deliberately large chunk loses pooling
as collateral: the caller gets the memory *and* the `calloc`. "The remedy is
already the user's" was circular, since the hazard is the user's only because
the flag put it there. The remedy is still real and still stated in `--help`,
so this is not a register entry; the fix — pool a buffer whose length is the
configured `chunk_size`, keep the ceiling for everything else — is admitted as
`M55`, with `Blocks` empty. It moves no published number, because every figure
is taken at the default, and `io.rs` is in the `chunk-size` figure's `depends`,
so landing it re-takes that one figure. Its cost is honest: `POOL_SLOTS` is 4,
so a 16 MiB chunk could retain up to 64 MiB against the flat ~9 MiB this design
advertises.

**`DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE` is public for callers, not for the CLI.** The slice
exported it so `scan_options` could spell its `unwrap_or`, which needed no
export. It stays public because `ScanOptions::default().chunk_size` builds a
whole struct to read one number and is not a constant expression; the doc
comment now says so, and the CLI's use of it is incidental.

## `needs_quote` is admitted as a lever, and it is a `contains` rather than a second walk

`nested::needs_quote` is the largest single symbol in a typed
`--arrays --composite` query at **14.09%** of the run
(`runs/profile-query-typed-arrays-after79.txt`, taken at 7.9). What costs that
is not the number of walks over a token but what each byte costs inside one:
**10.10% of the whole run is `memchr_naive`**, inlined through `slice_contains`
→ `contains<u8>`, which is `syntax.force_quote.contains(&c)`. `force_quote` is
a `&'static [u8]` of four to six bytes, so every byte of every token pays a
linear scan of it. The remaining 1.99% is the iterator advance.

So the lever is `Syntax::force_quote`'s **representation**, not the scanner's
control flow, and it is `7.14`. A `const` 256-bit set tests one indexed bit per
byte, leaves `needs_quote`'s truth table identical by construction, and needs
no change to `scan_token`'s strictness rule. There are exactly three `Syntax`
values and all are `const`, so the set is built at compile time.

**It reaches both directions at once**, which is the half 7.9's filing had
wrong. That entry said the render half "is the CLI's … and no library change
removes it". `push_token` is library code in `nested.rs`, and the profile puts
4.82% of the run inside it (`→ render_braces`, 4.66%) against 5.28% under
`scan_token`. What the CLI decides is *whether* rendering runs, not what a byte
costs while it does.

**The fusion is not admitted.** 7.9's entry proposed a byte-classification
table fusing `scan_token`'s terminator walk with `needs_quote`'s second walk;
its prize is whatever `7.14` leaves, and nobody has measured that. Admitting it
now would commit the phase to a scanner rework sized against a number about to
move. `7.14`'s row carries the profile re-read, and the question is re-asked
there or not at all.

Two limits on the stake, recorded so the before/after is read against them:
14.09% is the query shape most favourable to this lever, and the same profile
puts `print_batch` at 59.1% of that run, so the library-only share is a
different number from the whole-query one.

## An unattended loop cannot satisfy P7's own admission rule

P7's spec says a lever the profile finds and the lever table does not name "is
admitted … by amending this table", and warns that filing every unlisted
finding forward "would really be a way of not acting on the evidence the phase
exists to gather". `.claude/skills/go/SKILL.md` requires the phase spec to be
left **untouched**. Both rules cannot hold at once, so the 7.9 session's choice
to file rather than amend was the process working as configured, not a lapse.

The resolution is to leave both rules as they are and write the route down:
filing to "Decisions worth another look" is how an unattended session
discharges that paragraph. The reasoning is beside the paragraph it governs, in
[`../../design/roadmap-P7-scan-performance.md`](../../design/roadmap-P7-scan-performance.md).
The instance above is why — the review that admitted the lever also caught the
two errors in the entry that proposed it, which an auto-admitting session would
have carried into a rework.

## `7.10` splits at the seam its own row named, and the builder's stake halves

The slice row read "scalar decode and the typed column build, split by the
profile into a `decode.rs` half and a builder-append half". The two halves are
two review cycles, and the decision to land only the first is a `7.10.1`.

**The seam is the confidence, not the scope.** The decode half is four pure
functions whose previous implementations can be kept verbatim as an oracle, so
the claim "no behaviour change" is asserted mechanically — `decode.rs`'s
`differential` tests, over a free-form corpus and a per-decoder shaped one, with
each test checked against a mutation of the function it covers. The build half
edits `batch.rs`, which `7.6`, `7.7.1` and `7.9` have each already reworked, and
has no such oracle. Bundling them would have forced one review to accept both at
the decode half's confidence.

**The split moves `7.11`'s gate, which is its one consequence outside the slice
table.** The viewing builder is conditional on the builder-append half pricing
the Arrow build above 1 µs/row on the arrays file, so that row now names
`7.10.1`.

**And the gate is nearer than it was.** `append_typed` was sized at 1.66 µs a
row and the budget re-read after this slice puts it at **0.84** — most of that
bucket was three `malloc`/`free` pairs per row belonging to the decoders it
calls, not to the Arrow appends. The re-read is
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "The library's
own per-row budget", and it reverses a claim that stood there since the
decomposition: the builder is no longer the largest library bucket in typed
mode, the unescape is, in both modes.

**`decode.rs` was a staleness edge nothing declared** — the third instance of
the shape `READ` and `PREDICATE` record, and the first found by a slice that
edited the file rather than by an audit. Every figure that runs a typed query
pays a per-type decoder on every scalar column, so a change taking a typed
control query down 4.55% would have read green against all four of them.
`DECODE` now exists in `scripts/measure.py`. The figures it does not reach were
each checked rather than assumed, and the reasons are in
[`../../design/roadmap-P7.10-scalar-decode-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P7.10-scalar-decode-notes.md).

`7.9`'s notes doc is corrected in the same change: it still said the render half
"is the CLI's … and no library change removes it", which the entry above
refuted when it admitted `7.14`. The refutation was recorded here and not there,
so the doc a reader of that slice meets went on asserting it.

## The render-back's per-byte `format!` is admitted as a lever

`decode::render_bytea` and `decode::render_uuid` get a row in P7's lever table.
The filing entry proposed it and reserved the amendment to a review, which is
the route the spec prescribes for an unattended session; the review admits it,
and found two things the filing did not carry.

**The shares were re-derived rather than transcribed**, from the committed
profile the filing cited (`perf report -i runs/profile-710-after-typed.data
--children -g none`). `render_field` **60.01%**, `render_bytea` **27.34%**,
`render_uuid` **10.41%**, all inclusive, all as filed.

**The number that makes the case was not in the entry.** Between them sits
`alloc::fmt::format::format_inner` at **42.68%** — the largest single bucket in
the run — of which these two are 37.75. For scale, `poll_next`, the library's
entire batch stream and everything an embedder pays, is **27.74%**. Two
functions rendering values back to text cost more than the whole extraction
pipeline they invert. The flat shares say why the fix is contained rather than
diffuse: `render_bytea`'s *self* time is 1.89%, and the rest is `core::fmt`
machinery (`format_inner`, `fmt::write`, `String::write_str`, `pad_integral`,
`LowerHex::fmt`) plus the allocator traffic it generates, which is about a fifth
of the run flat. Per row of the control, `render_bytea` does 64
`format!`-and-allocate calls and `render_uuid` 21 — against 16 `String`s for the
whole rest of `render_field`, so **~84% of the render path's allocation traffic
is these two functions**.

**The row states two limits, on 7.14's precedent.** The shares are a property of
the control's 64-byte `bytea` and 36-character `uuid` columns — 3.3% of a
3,956-byte row returning 27.34% of the time — and both symbols are absent from
`runs/profile-710-after-strings.txt` entirely. So the prize exists only where a
caller asks for typed values and renders them back to text; no `parse` figure
moves, and an embedder sees none of it.

**The entry's stated difference from 7.14 does not bear on admission.** It
called this row's render-only reach the distinguishing fact. But 7.14's
admission rests on "library code on a timed path" — bidirectionality was a
*correction of a filing error* in that entry, not the criterion. What does
distinguish this row is the beneficiary, which is now in the spec.

**The `render_field` sink is not admitted.** `render_field` returns a `String`
per field and `print_batch` collects a `Vec<String>` per row; a
`render_field_into(&mut String)` would remove 16 allocations a row across all
columns rather than two. It is refused here for the reason 7.14 refused the
byte-classification fusion: at 85 per-byte allocations a row against 16
per-field ones, its prize is whatever this row leaves, and nobody has measured
that. Admitting it now would size a public-API change against a number about to
move. The question is re-asked when this row's profile is re-read, or not at
all.

**Two facts for whoever builds it.** `predicate.rs` canonicalises a literal
through both functions (`comparison_form`), once per term per query rather than
per row — not part of the stake, but inside the blast radius. And the
before/after instrument already exists and is free: `benches/decoders.rs` has
`uuid` and `bytea` groups benching exactly these two, and neither feeds a
registered figure — `nested-decode-micro` runs `cargo bench -- nested` only — so
this row owes no `measurements.md` re-take. 7.10's `prior_shape`/`differential`
harness in `decode.rs` is the ready equivalence pattern.

`architecture.md`'s `query-profile` section already recorded the family finding
— that `core::fmt` plus its allocator traffic is about a fifth of a typed
profile. What is new is the attribution to two named library functions, and that
section is what a fold-in re-reads: its heading states the measured finding
"the CLI's render-back is the largest bucket, not the decode", which this row is
aimed squarely at.

## The builder half of the typed row is 52 ns, and 7.11's gate has no reading on the file it names

`7.10.1` was the second half of the lever the spec sizes at "2.61 µs/row of the
library's 4.06 — `append_typed` 1.66 and `decode_field` 0.95". `7.10` took the
decode half and the budget re-read then put `append_typed` at 0.84 µs. This
slice splits what is left, by walking each profile sample's stack through the
inline frames and asking whether an `arrow-*` frame sits between the leaf and
`append_typed`: **0.68 µs of decoders, 0.06 µs of dispatch, 0.052 µs of Arrow
appends.**

So the builder-append half is **1.6% of a typed library row**, and the phase's
largest-looking remaining row was never the builder at all — it was the
allocations `7.10` removed from the decoders underneath it. The row is
discharged by a reading. The one obvious candidate inside it, pre-sizing every
`ColumnBuilder` to `max_rows` instead of `::new()`, is priced at under 6 ns a
row by the same profile and refused: it would also make a one-row batch
allocate 8192 slots per column.

**`7.11`'s gate names the arrays file, and the arrays file has no
`List<Utf8View>` in it.** The registered `arrays` input's two array columns are
`integer[]`, chosen so that element count is the only variable
(`generate_perf_data.py`, `ARRAY_COLUMNS`), and no committed input carries a
text array. The gate as written therefore reads zero for a reason that is about
the apparatus rather than about the lever, which is not an answer.

The `List` leg was read on a purpose-built `runs/` input instead — the
`--arrays` shape with `text[]` columns, 50 elements a row, every element 21
bytes. That width is the load-bearing choice and it is deliberately generous:
`arrow`'s `make_view` inlines a value of 12 bytes or fewer into the view itself,
so below that threshold a copy and a view are the same instructions and the
viewing builder's prize is exactly zero. Isolated with `--column`, one
50-element `text[]` builds its `List<Utf8View>` in **0.383 µs a row — 7.7 ns an
element**, and what `7.11` would win is the copy-to-view difference, at most
~4.7 ns an element or **0.237 µs of an 11.08 µs row**, before any of the
recursive chunk-retention and block-invalidation machinery the change needs at
every nesting level.

**What changed in the plan is `7.11`.** It was specified as a build conditional
on a reading; the reading is in and the slice is now a refusal, which is a
delivered row on this phase's own terms.

**One thing corroborated on the way past.** On that same isolated run
`nested::decode_array` is 3.88 µs a row against the build's 0.383, and
`memchr_naive` — `Syntax::force_quote`'s linear `contains`, which is `7.14`'s
row — is **15.3%** of the run against the 14.1% the `--arrays --composite` file
gives. Nothing about `7.14` changes; it is now known not to be a property of the
composite column.

## `7.14` runs before `7.12`, on `7.15`'s reason

`STATUS.md`'s P7 header said "All three orderings that bind are discharged …
What remains is unordered". Both halves were wrong. The spec's third binding

## `7.11`'s gate is amended to be on the prize, and its refusal gains a registered leg

Review of the `7.11` entry under `STATUS.md`'s "Decisions worth another look".
The entry asked whether the gate should be re-read on a registered input, since
the `text[]` file it was read on is a `runs/` artifact whose element width was
chosen rather than measured. Three things came out of it, and the entry's own
framing was the narrowest of them.

**The gate was on the wrong quantity.** The row read "the `List<Utf8View>`
build above 1 µs/row … the smallest prize this table can honestly claim", and
the build is not the prize: `7.11` replaces a copy with a view write, so what it
wins is the build *minus* that write. `nested-decode-micro` floors one
`append_view_unchecked` at 2.96 ns of a 7.66 ns element build, so a build
reading of exactly 1 µs/row would have licensed a change worth ≤0.62 µs/row —
below the floor the gate was chosen to be. The row is amended to gate on the
prize. The amendment is loose in the safe direction and does not reopen the
refusal: it strengthens it, from 38% of the threshold to **24%**. It also moves
the sensitivity the entry was anxious about — the prize reaches 1 µs/row at
about 210 elements of this width, where the build reaches it at 130.

**One leg of the refusal was registered all along.** `batch::append_nested`'s
`ColumnBuilder::Utf8View` arm is a single `append_value`, and it serves a
`Struct`'s fields exactly as it serves a `List`'s elements — which the spec's
own "recursively at every level of `List` and `Struct` nesting" already said,
while the lever row and the checklist both described `7.11` as `List`-only. The
registered `--arrays --composite` file's `perf_comp AS (a integer, b text)` is a
`Struct{Int32, Utf8View}` whose `b` is a quoted-but-unescaped lorem token, so
`scan_quoted` hands it back `Cow::Borrowed` — the eligible shape. That file's
whole Arrow-append budget is 0.19 µs a row across nineteen columns, which bounds
the `Struct` leg below a fifth of the gate on committed bytes. Only the `List`
leg ever needed a file the tree does not have, so no `generate_perf_data.py`
flag and no figure is owed.

**The adjacent lever was weighed and left where `7.9` put it.**
`decode_record` and `decode_range` still `Cow::into_owned` every field, on the
same `scan_quoted` the array borrows through. It fails the same test `7.15`
applies to the `render_field` sink and `7.14` to the terminator fusion — its
prize is whatever the landed rows leave, which nobody has measured — and the
figures argue the same way: `record_2/decode` fell 190 → 114 ns when the array
borrowed and the record did not, because the win was `scan_quoted` sizing a
quoted token rather than the final `String`, and the whole composite column is
+0.98 µs a row against the two array columns' +13.21. Filed as a rejected
alternative beside the nested literal codec; no slice, no ledger row.

Two smaller calls settled in the same review.

**The `text[]` input stays a `runs/` artifact.** A `generate_perf_data.py
--text-arrays` flag was weighed and refused: the notes doc specifies the shape
completely, the reading is discharged, and it published no number, so there is no
command to preserve. A registered figure for it would be worse — every sweep
paying an hour a time for a closed question. Filed beside the specification it
governs.

**The spec's ordering paragraph is corrected to four.** `d83ece1` left it
knowingly at "Three orderings bind, and they are the whole of it" while its own
checklist named a fourth, on the grounds that a spec amendment is the
maintainer's; it was never filed under "Decisions worth another look", so nothing
would have surfaced it. It is a sentence known false on the day it was written,
which [`../../process.md`](../../process.md) corrects in the change that
falsifies it rather than in a later slice, and the reasoning already existed one
section above.

## A criterion baseline taken from a worktree measured the wrong thing

`7.14`'s before/after was first taken by building the old revision in a
`git worktree` with `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` pointed back at the main `target/`, so
both baselines landed in one `target/criterion` and the comparison needed no
edit to the tree under review. That reads as hygiene and is the opposite: it is
the same source built at a different path, which is
[`../../design/measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md), "Two builds of
one source can differ by layout", with nothing announcing itself. Taken that
way, `nested-decode-micro`'s three decode rows read **−1.2%, 0% and −3.6%**
against a whole-query instruction count that was already saying **−12.5%**.

**The tell was a control.** `text_copy/array_50_len` is `String::from` over 601
bytes and neither revision touches it; it moved **+70%**. Re-taken by checking
the old file into the same tree and running both legs from it, the same three
rows read −27.5%, −25.4% and −18.6%, which is what the instruction count says
independently.

What makes this worth a rule rather than a note is the direction: the
contaminated reading was **conservative**, so it looked exactly like an honest
refusal — a lever that profiled well and benched flat. A session that trusted it
would have filed `7.14` as measured-and-not-worth-it and moved on. The rule is
beside the layout one it is a corollary of; a bench group carrying a control it
cannot move is what makes the failure visible at all.

## The per-value renderers are admitted as one lever, and the sink comes with them

`7.15`'s filing entry asked whether P7 admits the date and time renderers'
`core::fmt` cost as a row. The review admits it — as **one** row covering both
the renderers and the `render_field_into` sink `7.15` refused, which is
`7.16`. Four things decided it, and the first is that this was not a reversal.

**The re-ask was scheduled, and its condition had fired.** `7.15`'s spec row
refuses the sink because "its prize is whatever this row leaves, which nobody
has measured", and its filing entry set the trigger explicitly: *the question is
re-asked when this row's profile is re-read, or not at all*. `7.15`'s own notes
doc re-read that profile. So the number the refusal named as missing was taken
by the slice that wrote the refusal, three paragraphs further down the same
document, and neither half referenced the other. Admitting the row is the
refusal's own re-ask coming due on the terms the spec set, not a decision
changing its mind.

**The two halves are one lever because neither collects the prize alone.**
`render_field` returns `Option<String>` per field and each date/time renderer
builds its own `String` internally before returning it —
`render_timestamp_micros` allocates three (`format_hms_frac`'s
`format!("{micros:06}")`, its assembly `format!`, and the outer one). A sink
alone therefore still pays every internal allocation and adds a copy; hand-rolled
zero-padding alone removes `pad_integral` and still allocates per value. Only
both together reach the residual, and they share one review question — does the
render path still produce identical text — and one public-signature change.

**The share is the realistic case, not the favourable one, which is the
opposite of `7.15`'s.** `7.15`'s row had to state two limits because its 37.75%
was a property of the control's 64-byte `bytea` and 36-character `uuid`. This
row needs no such caveat: `generate_perf_data.py` draws the microsecond
component of all three date/time columns **uniformly**, deliberately and with
its reasoning written down — koji's `task.create_time` holds no value with an
empty fraction, and 9.9% end in a zero digit, which is about what a uniform draw
gives. So nearly every timestamp renders a six-digit fraction and takes
`format_hms_frac`'s trailing-zero trim, and four of the control's sixteen
columns are date/time. The beneficiary limit `7.15` states is inherited
unchanged: zero under `--schema-mode strings`, zero for an embedder taking
`RecordBatch`es, and no `parse` figure moves.

**The stake is the profile share, and one number in it does not survive.** The
row is admitted on `render_timestamp_micros` at **11.81%** of a typed control
profile with `render_decimal`, `render_f64`, `render_time64_micros` and
`render_date32` each under 3% — a library-path profile share, which is the kind
of number this table already accepts, and not one of the CLI subtractions that
had to be corrected. What does **not** carry over is the **16 allocations a
row** that both `7.15`'s row and the sink's refusal quote for the rest of
`render_field`: by inspection the four date/time columns alone account for nine
of them, which would leave seven for the other twelve columns. Either the 16
counted only `render_field`'s returned `String`s and missed the renderers'
internal temporaries, or it is wrong. It is quoted in a refusal that is now
being lifted, so `7.16` re-derives it rather than inheriting it — that is a
number to take, not an evidence slice to buy.

**Declining had no destination, which is why it was the weakest option.** No
sketched phase owns the CLI render path, and P6 is the one phase structurally
excluded from owning it, since an embedder never pays this cost. Declining would
have left a 15%-of-a-typed-query finding with a known remedy as a roadmap
"Future" item at best; P7 is the phase about exactly this.

**One slice, and the seam this phase keeps meeting does not cut here.** The five
earned `<N>.<M>.<K>` splits were all at *evidence then mechanism* — an
instrument paired with the lever it sizes. Both halves of `7.16` are mechanism,
and the review question is genuinely single: does the render path emit identical
text. Splitting would land a sink whose measured prize is zero until the
renderers follow, which inverts the ordering the seam exists to enforce.
`predicate.rs`'s `comparison_form` calls these renderers once per term per query
rather than per row — inside the blast radius, not in the stake, exactly as it
was for `7.15`.

**The row stops at date and time.** `render_decimal` and `render_f64` are in the
residual at under 3% each and are out of scope, named in the row so a later
session does not read the omission as an oversight. They are different problems:
`format_shortest`'s float algorithm and `numeric` digit-string surgery, each
owing its own correctness argument against `float8out`/`numeric_out`. Either is
worth its own row with its own stake, or neither is.

**The ordering rule has fired three times and is restated as a property.** Any
