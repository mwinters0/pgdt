# 2026-08-30

## The sweep pair, and the resolution floor it forced

Two full `scripts/measure.py --all` sweeps ran detached against `4c2c3e7`, back
to back with a 120 s settle — `runs/measure-20260830T025850` and
`runs/measure-20260830T032507`, both exit 0, 48 readings each, no figure failed
and neither `tables.md` was marked unpublishable. Sweep 1 is what
`measurements.md` carries; `session-drift` is derived across the pair. Both
`raw.json` files are kept.

**The pair is about two minutes apart, where the published pair was two hours**,
and that turns out to be a term in the figure rather than a detail of how it was
taken: median absolute drift is **0.4%** here against **5.6%** there, largest
7.3% against 8.5%. The cold `INSERT` scan is the clearest case — it drifted
8.1% at two hours and 0.3% at two minutes, so the reading that was read as
"a cold scan with real CPU in it drifts like a warm one" is really "…given
enough elapsed time". The two-hour number stays the one to plan against, since
that is what comparing across sessions actually costs.

**The two sweeps looked like they failed different witnesses, and the real
finding is that the comparison was below the instrument's resolution.** Sweep 1's
warm `dd` floor is flat (0.311 s on the control file against the doc's 0.309)
but its last two figures ran under a CPU episode the counters saw — ≤12% and
≤9% machine-busy against ≤5% everywhere else in both sweeps, under the armed
gate's 15, with `map-only`'s reps spread 7–11%. Sweep 2 never exceeded 4.7%
machine-busy and its reps are tight throughout, but its warm `control` floor
read 0.291–0.294 s, 5.5% below the standing one.

That 5.5% was briefly read as disqualifying, and it is not. The floor witnesses
bandwidth contention, and contention makes a floor **slower**; sweep 2's is
faster, in the direction contention cannot produce, and 5.5% sits inside the
8.5% envelope `session-drift` itself measures. So the check now reads the floor
**directionally and inside a tolerance**, and both sweeps pass it. What
separates them is then nothing: every table they disagree on disagrees by less
than the apparatus resolves, so re-stamping from one to the other would move
numbers without moving information, and the doc keeps the sweep already folded
in.

**The general rule this produced.** `measurements.md` now states a resolution
floor per regime — a warm absolute across sessions resolves to ~8%, a cold
device-bound one to ~0.5%, a cross-file per-row difference to ~0.5 µs/row — and
requires a sub-floor move to be written up as *reproduces* rather than as a
change. The numbers are read off `session-drift` rather than asserted. Every
one of them was already in the doc; what was missing was a rule saying when a
move is too small to mean anything, and its absence is what turned a
below-resolution difference into an argument about which sweep to publish. The
symmetric version of the floor check was weighed and refused, beside the
mechanism in `measurements.md`.

**What moved past drift.** Most figures reproduce the previous stamp within 2%.
The exceptions worth knowing: the census on brace-free rows reads +8% (0.038 s,
47 ns/row) against +7%, both inside the range earlier sweeps read; the
`INSERT`-to-`COPY` per-byte ratio reads 14.4× against 14.6×, and the four
witnessed-quiet sweeps now span 14.4–14.9×; the 601-byte `String::from` control
in the nested micro read 42 and 41 ns in this pair against 24 ns before, which
alone swings that row's `÷ copy` from 221× to 122× while `decode` moves 2% — so
that column is written up as an order of magnitude rather than a figure.

**The per-block quadratic's derived split no longer resolves.** `map-only` minus
the throttled `parse` at 4000 blocks is the cache's share, and it is the
difference of two ~21 s readings taken in different figures: 0.76 s at the
previous stamp, 0.17 s in sweep 1, 1.41 s in sweep 2. The doc says "under a
second" and says why, rather than quoting a number the instrument cannot
resolve.

## A figure's number in a live spec

`roadmap-P5-pushdown.md` repeated `nested-end-to-end`'s per-row number and
`cross-file-floor`'s, while neither figure's `quoted_by` named it — so a sweep

resolution that leaves the spec/notes split intact. The rule generalises: an
open spec cites a figure by id, never by value.

## An acknowledgement cannot excuse the commit that carries it

`measure.ACKNOWLEDGED` lived in `scripts/measure.py`, which `session-drift`
declares — so the commit that *adds* an acknowledgement marks that figure stale
by existing, and no entry can name its own sha. Chasing it with a follow-up
commit only moves the problem by one. The register therefore moves to
`scripts/acknowledged.py`, which no figure declares, after which an ack-only
commit touches no measured path and the sequence terminates. This is `M27`; it
is a move plus an import, and it changes none of the register's four
properties. Its own commit was the last turn of the regress, and the entry
closing it is the first acknowledgement here that adds no diff to a measured
path.

## `infinity` and `NaN` are ordered values, and the ordering register said otherwise

`P5.6`'s call to raise `Error::FieldDecode` when an ordering filter meets a
value that does not decode was reviewed and reversed. The reasoning it rested
on — that such a value is the file contradicting its own DDL — does not hold
for either example it cited.

Verified against the live koji replica (`sudo nerdctl exec koji-pg psql`), and
recorded as the evidence under I34, which `P5.8` wrote:

| comparison | server |
|---|---|
| `'infinity'::date > '2020-01-01'::date` | true |
| `'2020-01-01'::date > '-infinity'::date` | true |
| `'NaN'::numeric > 5::numeric` | true |
| `'NaN'::numeric = 'NaN'::numeric` | true |
| `'NaN'::float8 > 'infinity'::float8` | true |
| `'NaN'::numeric(5,2)` | casts, yields `NaN` |

So these are legal values of their declared types with a total order, not
sentinels and not corruption. What cannot hold them is Arrow — `Date32` has no
infinity, `Decimal128` no NaN.

Behaviour found in the shipped tree, against `fixtures/17/types/default.sql`
whose `t_date` holds both infinities:

- `query --table t_date` errors — the *build* path, and this predates `P5.6`.
- `query --table t_date --filter 'id=3'` succeeds: the offending rows were
  filtered away before anything decoded them.
- `query --table t_date --filter 'v_date>0002-01-01'` errors — `P5.6`'s case.
- `--schema-mode strings`, which that error names as the escape, **refuses
  ordering outright** ("the column's declared type did not resolve to an Arrow
  type, so it has no order of its own"). The remedy the message offers does not
  address the failure that produced it.

The consequence beyond the reversal itself: `ordering_register` records
`Date32` as `Agrees` with the server's order, which was false wherever the
server answers and this project errored.

An integer sentinel at each end of the range — what the server itself stores —
looked like the fix, and it is free for `Date32`, whose `i32` sits inside
`OrderKey::Int`'s `i64`. It is **not** free for a timestamp, whose key is
already the full `i64`: `i64::MAX` micros since 1970 is 294247-01-10, inside
PostgreSQL's own ceiling of 294276 AD, so a sentinel there would tie against a
real value. `P5.8` carries each special value as its position in the order
instead.

Settled in `roadmap-P5-pushdown.md` (struck at the keystone; git holds it),
"PostgreSQL's special values are ordered, not undecodable"; the work is `P5.8`.

## `P5.7`'s sweep pair was taken against `b70589f`, on a rebuilt census baseline

Two full sweeps of twelve figures, back to back with a 120 s settle, detached
from 19:14 to 20:13 into `runs/p57-sweep-20260830-1915/`, then a derived
`drift.md`. What was taken and how it was read is the next section.

**The figure list was spelled out rather than `--all`**, because `--all`
overwrites `--figure` in `measure.main` and `projection-widths` was still in
`measure.UNTAKEN` at the time. Promoting it — and deleting `composite-isolated`
— is fold-in work, not sweep work: `scripts/measure.py` is a declared path of
`session-drift`, and `measure.git_head` stamps a run "(with uncommitted changes
under a measured path)" if any uncommitted change touches a figure's declared
path, so the register move before the launch would have tainted the stamp of
every table it produced.

**A second sweep is what makes the re-stamp honest, not redundancy.**
`session-drift` is derived across a pair taken on one commit, and the stamp
sentence claims every table under it was taken at that commit; publishing from
one sweep would leave the drift table belonging to the previous stamp.

**`runs/pgdq-nocensus` was rebuilt from `b70589f`.** It dated from 2026-08-28
and predated a ~1600-line diff in the same crate, so the census subtraction was
about to compare two binaries built two days apart. The parse path itself is
unchanged in substance across that window — `map.rs` and `index.rs` differ only
in comments and import lists since `952e492`, `scan.rs` and `copy.rs` not at
all, and `stream.rs`'s diff begins below `map_file` — so a census reading that
moves past `measurements.md`'s resolution floor is a finding rather than the
new baseline. Census-off was verified on `fixtures/17/types/default.sql`:
`v_multidim` reads `List(Int32)` against the census-on `List(List(Int32))`, and
6 unmapped columns against 8.

### What the fold-in changed, beyond the numbers

Both sweeps finished `rc=0` with no figure failing in either;
[`measurements.md`](../../design/measurements.md) is re-stamped at `b70589f`
from sweep 1, with `session-drift` derived across the pair. The detail a later
session needs is in
`roadmap-P5.7-projection-figure-fold-in-notes.md` (struck at the keystone; git holds it);
what changed a claim rather than a number:

**The `INSERT`/`COPY` per-byte ratio is carried as "mid-teens" everywhere, with
no number.** It read 16.7× here against 14.4× under the previous stamp, and
both of its legs are warm absolutes that moved inside their own drift
envelopes. Five witnessed-quiet sweeps now span 14.4–16.7×. Five documents
quoted the three-figure value and all five now quote the magnitude, because a
number that moves 16% per sweep makes all of them wrong at once.

**`census-attribution`'s census-off gap changed sign** — −0.058 s to +0.036 s —
so the section no longer says the gap "reverses" with the census off. Both
readings are inside the instrument's resolution and both support the claim that
actually matters: 97% of the untyped baseline's file-to-file gap is the census.

**`map-only` is 4–14% faster and its apparatus caveat is retired.** The
previous stamp took that table under a witnessed 12%-machine-busy episode; this
sweep is ≤5% for every table and sweep 2 reproduces it within 1.0%. The
downstream consequence is a real one: at 4000 blocks the map is 18.8 s of a
20.3 s throttled `parse`, so the cache remainder is ~1.5 s rather than the
~0.2 s the distorted pair implied.

**One reading moved further than the doc's own warm resolution floor**, and it
is a floor rather than a figure: the `control` file's warm tmpfs `dd` moved
13.6% between two sweeps three minutes apart, in the fast direction, with no
contention available to produce it. The same per-file effect was the previous
pair's notable mover at 5.5%. The ~8% rule is unchanged and now says it bounds
warm *figures*; whether the sweep-disqualification threshold reading off the
same number should move is filed under
[`../STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md)'s "Decisions worth another look".

**Deleting `composite-isolated` took its generator support with it.** The spec
asked only for the register entry, but `measure.UNTAKEN` exists so that a
built-and-unrun instrument is *named*, so a `--weak-composite` flag with no
entry pointing at it is precisely the latent-instrument case that list was
written against. The whole apparatus went: the flag, the `composite_text`
input, the run function, the `IsolatedPair` tests and the
`perf_generator_fidelity.rs` pairing case. All five inputs the generator still
produces are byte-identical at 20 MiB against `HEAD`, which is the evidence the
acknowledgement for that commit carries.
