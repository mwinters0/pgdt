## `M103`: the pre-throttle column is retired, not repaired

The maintainer settled it in session: the quadratic table's "before" column and
the machinery behind it go, and the "after" columns stay as the live numbers.
Each of the facts that decide it, checked here rather than carried over:

- `measure.BEFORE_COMMIT` was `b726f6b`, dated 2026-08-27. `git rev-list --count
  b726f6b..HEAD` is **453**.
- `measurements.md` already conceded the column was "a whole-commit comparison,
  not a throttle-isolating one". That is tolerable across a few commits. Across
  453 it is the whole of what the column measures: it priced 453 commits of
  unrelated work and put the save throttle's name on the result. A caveat
  written once does not scale with the gap it caveats, and this one had grown
  by two orders of magnitude since it was written.
- The `--jobs` collision is the symptom, not the disease. `git grep jobs
  b726f6b -- pgdump_query-cli/src` returns nothing; the flag entered the CLI on
  2026-09-07 (`52313fc`, then `082e2b6`). `4ee40dc` — `M69`, the same day — put
  `SWEEP_JOBS` on every shape `_script` builds, so from that commit the leg
  could only exit 2. Nothing took the figure in the six days between, so it
  broke in silence and stayed broken until an `--all` run reached it.
- The contrast is what makes it a defect rather than bad luck. The register's
  other two-build figure, `census-*`, **is** guarded: `census_binary_problem`
  refuses it unless the census-off binary's stamp is an ancestor of the commit
  being measured with no declared path changed in between. The pre-throttle
  binary had no expiry condition of any kind — not a stamp, not an ancestry
  check, not a path check — and the harness built it for itself, which is
  exactly why nothing ever asked how old it was.

*Rejected: a third `SWEEP_JOBS` exemption keyed on the binary.* It is defensible
only while `SWEEP_JOBS` is 1, where the flag is ceremony and a flagless run of a
binary with no parallel path is the same arrangement. The day the count is
raised, the before column stays serial while the after column moves, and nothing
says so — which is a latent trap of exactly the shape being retired, added to
close one.

What went: `BEFORE_COMMIT`, `Config.bin_before`, `PGDQ_MEASURE_BEFORE_BIN`,
`ensure_before_binary` and its git worktree, the `"before"` arm of
`Session.binary_path`, both `before` legs of `run_per_block_quadratic` and the
`saves_before` strace, and the docstrings in `census_binary_problem` and
`ensure_pgdq_binary` that cited `bin_before` as the affirmative case for a
harness knowing its own build's provenance — that argument still stands and now
stands on `bin_pgdq` alone. The table becomes `blocks | dump | final cache |
parse | saves`, and its note is **computed** from the save column rather than
asserted beside it.

**What the throttle bought is kept as a historical fact, beside the mechanism.**
[`architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "`parse` resumes, and saves
as it goes" now carries 47.7 s at 4003 saves on the `b726f6b` build, measured
2026-09-05 and stated as a reading taken once, together with the gate's 20.8 s →
0.113 s on the same terms. That is the trade the evidence skill's seventh rule
names: re-measurement buys apparatus consistency, and once the gap is 453
commits wide that is no longer what dominates the reading.

**`preamble-prepass`'s borrow survives, as predicted.** Its `Shared` spec is
`RunSpec("pgdq", "blocks4000", "parse-cache-out", "warm", "")` — the *after*
leg, on the shipped binary — so nothing it borrows was in the deleted half. Of
the figure's two declared consumers, `architecture.md` did quote the before
column (the 47 s above) and is rewritten in this change; `STATUS.md` quotes no
cell of it.

## `M104`: a sweep that lost a figure still wrote a full session stamp

`emit` chose between a session stamp and a partial-sitting header on
`whole_sweep`, computed **before the run** as "did the selection cover
`FIGURES`". A run that selected all 22 and took 20 therefore emitted
"**All 23 figures below come from that sitting.**" over 20 tables, three
paragraphs below a blockquote naming the two that failed. Pasting it would have
asserted something untrue of two tables that stay in the document from an older
sitting — and the stamp speaks for those too, which is the whole reason a
partial sitting does not re-stamp.

`whole_sweep` is now `selected_sweep and not failures`, decided **after** the
figure loop. That means the marker each table carries cannot be decided inside
the loop either — a figure taken third cannot know the nineteenth will fail — so
the loop collects `(id, heading, tail)` and the markers are filled in once, when
the predicate is known. A degraded sweep says so in the log, and its lead counts
the tables it actually emitted rather than the figures it selected.

`--render` gets the same correction from the other side: it iterated
`raw["figures"]`, which is the *selection*, so replaying a sitting that lost a
figure would have died on a missing reading key rather than rebuilding the
tables it did take. It now drops the ids in `raw["failures"]` and re-derives
`whole_sweep` against them, so an older `raw.json` carrying the pre-fix value
cannot restore a stamp its run was not entitled to.

## A plain `parse` is faster than serial warm, which reverses the record

`parallel-scan-throughput`'s plain `parse` leg read 0.81× at twenty-four workers
at `20fd77c` and reads **1.36× at two and 1.41× at four** now, falling back to
1.18× once `POOL_DEPTH` stops admitting another. The cause is not new evidence:
the old leg was taken while a partition read went through `read_by_blocks`'
copying arm into the chunk pool, so four slots capped the fused workers flat,
and `19.19`'s `partition_bytes` → `reader_bytes` repair put the cut back on the
source's own units. [`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md),
"What parallelism buys, and where it stops" had predicted exactly this and
flagged the leg as taken at an arrangement the code had left; the re-take is the
confirmation.

What it costs is the *stated* justification for a plain source recommending one
worker — "splitting one is slower than not splitting it" — which the manual and
that section both carried. Both are corrected to the true one: on storage a
plain `parse` is device-bound at 1.00–1.06× the `dd` floor, so the speedup
exists only where the bytes are already resident. Whether the default should
change is `19.8`'s rule and binds beyond `19.11`, so it is under STATUS's
"Decisions worth another look" rather than settled here.
