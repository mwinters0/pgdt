## The parallel-scan work is struck, and the ledger with it

Distil, retarget, sweep, delete — in that order, because deleting first saves
nothing and destroys the source you were about to distil from.

**Distilled.** The wrap's audit had already filed the mechanisms, so the
keystone's harvest was the spec's own: six things that existed nowhere else.
The **split decode-pool/parse-pool** arrangement, refused because the ratio it
would have to be tuned to is a property of the *command* — ~16 decode workers
per discovery thread against ~1.5 per typed-extraction thread, an order of
magnitude apart — which is the reason the fused worker is the shape at all;
**speculative splitting**, refused because a seekable source hands over byte
ranges and not scanner state, so the leader makes speculation unnecessary
rather than cheap; and **cross-block pipelining**, refused because finding a
block's end *is* the work — all three now beside the leader, under "The
interior split". **Copying a field's bytes on the compressed extraction path**
(+7%, and the zero-copy view given up for a whole source class) is beside the
block cache, under "The compressed source". The **`spawn_blocking` dispatch**,
with `std::thread` and `rayon` refused and a blocking task's uncancellability
stated, is under "Execution model and API surface". And the interrupt's
**refusal to bank a partially scanned block** — a partial census being a wrong
answer rather than a weak one — joins the behaviour it explains, under "The
mapping pass is the leader".

**The thesis became a section rather than being deleted**, and it is the one
place the distillate says something the spec did not:
[`../../design/architecture.md`](../../design/architecture.md), "What
parallelism buys, and where it stops". The rule the phase was built on holds —
parallelize what is CPU-bound, which picks decode always, extraction always and
discovery only behind a decoder — but the arithmetic that rule was projected
through does not survive contact with `parallel-scan-throughput`. Two legs
behave as projected and two do not: a compressed `parse` reaches 5.82× at
twenty-four workers, a compressed typed `query` only 1.60×, a plain `parse`
*falls* to 0.81×, and a plain typed `query` is flat at 0.98× although the rule
says it is CPU-bound. So the projections were floors on what would be needed
rather than estimates of what would suffice, and **converting cores into
extraction throughput on a plain source is unfinished work** the
efficient-defaults phase inherits. That section is also what
`parallel-scan-throughput`'s `quoted_by` now names: the figure had exactly one
declared consumer, the spec's projection table, and a figure whose only
consumer is being deleted needs a new one or the edge is a lie.

**Retargeted.** `CLAUDE.md` had no trigger for either parallel arrangement. It
now has one that files them apart — the cold side is the leader's interior
split, the cached side is partitioned replay, they share `stream::cut` and
`worker_count` and differ in what they cut — because the failure it prevents is
reading the wrong section for the arrangement in hand. The performance trigger
gained the parallelism section beside the decomposition, with the fact that
three of the four measured legs are already at a ceiling, so a proposal to
parallelize something meets the evidence before the mechanism.

**Swept**, scoped by the live doc set. Twenty-one code and script citations of
the spec moved to `architecture.md` sections. Four inboxes named the struck
phase and three of them asserted things in the future tense that have since
happened — P14's partitioning advisory, P6's partitioned scan surface, P10's
sparse row index — so each Fact is rewritten to what is now true and each
Origin points at the mechanism rather than at the deleted spec. **Two
contingencies were discharged rather than carried**: P10's index entry was
filed "contingent on the leader arrangement surviving its own slices", which it
did, and P19's plain-path entry was filed contingent on a re-take that has
since happened — its numbers were the superseded `e29939c` sitting's and are now
the published ones. `roadmap.md`'s own phase watermark was a commit behind and
read `P18`; `P19` was allocated on 2026-09-08.

**The ledger is struck to a watermark, and the queue keeps its number.** `M64`
through `M73` and `M75` have landed, so their rows are provenance and go; each
one's mechanism was checked to be filed by subject first, which it was in every

## P19 is specified and sliced, and its grilling reversed two filed positions

The spec is [`../../design/roadmap-P19-efficient-defaults.md`](../../design/roadmap-P19-efficient-defaults.md),
eleven slices, roadmap row `Current`, inbox drained and deleted. Most of it
follows from what 2026-09-08 settled; two things do not, and one of them is a
decision the phase was convened to *adopt*.

**The in-binary arena cap is declined, not adopted.** It came out of `Future`
into this phase on the grounds that a budget discovered from the cgroup and then
blown by arenas seeded at startup is not a sound default. That reasoning stands;
what fails is every available implementation. `mallopt(M_ARENA_MAX, …)` bounds
arena *creation* and frees nothing already made, and glibc seeds an arena per
thread at that thread's first allocation — so a cap keyed to the resolved worker
count removes none of the arenas that already exist by the time it could run,
and the count this phase resolves is the source-dependent one, which is
downstream of source recognition, which is I/O. A cap keyed to the *stated*
`--jobs` is reachable early and is not the number that matters. What is left is
a constant, which re-bases every registered figure's apparatus in exchange for a
value that cannot follow the worker count.

**What replaces it reaches the same place by not creating the threads.** The
CLI moves to a `current_thread` tokio runtime — the library dispatches through
`spawn_blocking` and has never required `rt-multi-thread` — so the threads that
exist are the blocking pool's, created on demand, and the arena count follows
the concurrency dispatched rather than the host's CPU count. `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX`
becomes a published deployment setting for the residual. The price is charged
where it falls: the shipped reserve is sized for the operator who did *not* set
it, so the operator who did gets a smaller budget than their machine could
support, and the spec says so rather than leaving it to be rediscovered.

**`available_parallelism` gets a register entry, against what the inbox filed.**
The inbox concluded the CPU side needed none because the behaviour is `std`'s.
The register's trigger is a decision depending on external behaviour *we do not
control*, and someone else's code is more external, not less: this phase's whole
CPU default rests on `available_parallelism` continuing to read the cgroup
quota, and without an entry a future toolchain moves that default silently.

**Two inbox positions were sharpened rather than reversed.** The span-allowance
entry proposed dividing one allowance among sub-streams; the arithmetic says
that buys no sub-stream at the shipped defaults — `(budget − max_source_span) /
footprint` is zero at 64 MiB against 64 MiB — and that a **plain** source plans
one sub-stream there too, which the entry framed as the compressed case's
problem. The real defect is that the span term is a chunk-shaped source's cost
being charged to a block-shaped one whose `partition_bytes` already covers it,
so the retained unit becomes the source's own to state. And the discovered
budget is `limit − reserve` rather than a fraction of the limit, because the
overhead above a stated budget is measured constant, not proportional — 128 MiB
stated reads the same ~328 MiB resident as 256 MiB stated.

**`KD16` moves to `(b)`, owned by P19 and paired with slice 19.4.** Closing it
re-bases `parallel-scan-throughput`'s one-job row, which runs at the library's
64 MiB default precisely because of the collapse `KD16` describes; that lands
inside the phase's closing sweep.

## `19.2`: the plain `parse` regression is a double read

**A plain `parse` at `--jobs ≥ 2` reads every byte of the file twice.** Summing
`pread64` returns over the 3.00 GiB warm control: 3.02 GiB read at `--jobs 1`,
**6.02 GiB** at `--jobs 2`, and the ratio is flat in the worker count (6143
calls at two workers, 6147 at four, at the shipped 1 MiB chunk). A plain
source's partition is exactly one read chunk (`LocalFileSource::partitions`
answers `Partitioning::anywhere(pool.slot_bytes())`), and `scan_partition`'s
mandatory tail read is another whole `options.chunk_size` — so each piece's tail
re-reads the next piece's body in full. On a block-shaped `.xz` source the same
tail is 1 MiB against a 24 MiB partition and costs 4%, which is why the
compressed legs scale and the plain one does not.

It is charged in **system** time, which is where a warm `parse` already lives —
0.28 s of a 0.38 s serial run. That is also why the profile the spec asked for
could not see it: `perf_event_paranoid = 2` gives `exclude_kernel = 1`, so a
`perf record` here samples user space only, and total *user* cycles rise only
8% between the two legs. **A profile of a syscall-bound shape on this machine is
blind to its own answer** — worth knowing before the next one is commissioned.

The account, the two candidate repairs and what the closing session owes are in
[`../../design/roadmap-P19.2-plain-path-account-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P19.2-plain-path-account-notes.md).

## The plain-path fork routes to both branches

**The sitting was read and the spec's fork turned out not to be exclusive.** It
asks whether the account lands on the pool depth *or* on coordination; the
account names three costs, and forcing them into one arm is what made the
readings look self-contradictory. The spec's own words are *"only one of the two
branches is this phase's"* — which is a claim about which **repair** the phase
owns, not about the account having a single cause.

**What the sitting measured.** Two `--alone` legs differing in one expression,
`POOL_DEPTH` against `POOL_DEPTH.max(jobs)` on the plain source. The depth term
is real: the lifted leg is 4–10% faster above four workers, on per-rep ranges
disjoint at 12, 16 and 24, and unchanged below five where the clamp cannot bind
— a leg-level offset is excluded because the typed-`query` column from the same
sittings shows none and an offset would be flat where this grows. But it does
not remove the 4→8 step it was charged with (+0.076 s lifted against +0.087 s
stock, inside the spreads), and it **refutes `POOL_DEPTH` outright** for the
typed-`query` half: no cell moves by more than 0.8%, both legs plan identical

So the sizing and depth terms are the first branch and their repair folds into

second and leave the phase. **Attributing the step gates nothing** — it is

**The plain worker default is settled ahead of the routing, which the entry had

**`KD17` is allocated rather than deferred.** The closing session held it back

## The plain partition's cap, reviewed

`19.5` sized a plain partition at `PLAIN_PARTITION_CHUNKS` read chunks capped
at `POOL_MAX_BYTES` and filed the size under "Decisions worth another look",
weighing three alternatives that all asked where to put the ceiling. The review
read the mechanism cold and the framing did not survive; the code did.

**The cap is a symptom of a single-unit pool.** `BufferPool` keeps exactly one
announced read length, and the parallel plain path has two read units —
`leader::scan_partition` reads `[start, end)` in a single `read_range` and only
then falls to chunk-sized tail reads. The partition read therefore cannot be
the announced length and survives release only under the ceiling. Every
alternative ceiling answers the wrong question. The arrangement that answers it
is the one `XzSource` already runs — two pools dividing one stated budget
through `held_bytes()` — so it is a Future item ("A two-unit plain source")
rather than a phase-19 slice: it changes what a source is, and P19 is about
defaults.

**Two things the entry did not consider.** `BufferPool::pick` takes the
smallest free buffer with `buf.len() >= len` and `POOL_DEPTH` slots are shared
between partition and tail reads, so the cap makes pooling *possible* without
making it happen, and no reading exists for the hit rate. And the pool's
accounting under-reports this path eightfold: `slots()` counts at
`slot_bytes()`, the announced 1 MiB chunk, while `keeps` admits anything up to
the 8 MiB ceiling and `release` never rescales by bytes — so four 8 MiB
partition buffers on the free list are 32 MiB that `held_bytes()` calls 4 MiB.
`held_bytes()` is what `XzSource::apportion` divides a budget with, so a budget
rule would trust it. **`19.7`'s spec row was amended to cover it**, since that
is the slice writing the budget rule and a separate item would have to land
first while changing the same decision.

**The shipped default sits exactly on the cap**, `DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE * 8`
being `POOL_MAX_BYTES` to the byte, with the three constants independently
justified. `a_shipped_plain_partition_is_eight_whole_chunks` makes the
coincidence a checked relationship; deriving one constant from another was
refused because it reads as though one causes the other.

## `19.6`: the reserve is a constant on one path and a multiple on the other

The budget rule `P19` ships is `budget = min(fraction × limit, limit −
reserve)`, and the spec calls the reserve the one number it rests on and the
fraction a ceiling that "bounds nothing anyone has measured". The diagnostic
sitting that was to choose the constant says that is the wrong way round.

**Plain sources have a reserve; block-decoding `.xz` sources have a
multiplier.** At `--jobs 24` a plain `parse` holds 37.4 MiB whatever budget is
stated — `POOL_DEPTH` slots of `POOL_MAX_BYTES` is 32 MiB and the pool does not
grow past it — so the stated number over-states that path by 27 to 475 MiB
across the axis. The same run over a 24 MiB-block `.xz` reads 243 MiB resident
at a 64 MiB budget and 1242 MiB at 512 MiB. `stream::worker_count` divides the
budget by a 25 MiB divisor, so those rows plan 2, 5, 10 and 20 sub-streams, and
the line through them is `160 MiB + 54 MiB` a sub-stream: **a decoding worker
holds about 2.2× what the divisor charges it**, the divisor charging the decoded
block and a chunk buffer where the worker also holds an 8 MiB LZMA2 dictionary,
its compressed window and the batch in flight. The wall clocks corroborate the
counts against `parallel-scan-throughput`'s published `--jobs` axis
independently of the resident readings.

That a compressed parallel scan holds more than the budget names is a documented
property, not a defect. What is new is that the excess is **proportional**,
which is what a constant subtraction cannot absorb: substituting the worst cell
into a 3 GiB limit gives a budget whose own line reads about 5 GiB.

**The three `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX` legs agree.** Uncapped, the worker count plus
one, and 2 — the value the manual publishes — are inside the per-rep spreads at
all eight cells, and the single pair whose ranges are disjoint reverses sign one
row later. `19.3`'s `current_thread` runtime is the explanation: the threads are
the blocking pool's and are created on demand, so a cap at or above the
dispatched concurrency binds nothing. This is not a controlled refutation of the
koji probe that produced the spec's 200 MiB — different file, different scale,
`rt-multi-thread` — so the claim is the bounded one, and the spec and the manual
were left alone.

The spec was not amended: reversing a spec rationale is outside what an
unattended session settles. Both consequences are filed under `STATUS.md`'s
"Decisions worth another look", and the sitting is `runs/measure-20260909T084032`
(`--alone`, NOT PUBLISHABLE — no document may quote it as a measurement). The
instrument is `measure.UNTAKEN`'s `reserve` entry and the reading is owed again
after `19.7`, which changes the accounting a budget rule trusts.
