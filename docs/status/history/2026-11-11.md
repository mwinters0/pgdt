# 2026-11-11

## 33.16's two reviews: nothing truncates, and 33.16.3 prices a nested column

Settled at the maintainer's direction by the terms of P33's re-target (2026-11-10): a dump read under the `--memory`
PostgreSQL restores it in, only the types that grow long streaming, and the hot path restored where P33 overshot.

- **No builder truncates a field's parts** (D153; the spec's "What a held line costs" and "Where it lives" amended).
  A refused field ends its query, so no reader meets its parts. The mode reading a value as NULL already exists, the
  default `unrepresentable=null` (D98), and its columns, every container of a datetime, `interval` or decimal
  `numeric` (`Leaf::of`), are tested whole for their NULL before they are decoded; truncation would need builders of
  our own. How that test holds them is the next section's
  ([`roadmap-P33.16-builders-notes.md`](../../design/roadmap-P33.16-builders-notes.md)).
- **33.16.3, next, prices 33.16 on a nested column.** `--count-pair`'s one input, the control, holds none
  (`COUNT_PAIR_INPUT`), so 33.16's pair priced only `push_field`'s dispatch, and the spec's "How it is shown" rejects a
  reader's speed left unpriced. It comes before 33.16.1, whose factors describe the walk a rework would change.

## A nested value's copy: reserved, and tested without staging; 33.24 and 33.25 admitted

Settled with the maintainer in a review of 33.16.1's entries (the spec's "What a held line costs" amended).

- **A nested value past its chunk term is decoded into reserved builders (33.24).** D154 left a builder's doubling
  uncharged on D141's ground, a batch's growth being its span's. That covers a batch filling within its span, not one
  value past its chunk term: there Arrow's buffers grow through the allocator, the outgrown one beside its successor
  and, under the mimalloc `pgdt` links (D13), resident to its purge — the habit the spec rejected for the held line.
  Doubling every factor would over-charge an allocator growing in place. Reserving to the field's bound, the batch cut
  before it, holds what is charged on every allocator.
- **A tier test walks a container, staging nothing (33.25).** D153's ground for staging, a tier-tested type never
  growing long, holds for a scalar and not its container: `tier_of` reads one by `decode_array`
  (`unrepresentable.rs`, `Counter::tier_of`), a 24-byte slot an element of two text bytes, `14` the field for a `date[]`
  in a batch (`factor/tests.rs`). `tier_of` is shared, so the census and a parse's gatherer stage the same, under no
  factor. A batch tests only in a block counting such a value (`unrepresentable_reads`), so the second read the walk
  costs there is off the hot path. P40 keeps the walk's resumable form.
- **P33's 2026-11-10 re-target stands.** Its facts hold (Datanommer's long field is `msg text`,
  `runs/datanommer-survey/report.md`) and the ledger is next but one. The factors were built ahead of the ledger reading
  them, and both flaws above are their shape's, so the order is 33.16.2, whose `int8[]` leg reads today's builders,
  then 33.24, 33.25, then 33.4.
- **33.16.2's legs read the terms no buffer count sees.** `int8[]` is not the largest factor (`factor/tests.rs`:
  `text[]` `161/16`, `numeric(76,0)[]` `257/16`), but the largest reads the same form term, which that test already
  holds to Arrow's count per type. A resident reading alone sees the stage term, largest over a `text[]` of one-byte
  elements, and a builder's growth, so those are the legs. `LONG_LINE_WHOLE_VALUE_CHARGE`, `2`, is below `int8[]`'s
  `1 + 65/16` and would bind; each leg's factor is read from the `introspect` build instead, the factor being
  crate-private, and every leg is re-taken under the new allowance at 33.16.2's commit.
