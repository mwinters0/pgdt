- **Every koji cache in `runs/` is unreadable by the current build** (`KD30`),

## P20 reopened: granularity follows row density

**Pick up at 20.1, then 20.2**, koji's row-density reading, which runs detached
before any granularity code. The decisions are
[`roadmap-P20-statistics-memory.md`](../../design/roadmap-P20-statistics-memory.md),
"Granularity follows row density", and the re-cut checklist is
[`../STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md), "P20 progress"; no slice has landed.

**What changed the plan.** A uniform `2^20` group costs an entry per column
however few rows it holds, so a table of wide rows carries statistics per row
that a table of narrow ones does not. The maintainer directed that a block's
group size coarsen by powers of two where rows are sparse, decided from the
whole block's row-density distribution within the mapping pass, and folded
into P20 rather than run ahead of it: coarsening is the cap's merge, and
20.1's account prices it. The facts it rests on are the spec's "Facts it rests
on".

**The slices are numbered in schedule order**, renumbered before any landed, so
a slice number in an earlier commit message names a different row. Admitted
here: 20.2 (the reading), 20.4 (the density minimum) and 20.5 (a stated
maximum). Sizing a fixed-width table from its header is a Future item in
[`roadmap.md`](../../design/roadmap.md), "Future — wanted, unscheduled".

## P20: 20.1 reopened, and 20.11 admitted

**Pick up at 20.1, then 20.11** ([`../STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md), "P20
progress"): each row says what it owes, 20.1's what landed. The maintainer
reviewed 20.1's three unattended calls; the reasons follow.

**Neither end of the cache file holds it whole** — the spec's "Workers are
resolved first; statistics take what is left". `cache::save` encoded the whole
file into one `Vec` before `std::fs::write`, on the throttle mid-pass as well as
at the end, beside every retained and gathering statistic; `read_cache_file`
held the file's bytes beside what it decodes, setting a `query`'s peak.
Predicting the buffer needs a ratio of a statistic's heap to its encoding that
nothing bounds (a varint `u64` takes nine bytes, an `Option` a tag), and
charging it once allocated is too late for a decline; nothing recorded refuses
streaming (D78 covers what the file shares). A save renames over the cache
because streaming widens the window in which a kill truncates it, and a
truncated cache reads `Unreadable`, starting the next `parse` cold.

**The shipped process prints the account**, as 20.1's row and the spec's "The
readings" say: 20.8's blind sitting, 20.9's gates and 20.10's koji run are on
the shipped build, where which term dominated is what explains a failed margin
(20.1's readings had interned above retained). A `query` has no account;
whether it earns a term is 20.8's.

**An open group's growth is charged in steps.** 20.1 charged an open group at
the most a close of its column had measured, and updates come only at closes,
so a first group went uncharged and a later one growing past that went unseen
— up to about 20 KiB a tracked column an open observer, growing with width and
workers. Refused: the first group at its bound, leaving later growth unseen;
every open group at its bound, D81's rejected per-group bound. The step,
64 KiB, is a judgement setting only how often a wide observer updates.

**The tolerance is re-registered as an account**, against 20.1's readings
(`runs/statistics-account-20260915/readings.txt`). The 20‰ slack absorbed a
shortfall that scales with nothing naming it — candidate: vector growth in
`ColumnGatherer::close_group` and `append`, charged after the push where map
growth is charged before the insert — and the one-sided update check left an
over-read at the peak unjudged, which is `Gatherer::join` releasing a piece's
charge only after raising its block's. An over-read declines blocks that fit.
